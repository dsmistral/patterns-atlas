3 · Name the move

§2 met the problem in its oldest clothes. The move, named:

Pattern P35 — Build two worlds (force independence). When a statement \\(S\\) resists every attempt to prove or refute it from axioms \\(\\mathcal{A}\\), stop hunting the proof and put the axioms on trial instead: (1) pin the language, the axioms, and \\(S\\) exactly — P01's discipline, because "worlds" only makes sense for syntax already pinned; (2) build two worlds: structures \\(\\mathcal W_1, \\mathcal W_2\\) interpreting the language, each certified to obey \\(\\mathcal{A}\\), with \\(S\\) true in \\(\\mathcal W_1\\) and false in \\(\\mathcal W_2\\) — P07's auxiliary objects, hired to disagree; (3) close with soundness: a proof from \\(\\mathcal{A}\\) holds in every world that obeys \\(\\mathcal{A}\\), so the disagreement forbids proofs of \\(S\\) and of \\(\\neg S\\) alike — the universal negative "no proof will ever come" becomes a theorem about two exhibited objects. The silence of the axioms is not observed; it is witnessed.

Or, in words: when the asker demands a verdict and the axioms have none to give, do not keep cross-examining the axioms — build the two societies that obey them and disagree, and the verdict's impossibility is on display. One definition was wanted; two worlds are supplied.

The move applies when three keys line up, and each key names its own jam-mode:

Key 1 — the axioms are pinned and listable. A world obeys exactly the pinned list; "obeys standard mathematics" certifies nothing. Jam-mode: the axiom list is vague, open-ended, or second-order in disguise — then no structure can be checked against it, and the question returns to P01: pin the list before building anything. The monoid page below pins two axioms, the field five, the disk a finite slice of Hilbert's list — small on purpose, and the smallness is honest.

Key 2 — the worlds are certifiable. Obedience must be checkable — by finite census for finite worlds, by a construction theorem for built ones. Jam-mode: the world is too large or too vague to certify (a "world" of all intuitions), or certification needs the very axioms in doubt — then the certificate is circular, and the move jams at the door. Box P35.A below makes certification a machine obligation; Example 3's disk shows the built case, certified inside Euclidean geometry, the trick Beltrami invented.

Key 3 — the disagreement is witnessed at \\(S\\) itself. The two worlds must differ on \\(S\\), not on a proxy: \\(\\mathcal W_1 \\vDash S\\) and \\(\\mathcal W_2 \\vDash \\neg S\\), checked at the statement, element by element where elements exist. Jam-mode: worlds that disagree about something near \\(S\\) — the eternal temptation, since near-disagreements are easier to build. The check below is mechanical: for \\(S = \\) "commutativity", the machine reads \\(a \\cdot b\\) and \\(b \\cdot a\\) off the rival table and finds them unequal — the disagreement at \\(S\\), not near it.

Box P35.A — the machine, in three obligations. Every instance of the pattern — the toy tables of Example 1, Beltrami's disk, Cohen's forcing — owes the same three duties, and the first two are checkable by machine at census scale:

1 · The axiom list is finite and decidable per instance. Each axiom, read inside a world, becomes a checkable claim: \\(2 \\cdot 2 = 4\\) identities in a three-element table; \\(27\\) associativity triples; sign rules for a field of fractions; a geodesic construction for the disk. The receipt: every world below is put through a census of its axiom instances — \\(16\\) and \\(19{,}683\\) candidate tables scanned, \\(32{,}768\\) ordered-field triples multiplied four ways — and passes or fails without interpretation. A world that cannot be census-put fails here, whatever it feels like.

2 · Both worlds are certified — by the same machine. The two witnesses owe identical duty: \\(\\mathcal W_1\\) and \\(\\mathcal W_2\\) each run the full axiom census, and \\(S\\) is read off each. The receipt: both tables of Example 1 pass \\(3^3 = 27\\) associativity checks and \\(6\\) identity checks; the field and the disk, the same machine discipline. Symmetry is the honesty: a standard applied to one world and waived for the other is advocacy, not certification.

3 · Soundness turns evidence into theorem. The closing step — "so no proof exists" — is not more census; it is an induction over proof length, the soundness ladder: every axiom is true in every world obeying \\(\\mathcal{A}\\), and every rule preserves truth-in-a-world, so by P66's ladder every proof is. Checked on the toys below at each rule for each world, borrowed as a theorem beyond them — and the borrow is printed every time it happens, because on this pattern the certificate is the argument.

Box P35.B — where the move appears. The birth certificate — Beltrami's 1868 Saggio, the rival geometry built inside Euclid's so its consistency is Euclid's problem now; Klein's 1871 projective recast; Poincaré's 1882 disk, Example 3's world. The algebraic miniature — the three-element nonabelian monoid of Example 1, independence at the smallest scale a census can separate. The arithmetic worlds — Skolem's 1934 nonstandard models and Robinson's 1961 calculus of infinitesimals, Example 2's field grown into a whole theory. The independence of the continuum hypothesis — Gödel's constructible universe \\(L\\) (announced December 1938), where CH holds; Cohen's forcing (1963–64), where it fails: the two-worlds move at the highest court, and the Fields Medal (1966) its only one for logic. The industry — model checkers and SAT solvers, which build small disagreeing worlds on demand: "no counterexample exists in any reachable state" certified exactly when a machine has enumerated the world that would refute it. The signature in the wild: any claim of the form "no proof / no counterexample will ever exist" — reach for the two worlds first; the next-best alternatives (searched hard, resisted long) are Saccheri's, and they took two centuries to fail.
