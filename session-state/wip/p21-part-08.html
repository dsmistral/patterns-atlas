5 · When it fails — three exhibits of fine print

The certificate of Box P21.A is the whole license, and the license has clauses: the room is bounded, the room is closed, and — when the catch is a maximizer — the record is continuous. Here is the wreckage when a clause fails, one exhibit per clause, each one run, not asserted.

Exhibit A · The open room — the crowd that leaks out. Take the runner who halves, thirds, quarters the distance to a wall: hit \(n\) lands at \(1/n\), in the room \((0, 1]\) — every point of the strip except the wall itself. The record is endless and bounded: two of the three keys turn, and half of you is already reaching for the machine. Run the machine anyway — on the closed room \([0,1]\), which is what its blueprint demands — and it catches \(0\): every window around \(0\) is hit infinitely often, because \(1/n < \epsilon\) is exactly \(n > 1/\epsilon\). But \(0\) is not in the open room. And no point of the open room is a limit point either: name any \(x > 0\), and the window of radius \(x/2\) around it holds only finitely many hits — for \(x = 0.1\), the window \((0.05, 0.15)\) holds exactly the 13 hits \(h_7\) through \(h_{19}\), and the lab confirms the count. The crowd is real, endless, and completely outside the room: the machine's fingers closed on a point that had been surgically removed. That is why Box P21.A's Half 2 speaks of closed intervals, and why the halves of the room must be rooms of the same kind: an open room leaks, and it leaks precisely where the crowd was gathering. The repair — close the room, admit the boundary — is sometimes the whole point of the problem: the crowd at the missing wall is the phenomenon. P54's far door is this repair practised deliberately.

Exhibit B · The runaway — the room that is not bounded. The same runner on the endless road: hit \(n\) lands at position \(n\). Endless record, no walls. The pigeonhole is still alive — every window is a box — but the trap is not: no window of radius \(1/2\), anywhere on the road, holds more than one hit, because two integers inside a window of radius \(1/2\) would be less than \(1\) apart, and no two integers are. Every box is quiet; the machine has nothing to keep. The repair is to build the room: bound the record, or restrict attention to the hits inside some bounded stretch — the theorem then applies to that sub-record, honestly labelled as one. The price is that the bound must be honest: the runner is somewhere is not a room; the runner never passes the thousand-kilometre mark is. Boundedness is the clause most often smuggled — an existence proof that quietly assumes its sequence stays somewhere is Exhibit B wearing a tie.
