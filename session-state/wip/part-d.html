4 · The move at work — three encounters

Example 1 · The cube, kept whole

Moves used: P25 the census that grounds every count · P16 the rotation group recognized as \(S_4\) in rotation's clothes · P08 the average, promoted to an inner product.

Return to §2's cube with the ledger open. The five breeds of rotation are the columns; the counts the average threw away become the character of the coloring action. Take the space of all two-color paint jobs — 64 configurations, but see it as P24 taught you: a vector space of dimension 64, one basis vector per paint job. Each rotation permutes the basis, acting by a \(64 \times 64\) permutation matrix; its trace is the number of paint jobs it fixes, and a paint job is fixed exactly when it is constant on each cycle of faces. So the character of the coloring action reads off the class census without a single matrix product:

\[\chi_{\mathrm{paint}} = (64,\; 8,\; 16,\; 4,\; 8) \quad \text{over the breeds } (1),\,(6),\,(3),\,(8),\,(6).\]

And the orbit count of §2 is now an inner product with the constant wave \(\mathbf{1} = (1,1,1,1,1)\): \(\langle \chi_{\mathrm{paint}}, \mathbf{1} \rangle = \frac{64 + 6 \cdot 8 + 3 \cdot 16 + 8 \cdot 4 + 6 \cdot 8}{24} = 10\). Nothing new is computed — the arithmetic is literally §2's average — but the costume change is the point: the average was a one-off answer; the inner product is the first column of a machine that answers everything. For \(k\) colors the same row is \((k^6, k^3, k^4, k^2, k^3)\), and \(\langle \chi, \mathbf{1} \rangle\) is the one-line formula \(\frac{k^6 + 3k^4 + 12k^3 + 8k^2}{24}\): 1, 10, 57, 240, 800, 2226 for \(k = 1, \ldots, 6\) — the last three far past any census, each checkable at census size where feasible and machine-verified in the lab for the rest.

Now the questions the average could not hear. Of the ten cubes, how many have exactly one black face? Give the colors weights — a white face contributes weight \(1\), a black face weight \(x\) — and count fixed colorings by weight: the identity's fixed set carries \((1+x)^6\), a quarter-turn's \((1+x)^2(1+x^4)\), a face half-turn's \((1+x)^2(1+x^2)^2\), a vertex turn's \((1+x^3)^2\), an edge half-turn's \((1+x^2)^3\). Average the five polynomials, breed-weighted:

\[\frac{(1{+}x)^6 + 6(1{+}x)^2(1{+}x^4) + 3(1{+}x)^2(1{+}x^2)^2 + 8(1{+}x^3)^2 + 6(1{+}x^2)^3}{24} = 1 + x + 2x^2 + 2x^3 + 2x^4 + x^5 + x^6,\]

and the coefficients are the inventory: one cube with zero black faces, one with exactly one, two with exactly two (adjacent or opposite), two with exactly three, two with four, one with five, one with six — summing to 10, and matching the machine's orbit census sorted by weight, coefficient for coefficient. This is Pólya's inventory: the same inner product, taken against a bookkeeping polynomial instead of the constant wave. One machine, the whole family of answers.

Two deeper receipts, both single inner products. First: the rotations act on ordinary space \(\mathbb{R}^3\) — each is a \(3 \times 3\) matrix with trace \(1 + 2\cos\theta\) — so the character of the rotation action itself is \((3, 1, -1, 0, -1)\) across the five breeds (quarter-turn \(90^\circ\): \(1 + 2\cos 90^\circ = 1\); vertex turn \(120^\circ\): \(0\); every half-turn: \(-1\)). Then \(\langle \chi_{\mathrm{rot}}, \chi_{\mathrm{rot}} \rangle = \frac{9 + 6 \cdot 1 + 3 \cdot 1 + 0 + 6 \cdot 1}{24} = 1\): the rotation space of the cube is irreducible — it contains no smaller rotation-invariant subspace — a fact that classically asks for matrix analysis, settled here by five multiplications and the integrality gate. Second: the action on the six faces has character \((6, 2, 2, 0, 0)\) — fixed-face counts per breed — and the inner products against \(S_4\)'s irreducible rows (via P16: the rotation group is \(S_4\), the four long diagonals its four letters) give multiplicities \(1, 1, 1\) against the trivial, standard, and two-dimensional waves, with \(\langle \chi_{\mathrm{face}}, \chi_{\mathrm{face}} \rangle = 3 = 1^2 + 1^2 + 1^2\): the six face directions split into three pure waves of dimensions \(1 + 3 + 2\). One table — \(S_4\)'s — now serves every cube question, past and future. That reusability is the move's real economy: compute the table once, count forever.
