4 · The move at work — three encounters

Example 1 · Two little worlds — commutativity on trial

Moves used: P01 pinning axioms, statement, and world · P25 the census that finds and certifies both worlds · P07 the rival world as the auxiliary object nobody supplied.

Pin everything first — P01's discipline, and Box P35.A's first obligation. The language has one symbol: a binary operation \\(\\cdot\\). A world is a multiplication table on a finite set. The axiom list \\(\\mathcal{A}\\) has two axioms: associativity — \\((u \\cdot v) \\cdot w = u \\cdot (v \\cdot w)\\) for all \\(u, v, w\\) — and identity — there is an element \\(e\\) with \\(e \\cdot u = u \\cdot e = u\\) for all \\(u\\). A world obeying both is a monoid. The statement on trial, \\(S\\): commutativity, \\(a \\cdot b = b \\cdot a\\) for all \\(a, b\\). The question: does \\(\\mathcal{A} \\vdash S\\)? Does associativity, with an identity to lean on, force the table to be symmetric?

The census answers in two movements, both machine-run. At order 2 — two elements — the \\(2^4 = 16\\) candidate tables collapse to \\(4\\) monoids, and all four are abelian: \\(0\\) nonabelian witnesses exist. Order 2 is too small to separate — the census is silent here, and Exhibit A returns to that silence. At order 3 the separation arrives: \\(3^9 = 19{,}683\\) candidate tables collapse to \\(33\\) monoids — \\(27\\) abelian, \\(6\\) nonabelian. The machine has found the pair of worlds; here they are, side by side, with \\(\\mathbb{Z}/3\\) renamed \\(\\{e, a, b\\}\\) for the abelian witness \\(\\mathcal W_1\\), and the "right-pass" monoid for the nonabelian one \\(\\mathcal W_2\\):

\\(\\mathcal W_1\\) · table\\(e\\)\\(a\\)\\(b\\)\\(\\mathcal W_2\\) · table\\(e\\)\\(a\\)\\(b\\)
\\(e\\)\\(e\\)\\(a\\)\\(b\\)\\(e\\)\\(e\\)\\(a\\)\\(b\\)

\\(b\\)\\(b\\)\\(e\\)\\(a\\)\\(b\\)\\(b\\)\\(a\\)\\(b\\)

Both tables pass the same census — Box P35.A's second obligation, symmetrically applied. \\(\\mathcal W_1\\): identity row and column \\(e\\), all \\(27\\) associativity triples hold (it is \\(\\mathbb{Z}/3\\), addition renamed), and \\(a \\cdot b = e = b \\cdot a\\): abelian, \\(\\mathcal W_1 \\vDash S\\). \\(\\mathcal W_2\\): identity holds (\\(e\\)'s row and column read the element back), all \\(27\\) triples hold — and the disagreement sits at \\(S\\) itself, not near it: \\(a \\cdot b = b\\) while \\(b \\cdot a = a\\). \\(\\mathcal W_2 \\vDash \\neg S\\). Key 3's machine check, done: two entries read off one table, unequal.

Now close the move — and watch the two censuses play their different roles. \\(\\mathcal W_2\\) already proves \\(\\mathcal{A} \\nvdash S\\) forever, by soundness: any proof from \\(\\mathcal{A}\\) would hold in every monoid, and it fails in \\(\\mathcal W_2\\). But the machine can also be pointed at the proofs themselves, \\(P34\\)-style: over the pool of words in \\(\\{a, b\\}\\) of length at most \\(3\\) plus the empty word \\(E\\), the axioms license exactly \\(38\\) instances — \\(30\\) identity steps \\(wE = w\\), \\(Ew = w\\), and \\(8\\) associativity steps \\((uv)w = u(vw)\\) — and every one flattens to a triviality once brackets and \\(E\\) are dropped. One more derivational step, applied to everything: \\(290\\) size-\\(2\\) derivations — \\(38\\) by symmetry, \\(100\\) by transitivity, \\(152\\) by congruence — with \\(31\\) distinct conclusions, and every one of them a triviality too: the derived equations all collapse to word-equalities the bracket-drop already knew, and \\(ab = ba\\) is not among them, nor anything that could carry it — the machine-checked fact that at depth two, the statement on trial never even enters the record. The relation between the two receipts is the whole page in one line: the proof census says \\(S\\) is not provable at size 2; the witness world says it is not provable at any size — P25's pond, and the ocean behind it, certified by one three-element table and the soundness ladder of Box P35.A's third obligation.
