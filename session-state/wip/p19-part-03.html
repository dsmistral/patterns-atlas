2 · The party nobody can seat

Six people at a party, and a fact you can check on a napkin: among any six, either three are mutual acquaintances or three are mutual strangers. Pick any person, call her Asha; the other five each either know Asha or do not, so by sheer counting — P10's pigeonhole, five objects into two boxes — at least three of them stand in the same relation to her. Say three know Asha. If any two of those three know each other, they and Asha are three mutual acquaintances; if no two of them do, the three are mutual strangers. The same two sentences run if three do not know Asha. There is no seating of six that escapes: the pattern is forced, and the pigeonhole did the forcing.

Now run it backwards, the direction this page lives in. Does the pattern become unavoidable any earlier? Five people: is there a seating where no three are mutual acquaintances and no three are mutual strangers? This time the pigeonhole is silent — five objects into two boxes only forces a group of three, and groups of three were never the crime. But a construction answers, and it is small enough to hold:

\(1, 2, 3, 4, 5\)the five neighboring pairs \(12, 23, 34, 45, 51\)the five diagonals \(13, 14, 24, 25, 35\)

Seat the five around a round table — acquaintances next to each other, strangers apart — and check the crime on pairs. Three mutual acquaintances would be a triangle with all three sides red; but the red pairs form a five-cycle, and a cycle of length five has no triangle inside it: starting anywhere and walking red edges you come home only after five steps, never three. Three mutual strangers would be a triangle with all three sides blue; and the blue star is secretly the same shape as the red ring — walk the blue edges in the order \(1 \to 3 \to 5 \to 2 \to 4 \to 1\) and you have traced \(13, 35, 52, 24, 41\), all five blue pairs, a five-cycle again, no triangle inside it either. So five people can escape both patterns, and the napkin argument says six people cannot: the boundary sits exactly at six, and the escape seating of five is the pentagon of Fig P19.1.
