Example 3 · A polynomial built from a coin — Bernstein, 1912

Moves used: P01 (the pinned claim: Weierstrass's theorem), P07 (the auxiliary object: a coin and a tally), P15 (the squeeze, in its probabilistic dialect: Chebyshev's split).

Pin the claim: Weierstrass's approximation theorem — if \(f\) is continuous on \([0,1]\), then for every \(\varepsilon > 0\) there is a polynomial \(p\) with \(|p(x) - f(x)| < \varepsilon\) at every \(x\) at once. An existence claim in its purest costume: a polynomial is promised, no recipe given. In 1912 — thirty-five years before Erdős's seatings — Sergei Bernstein made the room itself the experiment and found that this time the witness walks out of the proof holding a name tag.

The room and the experiment: fix a point \(x\) in \([0,1]\) and flip a coin that lands heads with chance exactly \(x\), \(n\) times. Let \(X\) be the number of heads. The tally \(X/n\) is the coin's own estimate of its own bias, and its arithmetic is honest: \(\mathbb{E}[X/n] = x\) exactly, and the spread is small — variance \(\frac{x(1-x)}{n} \le \frac{1}{4n}\), tightening like a vice as \(n\) grows. Now apply the function: since \(X\) takes values \(0, 1, \ldots, n\) with \(\Pr[X = k] = \binom{n}{k} x^k (1-x)^{n-k}\), the expectation of \(f(X/n)\) is a finite sum, and that sum is a polynomial in \(x\):

\[B_n(f)(x) \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[f\!\left(\tfrac{X}{n}\right)\right] \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{n} f\!\left(\tfrac{k}{n}\right) \binom{n}{k} x^k (1-x)^{n-k}.\]

Why is \(B_n(f)\) close to \(f\)? Because \(X/n\) is close to \(x\), and \(f\) cannot manufacture jumps. Uniform continuity on \([0,1]\) (a compactness fact, not a coin fact) gives a \(\delta\) with \(|u - v| < \delta \Rightarrow |f(u) - f(v)| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\). Chebyshev's inequality prices the far misses: \(\Pr[|X/n - x| \ge \delta] \le \frac{x(1-x)}{n\delta^2} \le \frac{1}{4n\delta^2}\). Split the expectation along that boundary — near \(x\), the gap is at most \(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\); far, the gap is at most \(2M\) (with \(M\) the maximum of \(|f|\)) on at most a \(\frac{1}{4n\delta^2}\)-fraction of the room:

\[|B_n(f)(x) - f(x)| \;\le\; \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{2M}{4n\delta^2} \;<\; \varepsilon \qquad \text{once } n > \tfrac{M}{\varepsilon\delta^2}.\]

The split runs for every \(x\) at once — the bounds \(M\), \(\delta\), and \(\frac{1}{4n\delta^2}\) nowhere consult \(x\) — so one \(n\) uniformizes the whole interval. That is the theorem. And now the difference from Examples 1 and 2, the one worth the ledger: here the witness is explicit. The polynomial is not merely certified to exist; it is the expectation itself, written down in closed form, computable by anyone for any \(f\). The proof and the recipe are the same object — existence and construction collapse into one, because the object the count controls is the object the theorem wants.

Watch it work on the worst customer: \(f(x) = |x - \frac{1}{2}|\), a kink — the exact shape smooth things approximate worst. Evaluate \(B_n(f)\) at the kink \(x = \frac{1}{2}\), where the error peaks:

The gap shrinks like \(\frac{0.39894}{\sqrt{n}}\) — the constant is \(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\), the coin's kink reading its own bell curve. Price the rate honestly: to halve the error you must quadruple the work. Bernstein's polynomial proves the theorem and earns a recipe, but the recipe is a slow one — approximation theory's later triumphs (splines, Chebyshev economization, and the constructive waves of P24's world) exist because the probabilistic witness, though named, was expensive. The move never promised efficiency; it promised existence, and here it happened to hand over the object itself.

Three counts, one ledger. The Ramsey bound priced abundance with an anonymous witness; the cut priced abundance and then — by walking the conditional expectations — cashed it into a deterministic recipe; Bernstein priced a bound and found the witness standing in plain sight, as the expectation itself. Three jams, one repeated shape: name the room, design the experiment, sum the per-event averages linearly, and state the price before spending. Every promise of Box P19.A passed in all three — and the wreckage in \S5 is where the promises go to die.
