The Patterns Atlas · P07 · the seventh move of the spine

Build an auxiliary object

The givens are never enough. The missing piece — a point, a line, a circle, a polynomial, a second copy — is yours to build: construct it, prove with it, then clear it away.

New word: auxiliary. From the Latin auxilium, help. An auxiliary object is one you introduce yourself, on purpose: it appears in your argument but never in your final claim. It is scaffolding — nailed up for the construction, taken down when the theorem stands. Every other page so far worked with what the problem handed you; this page is about the objects nobody handed you.

1 · Before we begin

The spine so far: P01 pinned the claim, P02 built the table, P03 attacked it with hostile cases, P04 paid for corners, P05 dressed every object in its uniform, and P06 placed stuck objects inside compositions and read off the invariants. P06's block 6 ended with a promise, and this page is the promise keeping itself: composing with an object you control becomes composing with an object you construct.

Here is the tension this page resolves. Every move so far assumed the problem's own objects can connect — that the givens, read the right way, touch each other. But some problems hand you objects that are structurally incomplete: the fact you need is true, but it runs through a point, a line, a function that nobody mentioned. The good news: nothing stops you from building the missing object yourself, and once built, the proof often collapses to one line. The bad news, stated honestly: no recipe tells you what to build. That is why this move is craft — the registry's heaviest technique, and the one that most rewards having the earlier moves as habits. What this page can teach is not the gift but the discipline: how to name the job the missing object must do, how to build it so it has a property you can compute with, and how to take it down again so the theorem is left standing on its own.
