The Patterns Atlas · P12 · the twelfth move of the spine

Name the obstruction

A proof that ends at "contradiction, done" has certified that the door is locked — and thrown away the key. Every impossibility leans on a wall: a class no candidate can reach, a count that cannot balance, a parity that refuses to flip. Name the wall, prove it holds for every route at once, and it answers a whole family of questions before you ask them.

New word: obstruction. An obstruction is a named property that blocks a whole family of attempts at once: every candidate that could possibly succeed must satisfy it, and the target violates it. "Cannot be a sum of two squares because it leaves remainder \(3\) on division by \(4\)" is an obstruction — the class \(3 \pmod 4\) is the wall's name. New word: necessity. The wall's first half — "every candidate must satisfy it" — is its necessity, and it is a theorem, never a sample: a property that merely happens to hold for the attempts you happened to try is a coincidence wearing an obstruction's coat. New word: census. The instrument that finds walls: list which classes the reachable objects can occupy — remainders, colours, parities — and the classes nobody can reach stand out by themselves. A census is a count taken over classes, not over candidates; it is what turns one dead target into a sealed family.

1 · Before we begin

The spine so far: P01 pinned the claim, P02 built the table, P03 attacked it with hostile cases, P04 paid for corners, P05 dressed every object in its uniform, P06 composed stuck objects inside identities, P07 built the objects nobody handed us, P08 divided by the twins, P09 found the quantity nothing can change, P10 forced collisions by counting, and P11 exploded the opposite of the claim. P11's closing pointer promised exactly this page: a successful contradiction often runs on a quantity that refuses to change; when the refusing quantity blocks a whole family of claims at once, promote it from a proof device to a named obstruction — that promotion is P12's first move, and P11 is its front door.

Here is the tension this page resolves. The good news: you have already built walls without naming them. P09's colour class sealed the chameleon meadow — not "this sequence fails" but "no sequence exists"; P11's parity census cornered \(\sqrt{2}\) — not "these fractions miss" but "every fraction misses"; the mutilated chessboard died on a colour count, not on a search through coverings. Each of those proofs used a wall and threw it away. Name the wall instead — prove it necessary, check the target against it, state the family it seals — and the same one-page argument answers "is \(2027\) a sum of two squares?" and every question shaped like it, instantly, with no search at all. The bad news, paid up front: the move is one-way — a wall rules out, it never rules in; the necessity must be proven for every route, not sampled from the routes you tried — a wall some legitimate route simply walks around is the move's most seductive wrong answer; and walls are family-specific — the census that seals one family may say nothing at all about its next-door neighbour, which is exactly the asymmetry P11's Exercise 3 left open and this page's Example 1 closes.
