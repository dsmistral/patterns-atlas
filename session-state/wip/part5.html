4 · Why it is true

Four claims carry this page: the diagonal form is honest (a relabelling really does turn the machine into its stretch table), the harvest law holds for every whole number of feeds, the machines that can be relabelled are exactly the ones the block has been pointing at — and the orbit closed form, with the tilt, is a theorem and not a hope. All four proofs are complete; each leans on the block's earlier claims by citation, not by repetition.

Claim 1 — the diagonal form is honest. Let \(M\) own two non-parallel rails \(\vec v_1\) with factor \(\lambda_1\) and \(\vec v_2\) with \(\lambda_2\), and let \(P = [\,\vec v_1 \;\; \vec v_2\,]\) be the dictionary whose columns are the rails. Then \(MP = PD\), where \(D = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1 & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2 \end{bmatrix}\) — and consequently \(P^{-1}MP = D\). Proof. Two tables agree exactly when their columns agree — a table is its two column-answers (B06). Compare first columns: the first column of \(MP\) is \(M\vec v_1\), the machine's answer to the first column of \(P\); the first column of \(PD\) is \(P(\lambda_1, 0)\), the dictionary walked with reading \((\lambda_1, 0)\), which is \(\lambda_1 \vec v_1\). These are equal, because \(\vec v_1\) is a rail: \(M\vec v_1 = \lambda_1 \vec v_1\) is Box B07 verbatim. Second columns likewise: \(M\vec v_2 = \lambda_2 \vec v_2\). So \(MP = PD\). The rails are non-parallel (B07's Claim 3), so \(P\) passes B05's health test, \(\det P \ne 0\), and the reverse dictionary \(P^{-1}\) exists (Box B06.A). Multiply \(MP = PD\) on the left by \(P^{-1}\): \(P^{-1}MP = P^{-1}PD = D\). Complete. The pure stretches need no relabelling at all, which is the same argument read backwards: for any healthy \(P\), \(P^{-1}(\lambda I)P = \lambda P^{-1}P = \lambda I\) — already diagonal, in every scheme at once.

Claim 2 — the harvest law. For every whole \(n\): \(D^n = \begin{bmatrix} \lambda_1^n & 0 \\ 0 & \lambda_2^n \end{bmatrix}\) and \(M^n = P D^n P^{-1}\). Proof. Both halves by induction — the climbing move, with its own page later in this book. The table first. At \(n = 1\) the claim is \(D\) itself. If \(D^k\) is diagonal with corners \(\lambda_1^k, \lambda_2^k\), then \(D^{k+1} = D^k D\) multiplies corner-wise — a diagonal table never mixes readings, so its powers never mix either — giving corners \(\lambda_1^k \lambda_1 = \lambda_1^{k+1}\) and \(\lambda_2^{k+1}\). Then the machine. At \(n = 1\), Claim 1 read right-to-left gives \(M = PDP^{-1}\). If \(M^k = P D^k P^{-1}\), then

\[M^{k+1} \;=\; M^k M \;=\; P D^k P^{-1} \, P D P^{-1} \;=\; P D^k \bigl(P^{-1}P\bigr) D P^{-1} \;=\; P D^{k+1} P^{-1},\]

the interior \(P^{-1}P = I\) annihilating pairwise at every seam — the telescoping that makes a hundred feeds cost no more than one. The anchor \(n = 0\) holds too: \(M^0 = I = P P^{-1} = P D^0 P^{-1}\). Complete. This is the law that printed \(M^n\) for every \(n\) at once in section 3(a), and it is exact — no limits, no approximations, honest for the first feed and the millionth alike.

Claim 3 — who can be relabelled. A real two-by-two machine is diagonalizable exactly when its characteristic polynomial has two distinct real roots, or the machine is a pure stretch \(\lambda I\). Proof, both directions. (When \(\Rightarrow\) relabelled.) Two distinct real roots \(\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2\): step two of the hunt exhibits rails \(\vec v_1, \vec v_2\) (Box B07.A), B07's Claim 3 makes them non-parallel, and Claim 1 diagonalizes. Or \(M = \lambda I\): already diagonal, as above. (Relabelled \(\Rightarrow\) when.) Suppose \(P^{-1}MP = \begin{bmatrix} \alpha & 0 \\ 0 & \beta \end{bmatrix}\) for some healthy \(P\). Read Claim 1 backwards: \(MP = PD\), so \(M\) times the first column of \(P\) equals \(\alpha\) times that column — a rail with factor \(\alpha\) — and the second column is a rail with factor \(\beta\). If \(\alpha \ne \beta\), the characteristic equation has the two distinct real roots \(\alpha, \beta\) (they satisfy it, by B07's Claim 1, and a degree-two equation has no room for a third). If \(\alpha = \beta\), then \(M = P(\alpha I)P^{-1} = \alpha I\), a pure stretch. Nothing else can happen. Complete — and the census from B07 rereads at a glance: of the 625 machines with entries from \(-2\) to \(2\), the 416 two-rail machines and the 5 pure stretches relabel — \(421\) in all, just over two in three — while the 56 shears and the 148 empty-handed machines stand outside, honestly, until the complex completion is earned.

Claim 4 — the orbit closed form, and the tilt. Fix any starting point \((x_0, y_0)\), read it in the eigen-scheme, and call the reading \((a, b)\) — so \((x_0, y_0) = P(a,b) = a\vec v_1 + b\vec v_2\). Then after \(n\) feeds,

\[M^n(x_0, y_0) \;=\; a\,\lambda_1^n\,\vec v_1 \;+\; b\,\lambda_2^n\,\vec v_2.\]

Proof. \(M^n (x_0, y_0) = M^n P (a,b) = P D^n (a,b)\) — the middle step is Claim 2 right-multiplied by \(P\), the last is the dictionary walked with the reading \(D^n(a,b) = (\lambda_1^n a,\; \lambda_2^n b)\) — and walking that reading home is \(a\lambda_1^n \vec v_1 + b \lambda_2^n \vec v_2\). Complete. For the block's machine, \(\lambda_1 = 1\) and \(\lambda_2 = 4\): the blue count \(a\) never changes, which is the invariant \(x - y = 3a\) (P09, arrived at by relabelling), and the red count quadruples. The tilt, made precise. Suppose \(b \ne 0\). Divide by \(4^n\): the orbit, scaled to year-zero size, is \(\frac{a}{4^n}\vec v_1 + b\vec v_2\). Name any fraction \(\frac{1}{m}\); the number \(\frac{|a|}{4^n}\) drops below it once \(4^n > |a|\, m\), and such an \(n\) exists because \(4^n \ge n + 1\) for every whole \(n\) (a one-line induction: \(4^0 = 1\), and multiplying by \(4\) adds at least \(1\)), so any whole \(n\) beyond \(|a|\,m\) does the job. The squeeze habit (P15) at its plainest: a fixed quantity divided by something that grows past every bound shrinks to nothing. So the scaled orbit closes in on the direction \(b\vec v_2\), the red rail: after twelve years every grid arrow's blue share is below one part in two million, and the island's long-run map is the diagonal's map. Complete.

Scope, honestly. Three boundaries. (i) This page's machines are two-by-two with real entries; bigger tables run the same hunt and the same harvest with more rails wanted — a three-by-three machine needs three — and that floor is named, not built. (ii) The sticking doors stand: the shear owns one direction and the turn owns none, and over the reals no relabelling repairs them — 204 of the census's 625 machines; the complex completion that gives the turn its rails stays deferred, exactly where B07 left it. (iii) The relabelling answers the machine's own story — powers, orbits, growth; a question that needs the directions as well as the factors travels with \(P\) and \(D\) together (the stretch table alone forgets which way its rails point), and the harvest law as stated is for whole numbers of feeds — fractional and negative powers are later floors, named here. What the page closes, it closes completely; what it defers, it names.
