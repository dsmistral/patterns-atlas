4 · The move at work

Example 1 · Desargues — the proof the plane cannot give itself

Moves used: P01 (the pinned claim), P07 (the auxiliary construction — an entire extra dimension), P16 (the shadow map, certified as a dictionary).

Build the room. Take three-dimensional space with coordinates \((x,y,z)\), and in it two planes that cross: \(\Pi_1\), the plane \(z = 1\), and \(\Pi_2\), the plane \(x = 1\). Two non-parallel planes meet in exactly one line, and here that line is \(\ell = \{(1, t, 1) : t \in \mathbb{R}\}\) — the points with \(x = 1\) and \(z = 1\), and any \(y\) at all. Hold onto \(\ell\); it is the entire engine of the proof. Now put one triangle in each plane, in perspective from the origin \(O^* = (0,0,0)\):

Triangle 1, in \(\Pi_1\) (\(z=1\))Triangle 2, in \(\Pi_2\) (\(x=1\))Scalar on the ray from \(O^*\)
\(A = (2,1,1)\)\(A' = (1, \frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2})\)\(A' = \frac{1}{2}A\)
\(B = (3,2,1)\)\(B' = (1, \frac{2}{3}, \frac{1}{3})\)\(B' = \frac{1}{3}B\)
\(C = (-2,1,1)\)\(C' = (1, -\frac{1}{2}, -\frac{1}{2})\)\(C' = -\frac{1}{2}C\)

Each primed vertex sits on its ray from \(O^*\) — the first two between \(O^*\) and their partners, the third on the opposite ray — so the lines \(AA'\), \(BB'\), \(CC'\) all pass through \(O^*\). Two triangles, in two different planes, in perspective from a point: Desargues's setup, one dimension up.

The proof is three sentences. The plane through \(O^*\), \(A\), and \(B\) contains \(A'\) and \(B'\) too (they sit on the rays \(O^*A\) and \(O^*B\)), so it contains both lines \(AB\) and \(A'B'\); call it \(\Sigma_{AB}\). The line \(AB\) also lies in \(\Pi_1\), and the line \(A'B'\) also lies in \(\Pi_2\), so their meet \(P = AB \cap A'B'\) lies in \(\Pi_1\), in \(\Pi_2\), and hence on \(\ell\). The same two sentences with \(B, C\) in place of \(A, B\) put \(Q = BC \cap B'C'\) on \(\ell\), and with \(C, A\) they put \(R = CA \cap C'A'\) on \(\ell\). Three meets, one line — Desargues, proved. And the meets can be watched: computing them, \(P = (1, 0, 1)\), \(Q = (1, \frac{8}{5}, 1)\), \(R = (1, 1, 1)\), and each one indeed has \(x = 1\), \(z = 1\) — this page's lab verifies every meet by substitution on both of its lines, both ways.
