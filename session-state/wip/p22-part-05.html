Box P22.A · The three certificates

The receipt splits into three independent statements, and each one is exactly where it is needed.

Certificate 1 — the catch exists (P21's machine, run by the orbit itself). Consecutive gaps obey the pull: \(|x_{n+1} - x_n| = |g(x_n) - g(x_{n-1})| \le c\,|x_n - x_{n-1}|\), so gap \(n\) is at most \(c^n\) times gap \(0\). Stack the pull along the tail: every hit after hit \(n\) stays within \(c^n d_0/(1 - c)\) of hit \(n\), where \(d_0 = |x_1 - x_0|\) — the tail's total future wandering, a geometric series, priced in advance. So the orbit builds its own nested rooms: the interval of radius \(c^n d_0/(1 - c)\) around hit \(n\) contains every later hit, the next room sits inside the last, and the widths shrink to zero. P21's Half 2 catches the chain: exactly one point \(x^*\) survives, the room is closed so \(x^*\) is in it, and the pull doubles as the continuity clause — \(|g(x^*) - x_{n+1}| \le c\,|x^* - x_n| \to 0\), so \(g(x^*) = x^*\). A contraction needs no separate continuity assumption: the pull is so strong it is continuous for free.

Certificate 2 — the catch is unique (the pull's arithmetic). Suppose two fixed points, \(p\) and \(q\). Then \(|p - q| = |g(p) - g(q)| \le c\,|p - q|\), so \((1 - c)\,|p - q| \le 0\), and since \(c < 1\) strictly, \(p = q\). The whole argument is one line; the whole weight is the strictness — at \(c = 1\) it degenerates to \(0 \le 0\) and certifies nothing (§5, Exhibit B).

Certificate 3 — the catch is priced (the geometric price). \(e_n = |x_n - x^*| = |g(x_{n-1}) - g(x^*)| \le c\,e_{n-1} \le \cdots \le c^n e_0\): after \(n\) hits, the error is at most \(c^n\) times the start's, and the guaranteed digit rate is \(\log_{10}(1/c)\) per hit. The price is a floor on the worst start in the room, not a forecast of the run — §4 measures three runs against their receipts, and all three beat them; the bonus comes from the flattening of the map at its own fixed point, and the certificate stays honest precisely because it never promises the bonus.

Neither certificate stands alone: existence without uniqueness is P21's honest plurality, uniqueness without existence is a vacant promise, and both without the price is a machine sold without a manual. The receipt is the triple, and the triple is why the move feels like closing a hand: the repetition runs, and something is forced to be in the fist.

Box P22.B · Where it shows up, and the three-key signature

Appearances. Square roots by averaging — Heron's recipe, §4's first run, old enough to be Babylonian. Newton's method near a simple root — the tangent line's map, a contraction once the room is small enough, §4's second run. The cosine's fixed point — Dottie, §4's third run, the slow honest one. Picard's successive approximations — orbits of an integral map that prove differential equations have solutions, §7. Banach's fixed point theorem — the whole receipt promoted to abstract rooms, §7. And at the research frontier, P57's Nash–Moser fast Newton, built directly on this page. One shape under all of them: a rule repeated, a room respected, a pull proved.

When to reach for it — three keys. First, the map genuinely returns into the room: self-mapping is a clause to verify, and §5's repeller is a map that passes the eye and fails the clause. Second, the pull is strict: one \(c < 1\) for the whole room — and if the best \(c\) you can prove is \(1\), you have proved nothing: Exhibit B's map pulls by exactly \(1\) and settles nowhere. Third, the room is closed: the catch must be inside the walls when the trap closes — Exhibit C's leak crowds at a point the room does not contain. If all three keys turn, the receipt reads: exists, unique, priced. No other move on the ladder sells all three at once.
