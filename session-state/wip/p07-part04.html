4 · The move at work

Three worked examples, one per domain. Each names every pattern used — and each ends with the scaffold coming down.

Example 1 (trigonometry) — the build your syllabus already named

From your class-12 shelf, here is the move under its school name: the auxiliary angle method. The problem: rewrite \(3\sin\theta + 4\cos\theta\) as a single shifted wave \(R\sin(\theta + \phi)\), and find its maximum value. First, the trap. A student in a hurry says: "\(\sin\) tops out at 1, so \(3\sin\theta\) reaches 3; \(\cos\) tops out at 1, so \(4\cos\theta\) reaches 4; the maximum is \(3 + 4 = 7\)." Pinned properly (P01): the maximum is a number the expression attains at some single \(\theta\) — and \(\sin\theta\) and \(\cos\theta\) do not peak at the same \(\theta\). The claim "7" needs one \(\theta\) making both terms maximal at once; no such \(\theta\) exists. (A grid check sees this in seconds — and a proof must too. Hold the thought.)

Now name the job: I need one clean sine wave out of two. The object that does the job: a right triangle with legs 3 and 4. Build it — the point \(P = (3, 4)\), with hypotenuse \(R = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5\) and base angle \(\phi\), so

\[\sin\phi = \tfrac{4}{5}, \qquad \cos\phi = \tfrac{3}{5}, \qquad \phi \approx 53.1301024^\circ.\]

The rewrite is now forced, and every coefficient is a leg of your build:

\[3\sin\theta + 4\cos\theta = 5\left(\tfrac{3}{5}\sin\theta + \tfrac{4}{5}\cos\theta\right) = 5\left(\cos\phi\,\sin\theta + \sin\phi\,\cos\theta\right) = 5\sin(\theta + \phi).\]

The maximum reads off the spot: a sine tops out at 1, so the maximum is \(R = 5\) — attained at \(\theta + \phi = 90^\circ\), i.e. \(\theta = 90^\circ - 53.1301024^\circ \approx 36.8698976^\circ\). The trap's gap is honest and large: \(7 - 5 = 2\). The lab confirms both facts by grid: over 721 angles (\(0^\circ\) to \(360^\circ\), half-degree steps) the identity \(3\sin\theta + 4\cos\theta = 5\sin(\theta + \phi)\) holds with worst error \(1.776 \times 10^{-15}\), and the grid maximum is \(5\) to within \(1.3 \times 10^{-5}\) (the grid's half-degree steps bracket the exact peak), with the exact peak point checked separately at \(36.8698976^\circ\).

The scaffold comes down: the answer is a statement about the original wave — "maximum 5, attained at \(\theta \approx 36.87^\circ\)" — and though the triangle is how you found it, the wave's fact now stands alone. The same build with legs \((a, b)\) is the general method: \(a\sin\theta + b\cos\theta = R\sin(\theta + \phi)\) with \(R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\) — Exercise 2 hands you \((5, 12)\). Moves used: P07 (the auxiliary triangle), P01 (pinning "maximum" as a value attained at a single \(\theta\) — the trap dies at the pinning), P02 (the grid that detects the trap and the identity alike).
