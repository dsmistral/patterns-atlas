4 · Three encounters

Encounter 1 — the two-island census, run to exhaustion. The almanac of §2 was one presheaf; the receipt a page owes is the whole census, so the lab enumerates every presheaf on the two-island world with values of size at most \\(2\\): \\(458\\) raw assignments, \\(190\\) respecting composition, and every one checked against every cover — the world owns \\(2\\) covers of the empty stretch, \\(2\\) of each island, \\(10\\) of the country. Five sheaves, all locally constant: shapes \\((1,1,1,1)\\), \\((1,1,2,2)\\) twice, \\((1,2,1,2)\\) twice — in each, \\(s_X = s_E \\cdot s_W\\) exactly. The failures name themselves: \\(156\\) die on the empty stretch (two records where the empty cover demands one); \\(9\\) fail locality (two world-records agreeing on both islands); \\(20\\) fail gluing with locality intact — the almanac's failure, consistent reports no record witnesses. The repair census: plus once separates all \\(190\\); plus twice sheafifies all \\(190\\); \\(5\\) needed nothing, \\(97\\) one round, \\(88\\) two. And the constant presheaf — the almanac itself, \\((1,2,2,2)\\) — repairs to \\((1,2,2,4)\\): two constant readings grow to four locally constant records, \\(\\eta\\) embedding the constants as \\((0,0)\\) and \\((1,1)\\). Universality, the last gate: every restriction-respecting map from any of the \\(190\\) into any of the \\(5\\) sheaves — \\(2{,}974\\) of them — extends along \\(\\eta\\), uniquely, with not one exception. The repair is not a choice; it is the only answer the census permits.

Encounter 2 — the diamond world: same data, two sites. Regions need not sit in a space: take four abstract regions \\(a, b, c, m\\) with meets — \\(a \\wedge b = b\\), \\(a \\wedge c = c\\), \\(b \\wedge c = m\\), and \\(m\\) beneath all — the diamond. The census of set-valued presheaves with values of size at most \\(2\\) is again \\(190\\) — and that is law, not coincidence: the diamond is the two-island world's own poset in different clothes, \\(m\\) the empty stretch, \\(b\\) and \\(c\\) the islands, \\(a\\) the country, so the raw census was always going to repeat; what changes is the site. First topology, the trivial site \\(J_0\\): the only cover of a region is itself, so locality and gluing are vacuous — all \\(190\\) are sheaves. Second topology, \\(J_1\\): declare one non-trivial cover, \\(b\\) and \\(c\\) jointly covering \\(a\\). The sheaf census collapses: \\(47\\) sheaves, \\(84\\) separated-but-not-gluing, \\(59\\) failing locality outright — one declared cover destroyed \\(143\\) verdicts. The repair census is different too: on the diamond, one round of plus always suffices — \\(47\\) sheaves outright, \\(143\\) repaired by a single round, none needing two — because the only non-trivial cover is at the top; the two-island world needed two rounds because its covers live below the top too, and a first-round repair at \\(X\\) can create fresh gluing work at the islands. Two receipts, small enough to hold: the presheaf with one record at \\(a\\), two at \\(b\\), two at \\(c\\), one at \\(m\\) — every pair of island-readings consistent, since \\(m\\) carries a single record — glues nothing at \\(a\\); its repair carries \\(4\\) records at \\(a\\), the three patch-reports the trivial bookkeeping had no home for. And the mirror: two records at \\(a\\), one at \\(b\\), one at \\(c\\), both restricting to the same readings — the repair merges them into one. The site decided everything; the data never changed.

Encounter 3 — the étalé route: repair by moving to the points. The plus construction repairs by decree at every region; the étalé route repairs at every point. The stalk at a point \\(p\\) is the germs of all readings at all neighborhoods of \\(p\\), two readings equal when they agree on some smaller neighborhood — the almanac's East-stalk is the set of possible East-readings, because East is its own smallest neighborhood. String the stalks over their points and a section is a continuous choice of germ, one per point; the sheaf of sections over each region \\(U\\) is exactly the honest records on \\(U\\). On the two-island world the receipt is clean: stalks are \\(F(E)\\) and \\(F(W)\\), and a continuous section over the country is any pair of germs — \\(s_E \\cdot s_W\\) of them, machine-checked for all \\(190\\) presheaves, which is \\(F^{++}(X)\\) exactly. The subtle world is the Sierpiński one: two points \\(o\\) (open) and \\(c\\) (closed), opens \\(\\varnothing, \\{o\\}, X\\). The closed point's only neighborhood is \\(X\\) itself, so its stalk is \\(F(X)\\), and continuity forces any section over \\(X\\) to read \\(\\sigma(c)\\)'s germ at \\(o\\): sections over \\(X\\) biject with \\(F(X)\\). The census: \\(36\\) presheaves with values of size at most \\(2\\); the \\(28\\) carrying two records on the empty stretch fail the empty cover instantly; the \\(8\\) honest ones are all sheaves — every cover of \\(X\\) contains \\(X\\) itself, the closed point's doing, so the gluing law has nothing to bite. A whole page of §2-style failures, erased by one closed point: the site, again, deciding everything.

Three worlds — a country, a diamond, a two-point space — and one job description behind all of them: name the site, plus twice, factor everything. The pattern box said it as a construction; the encounters say it as a census: the site decides which data is honest, the repair is forced by the numbers, and the universal property is checked, not asserted. The remaining question is what the repair costs — which is §5's subject, and the honest half of this move.

SKETCH — the research theorems behind this page: on general sites, the plus construction can need a transfinite iteration to converge, and for large sites the sheaf category may only exist under set-theoretic smallness conditions (a universe); the adjoint-functor-theorem route to sheafification, and Giraud's stack gluing one level up, are quoted as consistent with the literature, not source-verified. The parts this page deliberately does not build: the transfinite construction, the universe hypotheses, the topos-theoretic closure operator of Lawvere–Tierney. What this page verified directly: the two-island census to exhaustion (\\(458 \\to 190 \\to 5\\) sheaves, with the full failure census), the two-round convergence theorem on that world, the universal property over all \\(2{,}974\\) maps of the finite model, the diamond world's two topologies (\\(190\\) sheaves under \\(J_0\\), \\(47\\) under \\(J_1\\), one round always sufficing), and the Sierpiński census (\\(36\\) presheaves, \\(8\\) sheaves, sections over \\(X\\) in bijection with \\(F(X)\\)).
