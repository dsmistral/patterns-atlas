The Patterns Atlas · P62 · the tenth move of tier 5

Sheafify on a site

Local answers that agree on every overlap and still refuse to assemble are not lying — the world hosting them is too small. The move is to build the world the data was asking for: a repair that adds no facts and invents nothing, runs the same two steps every time, and ends in a proof that every other repair passes through it. The data glues at last, because gluing is what the new world's records are.

New word: the site. A site is a world of regions together with a notion of cover: a rule saying which families of regions jointly count as covering one region — a country is covered by its provinces, a wall by three photos that overlap on the seams. A presheaf is an assignment of data to every region, with restriction maps along inclusions that compose honestly: data that restricts, with nothing forced to agree. A sheaf — P33 defined it on this book's on-ramp — is a presheaf that passes the gluing clause: sections on covering pieces that agree pairwise come from exactly one section on the covered region. The site is the honest abstraction of "space": the regions need not be subsets of anything, and the covers need not come from open sets — any world with regions and covers counts, and this page's fourth section runs the whole machine on a world of four abstract regions that has never been near a map.

1 · Before we begin

P33 taught what to do when local answers fail to glue and the data is immovable: measure the refusal. The refusals form a space — the cohomology — and its dimension counts the ways the world can say no. P60 taught the sibling move in one place: when a world's arithmetic cannot hear your questions, complete the world, and the completion is forced on you by a universal property — P31's discipline, define by the job, delivering the verdict. Both moves were honest about their limits: P33's obstruction measures but repairs nothing; P60's completion completed one monoid, in one place.

Here is the tension this page resolves. P33's surveyors stood on the mountain and refused to edit their readings — the data was the data, and its refusal was a fact about the shape. But the data is not always immovable, and it is not always honest to treat it as if it were. Sometimes the "data" is your own bookkeeping — a rule you wrote down region by region, restriction maps included, and never checked the gluing clause at all. Then the refusal is not a fact about the world; it is a defect of the hosting structure, and the honest question changes shape. Not how much does the data refuse? — P33's question, answered by cohomology — but what is the least world in which the data stops refusing? The answer must add no facts, invent no choices, and repair every presheaf at once, or it is not a move but a habit. This page builds that repair, proves it is forced, and — because no repair is free — measures exactly what it erases.

2 · Meet the problem

The almanac that cannot say "cold here, warm there". A country of two islands, East and West, with no shared shore — the two-island world, in the notation this page keeps: opens \\(E\\), \\(W\\), the empty stretch \\(\\varnothing\\), and the whole country \\(X = E \\sqcup W\\). The almanac office keeps temperature records, and its bookkeeping is the simplest honest rule imaginable: every region carries the same two official readings, \\(0\\) (cold) and \\(1\\) (warm), and restriction does nothing — a reading of the country is a reading of any island. Formally: \\(P(U) = \\{0, 1\\}\\) for every \\(U\\), every restriction the identity. This is a presheaf. It is even separated: two different world-records already differ on each island, so no local information is doubled.

The islands report. East: \\(0\\). West: \\(1\\). The report is consistent — matched on overlaps to the letter, and there are no overlaps, so the matching condition is satisfied vacuously. Then the question that breaks the bookkeeping: which world-record do the islands' readings come from? A world-record is an element of \\(P(X) = \\{0, 1\\}\\), and restriction is the identity — the record \\(0\\) restricts to \\(0\\) on East but to \\(0\\) on West too, and the islands' \\(1\\) is lost; the record \\(1\\) fails East the same way. No world-record witnesses the report. The data agrees everywhere it must, and the assembly still refuses.

Notice what has not happened. This is not honest disagreement: if the islands shared a shore and their readings clashed on it, the matching condition itself would fail — inconsistent data, and no repair is deserved; P33's cohomology is the honest bookkeeping for that case, counting the clash. Here the data is consistent and the world is too small: it owns records for "cold everywhere" and "warm everywhere", and no record for the perfectly real situation cold here, warm there. The distinction is the whole diagnosis, and it has a name for each failure mode: a presheaf fails gluing when a consistent local report has no global witness — the almanac's case — and fails locality when two global records agree on every piece without being equal, which needs overlap-blind bookkeeping. The repair, done by hand before any machinery: the honest world-records are the locally constant ones — a reading for East and a reading for West, \\(2 \\times 2 = 4\\) records where the office kept \\(2\\). The two constants survive among the four, as the constant records; what is new is exactly what the data demanded.

The hand repair generalizes, and the generalization is this page. But a move needs receipts, so the page runs the two-island world to exhaustion — every presheaf on it with values of size at most \\(2\\), checked against every cover. The census, machine-verified and re-verified for this page's lab: \\(458\\) raw assignments of data and restrictions; \\(190\\) of them respect composition and earn the name presheaf; and of those \\(190\\), exactly \\(5\\) are sheaves — one with singleton records everywhere, two shaped \\((1, 1, 2, 2)\\) — a one-record East and a two-record West forcing a two-record country, \\(s_X = s_E \cdot s_W = 2\\) —, and two shaped \\((1, 2, 1, 2)\\). (The shapes are recorded exactly: \\((s_\\varnothing, s_E, s_W, s_X)\\) with \\(s_X = s_E \\cdot s_W\\) — the sheaves are exactly the locally constant bookkeepings.) The failure census is just as sharp: \\(156\\) of the \\(190\\) fail instantly, on the empty stretch — the empty cover of \\(\\varnothing\\) has exactly one consistent report, so an honest world carries exactly one record on \\(\\varnothing\\), and \\(156\\) presheaves carry two; \\(25\\) pass locality — \\(5\\) of them are the sheaves, \\(20\\) fail gluing exactly as the almanac does; \\(9\\) fail locality. Every failure has a name, and the repair will have to answer all of them at once.
