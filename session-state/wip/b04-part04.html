3 · Worked by hand

Three workings, simplest first, each carried to the end. Under all of them runs Box B04.

(a) 25 and 11 — the climb, in the small

The dance (B03): \(25 = 2 \times 11 + 3\), then \(11 = 3 \times 3 + 2\), then \(3 = 1 \times 2 + 1\), then \(2 = 2 \times 1 + 0\). Last nonzero leftover: 1. To climb, read the chain from the bottom and substitute each leftover away. The bottom division says \(1 = 3 - 1 \times 2\): true, but the 2 is not yet a combination. The division above it says \(2 = 11 - 3 \times 3\), so

\[1 \;=\; 3 - (11 - 3 \times 3) \;=\; 4 \times 3 - 11.\]

Still a leftover on stage: the 3. The top division says \(3 = 25 - 2 \times 11\), so

\[1 \;=\; 4 \times (25 - 2 \times 11) - 11 \;=\; 4 \times 25 - 9 \times 11.\]

Task one's hunt, produced by three substitutions instead of luck. Check it: \(100 - 99 = 1\).

(b) 2026 and 1491 — the certificate of 1321

The same climb, seven rungs instead of two. The dance, from B03's section 3(c):

\[2026 = 1 \times 1491 + 535, \;\; 1491 = 2 \times 535 + 421, \;\; 535 = 1 \times 421 + 114, \;\; 421 = 3 \times 114 + 79,\]

\[114 = 1 \times 79 + 35, \;\; 79 = 2 \times 35 + 9, \;\; 35 = 3 \times 9 + 8, \;\; 9 = 1 \times 8 + 1, \;\; 8 = 8 \times 1 + 0.\]

Now climb from the bottom, substituting one division per line — and check every line by pencil: on each of them, the two products differ by exactly 1.

\[1 = 9 - 1 \times 8\]

\[1 = 9 - (35 - 3 \times 9) = 4 \times 9 - 35\]

\[1 = 4 \times (79 - 2 \times 35) - 35 = 4 \times 79 - 9 \times 35\]

\[1 = 4 \times 79 - 9 \times (114 - 79) = 13 \times 79 - 9 \times 114\]

\[1 = 13 \times (421 - 3 \times 114) - 9 \times 114 = 13 \times 421 - 48 \times 114\]

\[1 = 13 \times 421 - 48 \times (535 - 421) = 61 \times 421 - 48 \times 535\]

\[1 = 61 \times (1491 - 2 \times 535) - 48 \times 535 = 61 \times 1491 - 170 \times 535\]

\[1 = 61 \times 1491 - 170 \times (2026 - 1491) = 231 \times 1491 - 170 \times 2026\]

Eight lines, and the last one is the identity: \(231 \times 1491 - 170 \times 2026 = 344{,}421 - 344{,}420 = 1\). Fig B04.2 compresses the whole climb into one view.

Two readings of the last line. As an equation over the whole numbers: \(231 \times 1491 - 170 \times 2026 = 1\). Reduced mod 1491: the term \(231 \times 1491\) vanishes, leaving \(-170 \times 2026 \equiv 1 \pmod{1491}\) — the class of \(-170\) is the inverse of the class of 2026. Dress it in census range \(0\) to \(1490\): \(-170 + 1491 = 1321\). And B03's brute-force witness falls out of the same line: \(2026 \times 1321 = 2026 \times (1491 - 170) = 2026 \times 1491 - 170 \times 2026 = (2026 - 231) \times 1491 + 1 = 1795 \times 1491 + 1\). The equation the scan found by testing classes — now with its reason attached.
