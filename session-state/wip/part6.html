Exercise 1 ●○○

The symmetric machine \(A = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\) — the machine of B07's first exercise, rails already hunted: \((1,1)\) with \(\lambda = 3\) and \((1,-1)\) with \(\lambda = 1\). (a) File the eigen-scheme's dictionary \(P\) — smaller stretch factor's rail first — health-check it, and write the reverse dictionary by Box B06.A. (b) Verify the diagonal claim: compute \(P^{-1}AP\). (c) Reap: \(A^5\) by the harvest law. (d) The return trip: feed \((1,0)\) five times, one feed per rung, and check the last rung against the closed form's first column.

Hint

(a) Columns are the rails in school numbers; the determinant is the health check; the adjugate over the determinant is the reverse. (b) Multiply in the order written. (c) The stretch table to the fifth is corner-wise; assemble \(P D^5 P^{-1}\) and let the factor \(\frac{1}{2}\) ride along. (d) Five feeds, five points, one column of the answer each.

Worked answer

(a) First column the \(\lambda = 1\) rail \((1,-1)\), second the \(\lambda = 3\) rail \((1,1)\): \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), \(\det P = 1 + 1 = 2 \ne 0\), healthy; \(P^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\) by Box B06.A. (b) \(P^{-1}AP = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 3 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 6 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3 \end{bmatrix}\): a pure stretch, \(\times 1\) and \(\times 3\), no mixing. (c) \(A^5 = P \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 3^5 \end{bmatrix} P^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 243 \\ -1 & 243 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix} 244 & 242 \\ 242 & 244 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 122 & 121 \\ 121 & 122 \end{bmatrix}\). (d) The ladder: \(A(1,0) = (2,1)\); \(A(2,1) = (5,4)\); \(A(5,4) = (14,13)\); \(A(14,13) = (41,40)\); \(A(41,40) = (122,121)\). The fifth feed's answer is \(A^5\)'s first column — and the closed form's first column is \(\left(\frac{1 + 3^5}{2}, \frac{-1 + 3^5}{2}\right) = (122, 121)\): three routes, one answer. The trace glance agrees: \(122 + 122 = 244 = 1 + 3^5\).

Exercise 2 ●●○

A second storm machine washes up, \(C = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 6 \end{bmatrix}\). (a) Hunt: characteristic equation, roots, both rails — one line each. (b) File the dictionary (smaller factor first), health-check it, reverse it, and verify \(P^{-1}CP\) is the stretch table. (c) Reap: \(C^5\) by the harvest law. (d) Confirm two ways: the trace glance against \(2^5 + 7^5\), and the school route \(C^2\), \(C^4\), then \(C^5 = C^4 C\).

Hint

(a) Trace 9, determinant 14 — the equation assembles itself, and both roots are whole. (b) Columns are the rails; \(\det P\) comes out 5. (c) \(2^5 = 32\), \(7^5 = 16807\); the two divisions by 5 both land. (d) The trace of a power is the sum of the powers of the factors; the school route is three honest multiplications.

Worked answer

(a) \(\operatorname{tr} C = 9\), \(\det C = 18 - 4 = 14\): \(\lambda^2 - 9\lambda + 14 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 7)\). For \(\lambda = 2\): \(C - 2I = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 \\ 2 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\), so \(a + 2b = 0\), rail \((2,-1)\) — check: \(C(2,-1) = (6-2,\; 4-6) = (4,-4) = 2(2,-1)\). For \(\lambda = 7\): \(C - 7I = \begin{bmatrix} -4 & 2 \\ 2 & -1 \end{bmatrix}\), so \(b = 2a\), rail \((1,2)\) — check: \(C(1,2) = (3+4,\; 2+12) = (7,14) = 7(1,2)\). (b) \(P = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\), \(\det P = 4 + 1 = 5\), healthy; \(P^{-1} = \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\). \(P^{-1}CP = \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 2 \\ 2 & 6 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 4 & -2 \\ 7 & 14 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & 1 \\ -1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 10 & 0 \\ 0 & 35 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 7 \end{bmatrix}\). (c) \(C^5 = P \begin{bmatrix} 32 & 0 \\ 0 & 16807 \end{bmatrix} P^{-1} = \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 64 & 16807 \\ -32 & 33614 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} 2 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = \frac{1}{5}\begin{bmatrix} 16935 & 33550 \\ 33550 & 67260 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 3387 & 6710 \\ 6710 & 13452 \end{bmatrix}\). (d) Trace: \(3387 + 13452 = 16839 = 32 + 16807 = 2^5 + 7^5\) — Vieta's sum reading one floor up, holding. School route: \(C^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 13 & 18 \\ 18 & 40 \end{bmatrix}\); \(C^4 = (C^2)^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 493 & 954 \\ 954 & 1924 \end{bmatrix}\); \(C^5 = C^4 C = \begin{bmatrix} 3387 & 6710 \\ 6710 & 13452 \end{bmatrix}\). Both routes, one table.
