Claim 4 — determinants multiply. For any two tables \(M = \begin{bmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{bmatrix}\) and \(N = \begin{bmatrix} e & f \\ g & h \end{bmatrix}\), the product's determinant is the product of the determinants. Compute honestly, watching the middle terms cancel:

\[\det(MN) \;=\; (ae + bg)(cf + dh) - (af + bh)(ce + dg)\]

\[=\; \underbrace{aecf}_{\text{dies with } afce} + aedh + bgcf + \underbrace{bgdh}_{\text{dies with } bhdg} - \underbrace{afce}_{} - afdg - bhce - \underbrace{bhdg}_{}\]

\[=\; ad(eh - fg) + bc(gf - eh) \;=\; (ad - bc)(eh - fg) \;=\; \det(M)\det(N).\]

No geometry in it — just expansion — but two consequences carry the page's weight. First, a composite route's exchange rate is the product of its legs' rates (the treasure route's \((-1) \cdot 1 = -1\), checked in section 3). Second, a route through a broken scheme is broken all the way: \(\det(M)\det(N) = 0\) as soon as either factor is. Health and brokenness are detected at every leg of the journey, not only at the ends.

Claim 5 — the determinant's second job: \(|D|\) is the cell's area, and the exchange rate for every area. The scheme's cell — one step of each direction, the parallelogram with corners \(\vec 0\), \(\vec u = (p,q)\), \(\vec u + \vec v\), \(\vec v = (r,s)\) — is what one unit of scheme-area looks like on scheme \(A\)'s map (Fig B06.1 drew Ben's). The claim: that cell has area exactly \(|ps - qr|\). Proof in the drawn standard position — both columns in the first quadrant, \(\vec v\) steeper (\(q < s\)) and to the left (\(r < p\)), Elena's shape — by dissection. Bounding box: \((p + r)\) wide, \((q + s)\) tall, area \((p + r)(q + s)\). Outside the cell sit exactly four triangles (Fig B06.3):

  • bottom — \((0,0)\), \((p+r, 0)\), \((p, q)\): base \(p + r\), height \(q\), area \((p+r)q/2\);
  • top — \((p+r, q+s)\), \((0, q+s)\), \((r, s)\): base \(p + r\), height \(q\), area \((p+r)q/2\);
  • right — \((p+r, 0)\), \((p+r, q+s)\), \((p, q)\): base \(q + s\), width \(r\), area \(r(q+s)/2\);
  • left — \((0, q+s)\), \((0, 0)\), \((r, s)\): base \(q + s\), width \(r\), area \(r(q+s)/2\).

Add the outside, subtract from the box:

\[(p+r)(q+s) \;-\; \big[(p+r)q + r(q+s)\big] \;=\; pq + ps + rq + rs - pq - 2rq - rs \;=\; ps - qr.\]

Four terms of the box die against the triangles, two \(rq\)'s survive against one another, and what remains is the determinant. Run it on Elena (\(p = 3, q = 1, r = 1, s = 3\)): box \(16\), outside \((4)(1) + (1)(4) = 8\), cell \(16 - 8 = 8 = |D|\) — the dissection of Fig B06.3, every triangle a legs-4-and-1, area 2. What about schemes not in the drawn position — a column pointing left, or \(D < 0\)? Each is reached from the standard position by mirrors (which preserve area and flip \(D\)'s sign) and by swapping the two columns (same cell, \(D\) flips sign) — so \(|D|\) = area holds in every position, and the sign keeps its own job: \(D < 0\) is the handedness flip B05 noted on Ben's scheme, a mirrored cell. The corollary is the exchange rate promised in section 3(b): a region tiled by \(B\)-cells translates, cell by cell, into a region tiled by \(A\)-cells of area \(|D|\) — so any shape measured in lattice cells carries its area number multiplied by exactly \(|D|\) under the dictionary (the fence: 4 school units became \(\tfrac{1}{2}\) Elena cells under \(D = 8\), because her dictionary runs school to Elena). Curved regions need one more tool — the integral, one floor up — and the page says so and stops.

Scope, honestly. Three boundaries. (i) This page's dictionaries are all two-by-two, because its schemes are all the plane; larger schemes (three-space, function spaces) get larger tables and the same two demands, one floor up. (ii) The determinant's jobs stop at area and handedness here; its algebraic life — solving systems wholesale, the characteristic polynomial — belongs to later pages (B07 uses it to hunt directions). (iii) The area proof is complete for lattice-tiled regions; curved regions are deferred to the integral, honestly, and nothing on this page quietly uses them. What the page closes, it closes completely; what it defers, it names.
