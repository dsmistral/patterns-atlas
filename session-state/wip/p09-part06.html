6 · What to try next

The verdict came back; now route it. Three endings, three doors. Disagreement — the invariant blocked the target: write the impossibility down; the case is closed, and closed forever is the whole point of this move. Agreement — the invariant said nothing: the door may or may not be open, and only two things move the question — an explicit construction (P03's other half: build the route, or watch it fail and learn why) or a finer invariant that separates what this one could not. Nothing conserved — every candidate died the audit: before quitting, audit for one-way movement instead — block 5's fourth exhibit — because a quantity that never turns back governs the process even while it changes.

The routes out of this page, each with its own door:

P10 — force a collision. The next page of the spine, and the mirror pose of this one: where the invariant proves that nobody can reach a target, the pigeonhole proves that somebody must — more objects than boxes forces two into one box, and the art is arranging the boxes. Impossibility here; existence there; the spine alternates them deliberately.

P11 — prove by contradiction. Look back at what this page actually argued: assume a route exists; then every step preserves the quantity, so start and target must agree; they do not; contradiction. The invariant argument is a proof by contradiction in miniature — P11 names the general shape and trains it on purpose.

P12 — name the obstruction. The direct heir. When an invariant blocks a target, mathematics does not stop at "no": it promotes the number that refuses to change into a named structure — an obstruction — and classifies everything that number blocks. Far up the ladder, this promotion grows into the machinery behind cohomology and impossibility theorems of every kind. P12 takes the first step of that promotion.

P72 — find the monovariant. The cousin from block 5: the quantity that is never conserved but never turns back. When this move has nothing to say, that one often ends the argument instead.

P75 — coloring arguments. The mutilated chessboard was this move plus one chosen colouring; the choosing is an art of its own, with its own catalogue of paints. When a grid or board resists, P75 is where the palette lives.

M03 — Pick's theorem. The multi-pattern showcase that runs this move end to end: experiment with lattice polygons (P02) until the invariant surfaces — the area minus the interior count minus half the boundary count refuses to move — and the theorem falls out of what refuses to move.

Deferred honestly, with reasons. Two deep relatives of this move wait up the ladder, and both need pages you have not climbed. Energy methods (P29) is the invariant idea at continuous scale: a quantity that only decreases forces a differential equation to settle, the way a conserved quantity forces a walk to stay in its class — it needs the equation-modelling move (P26) first. Localize at a prime (P30) is the digit-sum trick grown up: work one prime at a time, where the invariant is "divisibility by \(p\)," and reassemble — it needs the descent move (P13) first. Both are this page's descendants; neither is reachable yet, and pretending otherwise would violate the ladder's one law.

7 · Lineage

Invariants are old currency — casting out nines was a merchant's check centuries before congruence classes had a name (B02 only dressed it in 19th-century clothes; Gauss, 1801). The mutilated chessboard was posed by the philosopher Max Black in 1946, in Critical Thinking, as a parable about how evidence works: no pile of failed attempts adds up to a proof — one colour census does. Martin Gardner made it famous in his Scientific American columns. And at the research frontier the idea has a theorem with a name attached: Emmy Noether, in her 1918 paper Invariante Variationsprobleme, proved that every symmetry of a physical law forces a conserved quantity — time-translation symmetry is energy conservation; space-translation symmetry is momentum conservation. Noether's theorem is this page and P08 shaking hands: the move that preserves (symmetry) and the quantity preserved (invariant) are two views of one fact. None of this history is load-bearing here — but when a physicist says "energy is conserved," they are running the ninth move of the spine.
