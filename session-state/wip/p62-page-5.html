Exercise 1 ●○○ — the empty law and the almanac, by hand. (a) On the two-island world, list all covers of the empty stretch — including the empty family — and explain why exactly one matching family exists on the empty cover; conclude that \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) must carry exactly one record, for any sheaf \\(F\\). (b) The almanac presheaf \\(P = (1,2,2,2)\\) with identity restrictions: check it is separated — two different country-records differ on the cover \\(\\{E, W\\}\\) — and then exhibit the matching family on that cover that no country-record witnesses. (c) Compute \\(P^+(X)\\) by hand: list the matching families on the cover \\(\\{E,W\\}\\), quotient by Box P62.A's equivalence, and verify the count is \\(4\\).

Hint

For (a), a matching family on a cover \\(C\\) assigns a record to each member, agreeing on pairwise overlaps — with no members, there is one such assignment, the empty one. For (b), the report \\((0 \\text{ on } E,\\; 1 \\text{ on } W)\\) agrees on the (empty) overlap. For (c), two families are equivalent when their readings agree on every overlap of a member of one with a member of the other — most of the overlap checks are vacuous here, but the trivial family \\((\\{X\\}, s)\\) is equivalent to \\((\\{E,W\\}, (s,s))\\), and that is where the count drops.

Worked answer

(a) The covers of \\(\\varnothing\\) are the empty family and \\(\\{\\varnothing\\}\\). On the empty family there is exactly one assignment (none at all), it is vacuously matching, and gluing demands every record of \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) witness it while locality demands no two do: \\(|F(\\varnothing)| = 1\\). (b) The records \\(0, 1 \\in P(X)\\) restrict to \\((0,0)\\) and \\((1,1)\\) on the cover — different tuples, so separated. The report \\((0 \\text{ on } E, 1 \\text{ on } W)\\) is matching — \\(E \\cap W = \\varnothing\\) — and a witness \\(s \\in P(X)\\) would need \\(s|_E = 0\\) and \\(s|_W = 1\\), but restriction is the identity: no such \\(s\\). Gluing fails. (c) Matching families on \\(\\{E,W\\}\\): all \\(2 \\times 2 = 4\\) pairs. Equivalences: the trivial family \\((\\{X\\}, 0)\\) restricts to \\((0,0)\\), so it merges with the pair \\((0,0)\\); likewise \\((\\{X\\},1)\\) with \\((1,1)\\); the pairs \\((0,1)\\) and \\((1,0)\\) match no trivial family. Classes: \\(\\{\\text{const } 0\\}\\), \\(\\{\\text{const } 1\\}\\), \\((0,1)\\), \\((1,0)\\) — four records, which is \\(P^+(X)\\), and the second round changes nothing: \\(P^+ = (1,2,2,4)\\) is already the repair.

Exercise 2 ●●○ — a sheafification computed honestly. On the diamond world with the \\(J_1\\) site of §4, take the presheaf \\(F\\) with one record at \\(a\\), two at \\(b\\), two at \\(c\\), one at \\(m\\), all restrictions to \\(m\\) trivial. (a) Verify \\(F\\) is separated on the declared cover \\(\\{b,c\\}\\) of \\(a\\), and count the matching families on that cover. (b) Compute \\(F^+(a)\\) by hand — matching families modulo equivalence, over all covers of \\(a\\) — and verify \\(|F^+(a)| = 4\\). (c) Check that \\(F^+\\) passes the sheaf test on the cover \\(\\{b,c\\}\\) of \\(a\\): the restriction of \\(F^+\\)'s records at \\(a\\) to the pair \\((F^+(b), F^+(c))\\) must be a bijection onto the matching pairs.

Hint

(a) With one record at \\(a\\), separatedness is automatic; the matching pairs are the pairs \\((x, y)\\) agreeing on \\(m\\) — and every pair agrees, because \\(m\\) carries one record. (b) The covers of \\(a\\) are \\(\\{a\\}\\) and \\(\\{b,c\\}\\); the trivial family merges with the pair it restricts to. (c) \\(F^+(b) = F(b)\\) and \\(F^+(c) = F(c)\\) — the declared cover touches only \\(a\\).

Worked answer

(a) One record at \\(a\\) means the locality map \\(F(a) \\to F(b) \\times F(c)\\) is trivially injective; the matching pairs are all \\(2 \\times 2 = 4\\), since both restrictions to \\(m\\) are constant to the single record. Only \\((0,0)\\) — the restrictions of \\(a\\)'s record, under its two restriction maps — has a witness: gluing fails, and the three unwitnessed pairs are §4's growth receipt. (b) Families on the trivial cover: \\(1\\); on \\(\\{b,c\\}\\): \\(4\\); the trivial family is equivalent to the pair it restricts to, so \\(|F^+(a)| = 1 + 4 - 1 = 4\\) — the receipt \\((1,2,2,1) \\to (4,2,2,1)\\). (c) \\(F^+\\)'s four records at \\(a\\) are the classes: the constant class, and the three patch pairs; restricting to \\(b\\) and \\(c\\) reads off the pair \\((x,y)\\), so the map \\(F^+(a) \\to F^+(b) \\times F^+(c)\\) lands in the matching pairs (all pairs match here) and is a bijection — four records, four pairs, and the sheaf test passes. One round sufficed: the diamond's receipt, and the lab's too.

Exercise 3 ●●● — the diamond, two sites, by hand. (a) Prove that under the trivial site \\(J_0\\) (every region covered only by itself), every presheaf is a sheaf — locality and gluing on the trivial cover are automatic. (b) Now the \\(J_1\\) slice with \\(s_b = s_c = s_m = 1\\) and \\(s_a \\in \\{1, 2\\}\\: determine, by hand, which of these presheaves are sheaves, and name the failure mode of the others. (c) The slice with \\(s_b = s_c = 2\\), \\(s_m = 1\\): show that no presheaf with \\(s_a \\leq 2\\) is a \\(J_1\\)-sheaf, and explain the obstruction using matching families.

Hint

(a) On the cover \\(\\{U\\}\\), the locality map is the identity. (b) With \\(F(b) = F(c) = F(m) = 1\\), the matching pairs on \\(\\{b,c\\}\\) number one; a sheaf needs \\(F(a)\\) in bijection with it. (c) Count the matching pairs first — \\(m\\)'s single record makes agreement free — and compare with the largest allowed \\(s_a\\).

Worked answer

(a) The one cover of \\(U\\) is \\(\\{U\\}\\); the locality map \\(F(U) \\to F(U)\\) is the identity — injective; and every matching family on \\(\\{U\\}\\) is the restriction of its own member — gluing holds. No data can fail, so all \\(190\\) census presheaves are \\(J_0\\)-sheaves. (b) The matching pair is unique — the readings at \\(b\\) and \\(c\\) are forced, and they agree at \\(m\\) trivially. A sheaf needs the restriction map \\(F(a) \\to 1\\) bijective: \\(s_a = 1\\) passes (any one record, restricting to the forced readings); \\(s_a = 2\\) fails locality — two records at \\(a\\) agreeing on every member of the cover — and its repair merges them, the receipt \\((2,1,1,1) \\to (1,1,1,1)\\) of §4. (c) With \\(s_m = 1\\), the restrictions to \\(m\\) are constant, so every pair \\((x,y) \\in F(b) \\times F(c)\\) is matching: \\(4\\) matching pairs. The sheaf condition demands \\(F(a) \\cong 4\\) pairs via restriction; \\(s_a \\leq 2\\) cannot biject with \\(4\\). No sheaf exists in the slice — the obstruction is gluing for every \\(s_a = 1\\) presheaf (three unwitnessed pairs) and, for \\(s_a = 2\\), whichever failure the restriction maps pick first; the census of §4 records \\(84\\) separated-not-gluing presheaves, and this slice is their home ground.

Exercise 4 ◆ — the consultant's two offices.

Two offices keep identical bookkeeping: three regions — a point-office \\(\\{o\\}\\), the whole country \\(X\\), and the empty stretch — with records \\(F(\\varnothing) = \\{\\star\\}\\), \\(F(\\{o\\}) = \\{0, 1\\}\\), \\(F(X) = \\{0, 1\\}\\), and restriction \\(X \\to \\{o\\}\\) the identity. Office A audits and declares the bookkeeping a sheaf; Office B audits the same records and declares a locality failure. Both audits ran every cover. You have this page: what is going on, and which office is right?

Worked answer

The records do not decide; the site does, and the two offices must be auditing under two covering rules — this page's Key 2, arriving as a consulting report. Office A's world: the closed-point world of §4's Encounter 3, where the point \\(c\\) beside \\(o\\) has \\(X\\) as its only neighborhood, so every cover of \\(X\\) contains \\(X\\) itself — the gluing law has nothing to bite, the empty stretch is honest, and the bookkeeping is a sheaf (one of the \\(8\\)). Office B's world: the two-island shape in disguise — a second open region inside \\(X\\) beside \\(\\{o\\}\\), with \\(X\\) covered by two proper opens — where the two country-records \\(0\\) and \\(1\\) restrict to the same readings on both members of that cover: locality fails, and the repair merges them (Exhibit A's verdict). Both offices are right — about their own sites. The diagnosis the consultant owes the client: "sheaf" is a verdict about data and covers together; the audit report that does not state its covering rule has not said anything.

Exercise 5 ◐ — the paper trail.

(a) Find the plus construction in the literature: Godement's 1958 reference book is the classical home of the decree version and the flèche; modern texts build sheafification either by matching families, by stalks and germs (the étalé route), or by a transfinite iteration — locate one, and write down how your source phrases the equivalence relation on families and the canonical map \\(\\eta\\). (b) Compare your source's equivalence with Box P62.A's: the same decree, or a finer one? Check what happens on this page's two-island world, where every cover was enumerated, so the comparison is finite. (c) Open end: find a source that states how many iterations the construction needs on a general site, and reconcile it with this page's two-round receipt — what exactly does "two" depend on, and what did the lab verify?

Hint

(a) The classical name to search alongside faisceaux is germe (germ) and flèche canonique; the SGA 4 volumes are the site-level reference, and any standard topos-theory text carries one of the three constructions. (b) Look at whether the source's families range over all covering families or a fixed basis, and whether agreement is required on pairwise overlaps only. (c) The reconciliation turns on finiteness: what can be checked by exhaustion, and what needs a convergence proof.

Worked answer

(a) Honest protocol first, per the scope box: if the source and this page ever disagree, the source wins and this page's lab gets corrected — the exercise's product is the comparison, and the correction if one is needed. The receipts this page can already offer: Godement's Topologie algébrique et théorie des faisceaux (Hermann, 1958) and SGA 4's publication data (seminars 1963–64; Springer Lecture Notes in Mathematics 269, 270, 305, 1972–73) were web-verified for this page; the rest of the literature trail is the reader's to verify, and Exercise 5's answer is deliberately the protocol, not a citation list. (b) The standard decree equates two families when they agree on every overlap of a member of one with a member of the other — Box P62.A's relation verbatim where covers are unrestricted; sources that work with a basis of covers (or a basis of neighborhoods, in the stalk construction) restrict the families but produce the same classes on honest sites. On the two-island world the lab enumerated all ten covers of the country, so basis-free and basis-relative agree by exhaustion — the census is the check. (c) The two-round receipt is a finite-world exhaustion: the lab checked a third round against the second for all \\(190\\) presheaves and found identity. General sites do not admit exhaustion, and the honest general statement iterates plus to a fixed point — finitely on fortunate sites, transfinitely in general — with convergence proved, not assumed. What "two" depends on: the site being small enough that separated-then-glued is reachable in two passes — and the diamond world's one-round receipt shows even that is site-dependent. The open end is genuinely open: the transfinite construction is this page's named, unbuilt door, and the scope box says so.
