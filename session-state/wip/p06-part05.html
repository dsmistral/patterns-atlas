Example 2 (geometry) — rotations: the addition formulas are B12 on the circle

Rotate the plane by \\(\alpha\\), then by \\(\beta\\): the result is a rotation by \\(\alpha + \beta\\). Now write each rotation as what it does to the point \\((1, 0)\\) — it moves it to \\((\cos t, \sin t)\\) — and compose with B12's rule. The composition \\((\cos\alpha, \sin\alpha) \odot (\cos\beta, \sin\beta)\\) is

\[(\cos\alpha\cos\beta - \sin\alpha\sin\beta,\ \ \cos\alpha\sin\beta + \sin\alpha\cos\beta) = (\cos(\alpha+\beta),\ \sin(\alpha+\beta))\]

The angle-addition formulas — usually memorized as separate facts — are one fact: they are B12's recipe applied to the circle. The invariant explains why the circle stays the circle: every rotation pair has norm \\(1\\), so composing two of them multiplies norms \\(1 \cdot 1 = 1\\) — the answer is on the circle again, automatically. The lab verifies the composition formula over the full grid \\(\alpha, \beta \in \{-180^\circ, -172.8^\circ, \dots, 180^\circ\}\\) — \\(51 \times 51 = 2{,}601\\) pairs — with worst error \\(5.135 \times 10^{-16}\\). The encounter's \\(\cos 75^\circ\\) is one cell of that grid: compose \\(45^\circ\\) with \\(30^\circ\\), both routes agree — the exact value \\(\tfrac{\sqrt{6}-\sqrt{2}}{4} = 0.2588190451\\) against the direct \\(\cos 75^\circ\\) to ten decimals, and \\(\cos 70^\circ = 0.3420201433\\) the same way. Composing an angle with itself doubles it — the double-angle formulas \\(\cos 2t = \cos^2 t - \sin^2 t\\) and \\(\sin 2t = 2\sin t\cos t\\) are the self-composition, verified by the lab over 73 angles to \\(2.220 \times 10^{-16}\\). Fig P06.3 shows the composed turn.

One caution belongs here rather than in block 5, because the figure tempts it: the composition adds angles, and angles are not coordinates. What doubles is the angle, never the sine — block 5 runs the false line \\(2\sin 35^\circ = 1.147 \ne \sin 70^\circ = 0.940\\) as a must-fail witness. Moves used: P06 on the circle pairs; the grid verification is P02's detector discipline; the exact-route-vs-numeric-route comparison is the two-routes demand of Box P06.A.

Example 3 (algebra) — the Fibonacci machine: composing a matrix with itself

From your class-12 shelf, the second old friend: the \\(2 \times 2\\) matrix, and its determinant \\(\det\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\\\ c & d \end{pmatrix} = ad - bc\\). Matrix multiplication is a composition — an operation on matrices with identity \\(I\\) and a multiplicative invariant: \\(\det(AB) = \det(A)\det(B)\\), verified by the lab over \\(20 \times 20 = 400\\) matrix pairs to \\(4.441 \times 10^{-16}\\). Now compose the matrix \\(Q = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\\\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}\\) with itself, \\(n\\) times:

\[Q^2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 1 \\\\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix},\quad Q^3 = \begin{pmatrix} 3 & 2 \\\\ 2 & 1 \end{pmatrix},\quad Q^4 = \begin{pmatrix} 5 & 3 \\\\ 3 & 2 \end{pmatrix},\quad \dots\]

The Fibonacci numbers walk in: \\(1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, \dots\\), and the pattern \\(Q^n = \begin{pmatrix} F(n+1) & F(n) \\\\ F(n) & F(n-1) \end{pmatrix}\\) (the lab verifies it for every \\(n\\) from 1 to 40, where \\(F(41) = 165{,}580{,}141\\)). Now the invariant does the work. \\(\det Q = 1 \cdot 0 - 1 \cdot 1 = -1\\), so \\(\det Q^n = (-1)^n\\); computed instead from the entries of \\(Q^n\\), the same determinant reads

\[F(n+1)\,F(n-1) - F(n)^2 = (-1)^n\]

Cassini's identity — a real theorem about Fibonacci numbers — manufactured by composing a matrix with itself and reading the invariant. One composition, two routes (expand \\(Q^n\\) versus multiply determinants), one identity for free: that is the whole move. The same machine proves the addition formula \\(F(m+n) = F(m)F(n+1) + F(m-1)F(n)\\), because \\(Q^{m+n} = Q^m Q^n\\) — composing \\(m\\) steps with \\(n\\) steps is \\(m+n\\) steps; the lab verifies it over the full \\(25 \times 25\\) grid, with the instances \\(F(10) = 55 = 40 + 15\\) (\\(m = n = 5\\)) and \\(F(12) = 144 = 105 + 39\\) (\\(m = 5, n = 7\\)). And the legality probe runs here too: \\(Q^n I = Q^n\\), the identity matrix composing like the identity pair \\((1,0)\\) and the \\(0^\circ\\) turn. Moves used: P06, with the determinant as the multiplicative invariant; the pattern-spotting in \\(Q^2, Q^3, Q^4\\) is P02's table discipline, its proof-by-composition the upgrade this page teaches.
