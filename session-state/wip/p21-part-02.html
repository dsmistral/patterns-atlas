1 · Before we begin

The tier-2 ladder so far, one breath: P18 lifted a stuck problem into a room with one more dimension and descended the answer, P19 proved things exist by showing a random pick finds them, and P20 certified identities by testing at enough points instead of expanding. Three moves, three kinds of room — and notice what all three had in common: the work was finite. P18 lifted finitely many points, P19's coin was priced at finitely many chances per throw, P20's certificate tested \(d + 1\) points and stopped. The ladder arrives here carrying three tools this page leans on hard. P10, because the pigeonhole principle is this page's engine in miniature — more attempts than boxes forces a collision — and the whole move ahead is what happens when the attempts are not just more than the boxes but endless. P15, because its walls and its ε-trap already halved intervals to trap answers between computables, and this page is the first to ask what guarantees the trapped point is there at all. And P03, because a room is a claim, and claims are attacked: the §5 exhibits are hostile rooms, and each one kills a version of the move that forgot to check its fine print.

Here is the tension this page resolves. The shooter's record is infinite — no last hit, no complete census, no \(d + 1\) stopping rule. Every existence claim so far was paid for by exhibiting or certifying: P07 built the object, P19's witness existed because the expectation said so, P20's identity held because the difference had no room to hide. But the crowd an endless record forms is exhibited by nobody: no formula names the spot, and no finite battery of tests can certify where infinity gathers. The good news: the machine that catches it is three sentences long, uses nothing but school arithmetic, and every number on this page runs through it. The bad news, paid up front: the machine's fine print is where all the danger lives. The room must be closed and bounded — two separate requirements, each with its own wreckage in §5 — the catch is never unique unless the record is, and the whole engine silently spends one assumption P15 also spent without saying so: that the number line has no holes. This page names that assumption, spends it in the open, and shows what it buys: two theorems proved in full, a third that is the same proof in a costume, and the wreckage that prices the fine print.
