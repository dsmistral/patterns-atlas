The Patterns Atlas · P09 · the ninth move of the spine

Find the invariant

When every attempt fails, stop asking "what am I missing?" and start asking "what can nothing change?" — one quantity no move touches turns a pile of failed tries into a proof that nobody, ever, will succeed.

New word: state. A state is one complete snapshot of the situation: every number on the board, every colour count in the meadow, every coin face on the table. The moves are the rules that turn one state into the next, and the whole process is a walk from state to state. New word: invariant. An invariant is a quantity computed from the state that every allowed move leaves exactly unchanged — not roughly, not usually: exactly, always. New word: conserved. The physicists' word for the same coat: a quantity is conserved by a process when no step of the process can spend it. Same move, two coats; this page wears both.

1 · Before we begin

The spine so far: P01 pinned the claim, P02 built the table, P03 attacked it with hostile cases, P04 paid for corners, P05 dressed every object in its uniform, P06 composed stuck objects inside identities, P07 built the objects nobody handed us, and P08 divided by the twins. P08's closing pointer promised exactly this page: a symmetry is a move that preserves something; the something it preserves is the twin concept. P08 hunted the moves and divided by them. This page hunts the something — the quantity sitting dead still while everything visible churns.

Here is the tension this page resolves. P02 and P03 trained one instinct: try small cases, stress the claim, trust the wreckage. But some problems hand you a target that no amount of trying reaches — and the small cases do not "fail," they fail every single time. Sooner or later you owe yourself a question: is this hard, or is it impossible? From the inside, the two feel identical. The good news: one well-chosen conserved quantity separates them forever. If the start and the target disagree on something no move can change, then no sequence of moves — however long, however clever — connects them. That is the strongest sentence in mathematics: not "I could not do it," but "nobody will ever do it." The bad news, stated honestly up front: the invariant does not announce itself. No recipe takes a problem and prints its invariant; finding it is the skill, and a quantity that merely looks conserved is this move's most seductive wrong answer (block 5 collects the wreckage).
