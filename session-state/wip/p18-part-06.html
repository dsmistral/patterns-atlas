The descent, watched. The three sentences just proved Desargues for triangles in different planes; §2 asked about two triangles in the same plane. The gap between them is the return trip, and the honest way down is a construction — which means it can be run, checked, and audited. Run it on the very triangles of §2. Choose a center off the plane: \(S = (0,0,5)\), straight above the origin, and on the line from \(S\) down to the planar center \(O = (0,0,0)\) choose the lifted center \(O_1 = (0,0,2)\). Now walk each unprimed vertex of §2 partway up its ray from \(S\), and build each primed vertex as a two-line meet in a plane that contains \(S\): the line through \(O_1\) and the lifted vertex, met with the ray from \(S\) through the primed vertex. Why the two lines always meet — the one geometric sentence the construction needs: the line \(O_1A_1\) and the ray \(SA'\) both lie in the plane through \(S\), \(O\), and \(A\) (the first because \(O_1\) sits on \(SO\) and \(A_1\) on \(SA\); the second because \(A'\) sits on the line \(OA\), which is in that plane), and two lines in one plane meet unless parallel — the parallel case is a measure-zero accident this construction's choices avoid, and the lab checks all three meets by substitution. Now audit the structure: the two lifted triangles lie in two different planes (both different from \(z = 0\)); the lines \(A_1A_2\), \(B_1B_2\), \(C_1C_2\) all pass through \(O_1\) — \(C_2\) sits on the ray opposite \(C_1\), which perspectivity permits; and the center \(S\) lies on none of the lines involved. So the spatial theorem applies: the three meets of the lifted triangles' corresponding sides are collinear, on the line where the two lifted planes cross. Finally project the whole structure from \(S\) back onto the plane \(z = 0\). Projection from a point carries lines to lines and preserves meets; the six lifted vertices project exactly onto §2's six vertices — check one in your head: the ray from \(S = (0,0,5)\) through \(A_1 = (\frac{1}{2}, 0, \frac{5}{2})\) continues past \(A_1\) to \((1, 0, 0) = A\), and the same ray hits the plane at \(A\); the lab verifies all six — so the lifted configuration's side-line meets project exactly onto \(P\), \(Q\), \(R\), and the lifted axis projects to a line through them. The line \(y = -3x - 3\) is the shadow of the axis upstairs. §2's coincidence is explained — not verified, explained: the three meets are collinear because they are shadows of three points on one line, in a room the plane could not see.

What the construction teaches. The descent consumed three things the plane cannot supply by itself: a point \(S\) off the plane, a lifted center \(O_1\), and the freedom to leave and come back. A proof of planar Desargues from planar incidence alone would consume none of these — and Exhibit B will show why waiting for one is a category error: there are planes for which the whole construction is impossible, because there is no \(S\). Your plane keeps Desargues because it is a shadow, and the shadow has a cause one dimension up. That is the pattern's signature sentence, and every example on this page is a variation on it.
