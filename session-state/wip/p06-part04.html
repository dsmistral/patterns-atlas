4 · The move at work

Three worked examples, one per domain. Each names every pattern used.

Example 1 (numbers) — the mandated application: Brahmagupta–Fibonacci, read as multiplying moduli

From your class-12 shelf, one old friend: the complex number \\(z = a + bi\\), with \\(i^2 = -1\\). Read B12's pairs as complex numbers — the pair \\((a, b)\\) is \\(a + bi\\) — and B12's composition rule becomes plain multiplication:

\[(a + bi)(c + di) = (ac - bd) + (ad + bc)i\]

so \\((a, b) \odot (c, d)\\) is exactly the product of the two complex numbers. The modulus, \\(|z| = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}\\), is the length of \\(z\\) drawn as an arrow — and the norm of B12 is the squared modulus: \\(N(a, b) = a^2 + b^2 = |z|^2\\). In this language the bridge's identity becomes one clean line, and it is this page's mandated application:

\[|z_1 z_2|^2 = |z_1|^2 \, |z_2|^2\]

Why believe it? Box P06.A, demand by demand. Identity: \\((1, 0)\\), the number \\(1\\) itself — the lab probes \\((a,b) \odot (1,0) = (a,b)\\) over 169 pairs, zero exceptions. Multiplicative invariant: the norm — the lab verifies \\(N(z \odot w) = N(z)N(w)\\) on both routes over all \\(11^4 = 14{,}641\\) quadruples \\(a, b, c, d\\) from 0 to 10, zero mismatches. Routes: B12's two routes give different output pairs with the same invariant — two witnesses per composition. And one lemma pays for itself all session: the conjugate \\(\bar{z} = a - bi\\) (flip the second coordinate) satisfies \\(z \bar{z} = (a^2 + b^2) + 0i\\) — composing with the conjugate parks the whole norm on the horizontal axis. The lab verifies \\(z \bar{z} = (N(z), 0)\\) over all \\(13^4 = 28{,}561\\) quadruples. (B12 promised the reader a conjugation aside eventually; this is it, arriving with work to do.)

Now aim the machine. Composing with the identity manufactures nothing — that is its certification. Composing with \\((1, 1)\\) — the norm-2 pair — manufactures a doubler: \\((a, b) \odot (1, 1) = (a - b,\ a + b)\\), whose norm is \\((a-b)^2 + (a+b)^2 = 2(a^2 + b^2)\\). That is the doubling identity \\(2(a^2 + b^2) = (a+b)^2 + (a-b)^2\\), discovered rather than memorized, and it holds because \\(2 = N(1,1)\\) and norms multiply. Witness: \\((3, 4)\\), norm 25, composes to \\((-1, 7)\\), norm \\(1 + 49 = 50 = 2 \cdot 25\\). Fig P06.2 shows both compositions as one machine.

And the witness factory scales. B12's Lecture 0 produced \\(221 = 17 \cdot 13\\) through both routes — \\((-10, 11)\\) and \\((14, -5)\\); the encounter produced four hypotenuse-65 triangles by squaring and cross-composing. Here is a census, done the composed way. Is \\(1025\\) a sum of two squares, and in how many ways? Factor: \\(1025 = 5^2 \cdot 41\\), with \\(5 = 1^2 + 2^2\\) and \\(41 = 4^2 + 5^2\\). Build \\(5^2\\) first: \\((1,2) \odot (1,2)\\) gives the two routes \\((-3, 4)\\) and \\((5, 0)\\) — the second trivial, parked on the axis (composing a pair with itself can park a witness on the axis; the lab's conjugation lemma is the same phenomenon pointed backwards). Compose each with \\((4, 5)\\): from \\((3,4)\\), the routes give \\((-8, 31)\\) and \\((32, -1)\\); from \\((5, 0)\\), both routes give \\((20, 25)\\). So \\(1025 = 1^2 + 32^2 = 8^2 + 31^2 = 20^2 + 25^2\\) — three representations, every one route-produced, one of them needing the trivial rep. The lab census confirms three is exactly right (every pair with \\(x, y \le 33\\) scanned) and that all three arise this way. Hunting the same census by hand means testing 32 candidate squares; composing means three multiplications. Moves used: P06, anchored on B12's two routes; the census discipline is P02's; the small probes are P02's detector honesty.
