5 · Exhibits: when the repair forgets

Every pattern earns its keep by failing. Three exhibits — a repair that merges records the data never distinguished locally, a demand so small it is easy to miss, and an iteration that stops at two here but is not promised to — with the mitigation, the price, and the default.

Exhibit A — the repair that merges. A country office keeps one record on the empty stretch, one on each island, and two on the country — the presheaf \\((1,1,1,2)\\) — with both country-records restricting to the same island-reading. The office insists the two records are different: different files, different fortunes, different theories. The repair answers: not locally. Two records of a sheaf are equal exactly when they agree on every cover — and these agree everywhere they restrict. The repair \\((1,1,1,2) \\to (1,1,1,1)\\) merges them, machine-checked: the two become one, Fig P62.3. Universality delivers the same verdict independently, and this is the receipt to remember: any restriction-respecting map from this presheaf into any sheaf must already identify the two records — their images agree on the islands, and a sheaf's records are determined by their local behavior — so no honest enlargement could ever have kept them apart. The merge is not the construction's clumsiness; it is the data's own verdict about itself.

Mitigation: run locality before repairing — it is a finite check on covers (two records of \\(F(U)\\) agreeing on every member of some cover of \\(U\\)), and in the two-island census it catches \\(9\\) presheaves with \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) honest, before any machinery runs.

Price: the merge is information destroyed, and if the merged records carried meaning — two theories, two claims, two owners — that meaning was never local, and the repair is the wrong tool: the honest fix is better data, not a bigger world.

Default: treat a locality failure as a diagnosis, not a stain. The data disagreed with its own restrictions; find out which side was lying before building anything.

Exhibit B — the empty set's demand. The smallest cover in the world is the empty family covering the empty region, and it makes the loudest demand: exactly one matching family exists on it (the empty one), so honest records on \\(\\varnothing\\) are exactly one — \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) must be a single point, always. In the two-island census, \\(156\\) of \\(190\\) presheaves die on this alone: two records on the empty stretch, a bookkeeping fiction the empty cover does not recognize. The demand is not pedantry; it is the gluing law's anchor. Gluing over the empty cover says every element of \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) comes from the (unique) empty family; locality says at most one does; together: exactly one. And the anchor generalizes — gluing over disconnected covers is what forces records on a region to be families of records on its pieces, which is the entire construction.

Mitigation: normalize first — check \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) before anything else; it is the cheapest check in the machine, and in this page's census it resolves \\(156\\) verdicts instantly.

Price: none. The demand is free, and presheaves that fail it were never honest candidates.

Default: the empty-set law is the pre-flight check of the whole move; the Sierpiński world of §4 shows its reach — there, even with every cover of the country containing the country itself, the \\(28\\) presheaves carrying two empty-stretch records still fail, all of them, on this one demand.

Exhibit C — the iteration that stops here. On the two-island world, two rounds of plus provably suffice: the lab checks a third round against the second for all \\(190\\) presheaves and finds no change — sizes, restrictions, everything stable. On the diamond world with one declared cover, one round suffices, checked the same way. It is tempting to read "plus twice" as a theorem. It is not. On general sites the plus construction is iterated — finitely where fortune allows, transfinitely where it does not, taking a merged record at limit stages — and the proof of convergence is part of the general construction, not a remark. Worse, on sites too large to be sets of records in a fixed universe, the sheaf category itself can require set-theoretic hygiene (a Grothendieck universe) to exist. This page's receipts are exhaustion receipts of finite worlds, honestly labeled as such; the general theorem is named, not proved, and the scope box holds the debt.

Mitigation: on finite sites, iterate until stable and prove stability by exhaustion — the lab's third-round check is the receipt; never claim a round count you have not checked or cited.

Price: on general sites there is no finite census to run, and the honest statement carries its hypotheses (smallness, universes) openly.

Default: "plus twice" is this page's receipt, not the world's theorem. Say which world you are in, and check.

6 · Doors out

  • Door — straighten and unstraighten (P61): the nearest door, and this page's footer. This page built one repair and read it two ways — matching families at every region, germs at every point — and the two readings were the same sheaf. P61 makes that duality a discipline: compute in the straightened world, interpret in the curved one, and the invariant is what survives the round trip. The étalé route of §4 is P61's method arriving early.
  • Door — the sheaf cohomology bridge (P33): the prerequisite, read backward. P33 measured the refusal — data immovable, the obstruction a fact about the shape; this page repaired the world — data yours, the refusal a defect of the hosting. The two moves are one toolkit: measure first; repair only what was bookkeeping to begin with. A cohomology class on a repaired presheaf is a confession that the repair was the wrong question.
  • Door — group completion (P60): the tier sibling. P60 completed one monoid, in one place, by a universal property; this page ran the same discipline point by point over a whole site. The \\(\\eta\\) that embeds and the \\(\\eta\\) that merges are the same arrow, honestly priced on both pages — and P60's warning returns here louder: repairs that erase are verdicts.
  • Door — the diagram chase (P47): the consumer. Cohomology is computed from resolutions, resolutions are built by sheafified functors, and the exactness that makes a chase run is sheaf-exactness — this page's repairs are what P47's squares are drawn in. The door is one move away and already half-open.
  • Door — representable functor (P48): the universal property, one language up. "Every presheaf maps into sheaves, restriction-respectingly" assembles into a functor, and the repair represents it: sheafification is the left adjoint to the forgetful functor from sheaves to presheaves. P48 taught that an object is its maps; this page's repair is the representing object, and the adjunction is the dashed arrow made into grammar.
  • Door — the literature: the lineage below names the entries this page verified (Godement 1958; SGA 4) and the two it quotes as literature-consistent without source verification — Giraud's stacks and the Lawvere–Tierney closure. 7 · Lineage

  • 1946 — Leray; 1950–51 — Cartan; 1955 — Serre; 1957 — Grothendieck. The sheaf's own history, pinned on P33's page and quoted here as cross-page pins: Leray's deliberately abstract machine, born in a prison camp and published in 1946; Cartan's seminar rebuilding it for complex analysis; Serre's FAC running it on algebraic varieties; Grothendieck's Tôhoku paper re-founding the bookkeeping. Sheafification enters this story quietly: a sheaf, once named, immediately raises this page's question — what is the nearest sheaf to data that is not one yet?
  • 1958 — Godement. Topologie algébrique et théorie des faisceaux (Hermann, 1958; web-verified for this page) wrote the young theory down as a reference, and the canonical map — the flèche — from a presheaf to the sheaf of germs of its sections is this page's \\(\\eta\\) by the étalé route: Encounter 3's stalks, germs, and continuous sections, published three years after Tôhoku as bookkeeping. The plus construction of Box P62.A is the decree version of the same repair; both produce the left adjoint, and this page checks their receipt counts against each other on every presheaf of the census.
  • 1963–64 (published 1972–73) — Artin, Grothendieck, Verdier. SGA 4 moved the whole story to the site — regions and covers abstracted away from any underlying space — and defined sheaves, and sheafification, on it; the seminar ran in 1963–64 and was published as Springer's Lecture Notes in Mathematics 269, 270, and 305 in 1972–73 (publication data web-verified for this page). The site is why this page's diamond world is lawful: covers are declared structure, not geography, and the sheaf census changes when the declaration does — SGA 4's world, run here at desk size. Down the same decade and the next two doors: Giraud's stacks (1971) gluing categories with the same law this page glues sets, and the Lawvere–Tierney elementary topos with sheafification as an intrinsic closure — both quoted as literature-consistent, not source-verified, per the scope box.

None of this history is load-bearing for the pattern; the receipts above are internal to this page, and the lineage is a map of doors, not a proof.
