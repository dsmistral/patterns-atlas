Bézout's identity via descent

Run the Euclidean dance backwards and the gcd stops being a number you found — it becomes a combination you built: whole stacks of \(a\), whole anti-stacks of \(b\), adding to \(\gcd(a, b) = ax + by\).

What this page is. This is a bridge page: it teaches a concept, not a problem-solving pattern, and it directly extends B03 (the Euclidean algorithm). B03's dance computes \(\gcd(a, b)\) — but three of group A's open debts need more than the dance's answer; they need the gcd's address, its expression as \(ax + by\) for computable whole numbers \(x, y\). This page builds that expression — the climb — proves it by descent, and spends it at once: the inverse 1321 that B03 brute-forced gets produced instead, P05's fraction-uniqueness debt gets Euclid's lemma, and B02's scope-box promise about dividing congruences gets its precise condition. Take a pencil; you will hunt near-misses between multiples before you read.
