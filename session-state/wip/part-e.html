Example 2 · An infinite element — Archimedes on trial

Moves used: P03 the special case promoted to a counterworld · P25 the axiom census that certifies the field · P07 the element nobody asked for.

Pin the trial — P01 again, then Box P35.A's first obligation. The language is the language of ordered fields: \\(+\\), \\(\\times\\), \\(0\\), \\(1\\), \\(<\\). The axioms \\(\\mathcal{A}\\): the field axioms, plus the order axioms, plus the two compatibility axioms — sums and products of positive elements are positive. The statement \\(S\\): Archimedes' principle — for every element \\(y\\) there is a natural number \\(n\\) with \\(n \\cdot 1 > y\\); no element outruns counting itself. Every schoolbook field obeys it, which is exactly why it is worth putting on trial.

Build the counterworld \\(\\mathcal W_2\\) — an ordered field of rational functions. Its elements are fractions \\(\\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\\) of polynomials with real coefficients, \\(q \\neq 0\\); its operations are the school ones. The only design decision is the order, and it is the whole trick: \\(\\frac{p}{q} > 0\\) when, far out at \\(+\\infty\\), the fraction is eventually positive — formally, when the leading coefficients of \\(p\\) and \\(q\\) have the same sign. One rule decides everything; nothing else is touched.

Certify the world — the census, Box P35.A's second obligation, symmetrically applied. The machine built the pool of \\(32\\) elements: numerators of degree at most \\(1\\) with coefficients in \\(\\{-1, 0, 1\\}\\), over the four denominators \\((1)\\), \\((x)\\), \\((x-1)\\), \\((x+1)\\). Against that pool it ran every ordered-field axiom it could reach: \\(1{,}024\\) ordered pairs through four checks each, \\(32{,}768\\) ordered triples through four checks each — associativity and commutativity of both operations, distributivity, order-compatibility — and \\(1{,}024\\) positive-product checks. Zero failures. And \\(\\mathcal W_1\\) — the ordinary field \\(\\mathbb{Q}\\), say — passes the same census with the same machine: for \\(t = \\frac{1}{n}\\), the smallest \\(k\\) with \\(kt > 1\\) is exactly \\(n + 1\\) (checked \\(n = 1 \\ldots 12\\), witnesses \\(k = 2 \\ldots 13\\)) — counting always catches up, there.

The disagreement, at \\(S\\) itself. In \\(\\mathcal W_2\\), the element \\(x\\) outruns every natural number: \\(x > n\\) for \\(n = 1, \\ldots, 50\\), machine-checked, and for every \\(n\\) at once, since \\(x - n\\) has leading coefficient \\(1 > 0\\). Its reciprocal \\(\\varepsilon = \\frac{1}{x}\\) is the old dream of infinitesimal analysis: \\(0 < \\varepsilon < \\frac{1}{n}\\) for every natural \\(n\\), and \\(n \\cdot \\varepsilon < 1\\) — positive, smaller than every schoolbook fraction, and never zero. The census collects the stranger receipts: \\(\\varepsilon^2 < \\varepsilon\\) with \\(0 < \\varepsilon^2\\); \\((1-\\varepsilon) \\cdot \\frac{x}{x-1} = 1\\) exactly; and the geometric-series identity \\(\\frac{1}{1-\\varepsilon} - (1 + \\varepsilon + \\varepsilon^2 + \\varepsilon^3 + \\varepsilon^4) = \\frac{\\varepsilon^5 x}{x-1}\\) — the sum stopping one term short of the limit, the residue computed exactly, a P69–B11 receipt in a world school never mentions. Soundness closes both directions: \\(\\mathbb{Q}\\) forbids proofs of \\(\\neg S\\), the field of fractions forbids proofs of \\(S\\), and Archimedes' principle is independent of the ordered-field axioms. Neither world is counterfeit; Exhibit B takes that seriously.

Example 3 · The disk — Euclid's postulate on trial

Moves used: P07 the disk as the auxiliary world Beltrami invented · P25 the angle and geodesic censuses · P01 pinning "parallel" before disagreeing about it.

The original trial, now with the machine watching. The axioms \\(\\mathcal{A}\\): neutral geometry — the finite slice of Euclid and Hilbert that never mentions parallels: points and lines, betweenness, congruence, the plane axioms. The statement \\(S\\), Playfair's form of the fifth postulate: through a point \\(P\\) not on a line \\(\\ell\\), exactly one line runs parallel to \\(\\ell\\). World \\(\\mathcal W_1\\) is the Euclidean plane, which obeys \\(\\mathcal{A}\\) and \\(S\\) both — the two-thousand-year default. World \\(\\mathcal W_2\\) is the Poincaré disk, and it is built, not found: points are the interior of the unit circle; lines are diameters and arcs of circles meeting the boundary at right angles; angles are measured as the Euclidean angles between arcs — the disk is conformal, which is why angle censuses are honest here. Beltrami's transfer owns the consistency risk: the disk is a region of the ordinary plane with curves in it, so if Euclidean geometry is consistent, the disk world exists — Exhibit C keeps that ledger.

Certify the disk — Box P35.A's first two obligations, disk edition. The machine runs the geodesic construction: the circle through two interior points \\(P, Q\\), orthogonal to the boundary, has center \\(C\\) solving \\(2P \\cdot C = |P|^2 + 1\\) and \\(2Q \\cdot C = |Q|^2 + 1\\) — two linear equations, one circle, always a solution inside the geometry. Then the angle census, on triangles \\(T(s)\\) with vertices \\((0,0)\\), \\((s,0)\\), \\((0,s)\\): \\(T(0.5)\\) — Fig P35.1's triangle — has angles \\(90.0000° + 30.9638° + 30.9638° = 151.9275°\\), defect \\(28.0725°\\); and the defect in radians is the area — \\(0.4900\\), the Gauss–Bonnet law at census scale, cited as a theorem, checked as a number. \\(T(0.9)\\): sum \\(101.9851°\\), defect \\(78.0149°\\), area \\(1.3616\\). And the local-flatness receipt, the one that says the disk obeys the local axioms honestly: \\(T(0.25)\\) sums to \\(172.8473°\\), \\(T(0.1)\\) to \\(178.8541°\\), \\(T(0.01)\\) to \\(179.9885°\\) — defect ratios against \\(T(0.5)\\) of \\(0.2548\\), \\(0.0408\\), \\(0.0004\\): halve the triangle, quarter the defect, the signature of a world that is Euclidean in the small and curved in the large, exactly as neutral geometry requires.

The parallel census — the disagreement, at \\(S\\). Fix \\(\\ell\\) as the horizontal diameter and \\(P = (0.2, 0.5)\\). Every geodesic through \\(P\\) is a circle whose center \\(C = (c_x, c_y)\\) lies on the line \\(0.4c_x + c_y = 1.29\\) — the orthogonality condition at \\(P\\), one equation. The machine sampled \\(39\\) centers across it and sorted every circle into two exact piles. \\(19\\) with \\(|c_x| < 1\\) escape — they bend away from \\(\\ell\\) and run to the ideal boundary without meeting it, two witnesses drawn in Fig P35.3. \\(18\\) with \\(|c_x| > 1\\) cross \\(\\ell\\) strictly inside the disk, at \\(x = c_x - \\mathrm{sgn}(c_x)\\sqrt{c_x^2 - 1} \\in (-1, 1)\\) — the sampled center \\(c_x = 1.5\\), for instance, lands its crossing at \\(x = 0.3820\\), the marked point of the figure. And the knife-edge is printed, not hidden: the two remaining circles, with \\(|c_x| = 1\\), graze \\(\\ell\\) exactly at its ideal endpoints \\(x = \\pm 1\\) — the asymptotic parallels, meeting at infinity and nowhere else, the precise boundary between the two piles. Not one circle did anything else. The dichotomy is exact, and Playfair's "exactly one" dies of abundance: through \\(P\\) there are not one but at least nineteen parallels — nineteen escapes, before even counting the two that die trying. The disk obeys \\(\\mathcal{A}\\) and refutes \\(S\\); the plane obeys \\(\\mathcal{A}\\) and asserts it; soundness closes, and the two-thousand-year hunt is over by construction — no proof of the fifth postulate from neutral geometry will ever exist.

Read the receipt against the history of §2. Lambert, refusing to publish, had found that the rival triangles fall short of 180° by an amount behaving like an area; the disk's census hands back the same number twice — defect \\(28.0725°\\), area \\(0.4900\\) — the suspicion now an equation. Bolyai's "strange new world out of nothing" here has coordinates, a construction rule, and a machine-checkable census. And the oldest lesson is printed in the figure: the disk's geodesics are curved because the axioms never said straight — Key 1 again, the blindness of the axioms, now drawn to scale.
