Example 2 · The triangle — a table built by counting

Moves used: P01 pinning group, set, action · P24 the wave ledger the table tabulates · P08 the average, exposed as the first row.

The cube's table was borrowed from \(S_4\). Build one from scratch instead, at the smallest size where it has any structure — the six motions of the equilateral triangle: the identity; three reflections (each through a vertex and the midpoint of the opposite edge); two rotations (\(\pm 120^\circ\)). Two elements are conjugate when one is another wearing a rotated costume — the reflections form one class of 3, the rotations one of 2, the identity its own — and the class census \(1 + 3 + 2 = 6\) is the machine's first receipt.

Let the triangle act on its three vertices. The character of this permutation action is the fixed-point count, read straight off the motions: the identity fixes all 3 vertices, a reflection fixes the vertex on its axis, a rotation fixes none:

\[\chi_{\mathrm{vertex}} = (3,\; 1,\; 0).\]

Now the whole character table, and notice how it is built, not decreed. The constant wave is always a character — \(\mathbf{1} = (1,1,1)\). The sign wave — record \(+1\) for the identity and rotations, \(-1\) for the flips — is \(\varepsilon = (1,-1,1)\): motions split into gentle and flipping, and the sign is that split as a number. For the third wave, subtract: the vertex action visibly contains the constant direction (the vector \(e_1 + e_2 + e_3\), fixed by everything), so remove it, \(\chi_{\mathrm{std}} = \chi_{\mathrm{vertex}} - \mathbf{1} = (2, 0, -1)\), and check the remainder is pure: \(\langle \chi_{\mathrm{std}}, \chi_{\mathrm{std}} \rangle = \frac{4 + 0 + 2 \cdot 1}{6} = 1\) — irreducible, the standard wave: the plane in which the triangle genuinely rotates. Three checks, all one-line inner products, all machine-run. The vertex action decomposes: \(\langle (3,1,0), \mathbf{1} \rangle = \frac{3+3+0}{6} = 1\), \(\langle (3,1,0), \varepsilon \rangle = \frac{3-3+0}{6} = 0\), \(\langle (3,1,0), \chi_{\mathrm{std}} \rangle = \frac{6+0+0}{6} = 1\) — so the vertex space is one copy of the constant wave plus one copy of the standard wave, \(3 = 1 + 2\), and the safety gate agrees: \(\langle \chi_{\mathrm{vertex}}, \chi_{\mathrm{vertex}} \rangle = \frac{9+3+0}{6} = 2 = 1^2 + 1^2\). Then count with it. Two-color the vertices and ask for orbits under the full six-motion group — the bracelet count, since flips are now allowed. The paint character is cycles-to-the-\(k\): the identity has 3 cycles on vertices (\(2^3 = 8\) fixed colorings), a reflection has 2 — its axis vertex stands alone, the other two trade — (\(2^2 = 4\)), a rotation has 1 (\(2^1 = 2\)):

\[\chi_{\mathrm{paint}} = (8,\,4,\,2), \qquad \langle \chi_{\mathrm{paint}}, \mathbf{1} \rangle = \frac{8 + 3 \cdot 4 + 2 \cdot 2}{6} = \frac{24}{6} = 4,\]

and the census agrees: the four binary triangles are the all-white, one-black, two-black, all-black — 4 orbits. But the table keeps going where the average stopped: the split of the 8-dimensional space of all vertex colorings into the triangle's waves:

\[\langle \chi_{\mathrm{paint}}, \mathbf{1} \rangle = 4, \quad \langle \chi_{\mathrm{paint}}, \varepsilon \rangle = \frac{8 - 12 + 4}{6} = 0, \quad \langle \chi_{\mathrm{paint}}, \chi_{\mathrm{std}} \rangle = \frac{16 + 0 - 4}{6} = 2,\]

so \(8 = 4 \cdot \mathbf{1} \oplus 2 \cdot \chi_{\mathrm{std}}\), with the gate \(\langle \chi_{\mathrm{paint}}, \chi_{\mathrm{paint}} \rangle = \frac{64 + 48 + 8}{6} = 20 = 4^2 + 2^2\). Read what the 4 is: four copies of the constant wave, one per orbit — the invariant colorings are exactly the functions constant on orbits, so the orbit count is the multiplicity of the constant wave. That is the pattern's quiet engine: counting up to symmetry is measuring how much of the constant wave an action contains, and every count in this book's family — §2's average included — is that measurement with different clothes on.
