Exercise 3 ●●●

The Fibonacci machine \(F = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\), end to end — the harvest run on irrational rails, reaping the most famous closed form in the book. The counts first: \(F(0) = 0\), \(F(1) = 1\), and each month adds the last two, \(F(n+2) = F(n+1) + F(n)\) — the rabbit counts of section 2's history note. (a) Full hunt: trace, determinant, characteristic equation, discriminant, both roots — and the Vieta glance. (b) The rails: show \((\varphi, 1)\) and \((\psi, 1)\) are the machine's directions, where \(\varphi = \tfrac{1 + \sqrt 5}{2}\) and \(\psi = \tfrac{1 - \sqrt 5}{2}\). (c) File the dictionary \(P\), health-check it — the check comes out irrational, and the page owes you a sentence on whether that is legal — and write the reverse dictionary. (d) Reap: prove \(F^n = \begin{bmatrix} F(n+1) & F(n) \\ F(n) & F(n-1) \end{bmatrix}\) by induction, harvest \(F^n = P D^n P^{-1}\), and read off the \((1,2)\) entry: Binet's formula. (e) Three routes to \(F(10) = 55\): the integer ladder, the Binet float, and an exact irrational-free route that squares the surds away.

Hint

(a) Box B07.A, two steps, new numbers; the discriminant is not a perfect square, and the hunt does not mind. (b) Feed the machine and use what \(\varphi\) and \(\psi\) satisfy — each is a root of the equation from (a). (c) Columns are the rails; the determinant is the health check, and B05's test asks only nonzero, never whole. (d) Prove the table shape by induction first — the recurrence does the climbing — then multiply \(P D^n P^{-1}\) and remember Vieta's product \(\varphi\psi = -1\) at the crucial cancellation. (e) Route 1 is ten feeds of \((1,0)\); route 2 is a calculator; route 3 writes \(\varphi^n\) as \(\tfrac{a_n + b_n\sqrt 5}{2}\) and climbs the pairs.

Worked answer

(a) \(\operatorname{tr} F = 1 + 0 = 1\), \(\det F = 0 - 1 = -1\), so the equation is \(\lambda^2 - \lambda - 1 = 0\). Discriminant \((\operatorname{tr})^2 - 4\det = 1 + 4 = 5\), not a square — the roots are irrational, \(\varphi = \tfrac{1 + \sqrt 5}{2} \approx 1.618\) and \(\psi = \tfrac{1 - \sqrt 5}{2} \approx -0.618\). Vieta's two readings hold exactly as B07's Claim 2 promises: \(\varphi + \psi = 1 = \operatorname{tr} F\) and \(\varphi\psi = \tfrac{1 - 5}{4} = -1 = \det F\) — the product reading is the cancellation (d) will spend. (b) Feed the machine: \(F(\varphi, 1) = (\varphi + 1,\; \varphi)\). But \(\varphi\) satisfies \(\lambda^2 - \lambda - 1 = 0\), that is, \(\varphi^2 = \varphi + 1\) — so \(F(\varphi, 1) = (\varphi^2, \varphi) = \varphi(\varphi, 1)\): a rail with factor \(\varphi\). Identically \(F(\psi, 1) = (\psi^2, \psi) = \psi(\psi, 1)\). Two distinct factors, so the rails are non-parallel (B07's Claim 3) and a scheme exists. (c) Columns the rails, golden first: \(P = \begin{bmatrix} \varphi & \psi \\ 1 & 1 \end{bmatrix}\), and \(\det P = \varphi - \psi = \sqrt 5 \ne 0\) — healthy. The health check coming out irrational is legal because the test only ever asked nonzero: B05's determinant certifies that the two directions are independent, and it prices areas, not arithmetic — the dictionary may carry surds, exactly as Elena's fractional readings carried fractions. The reverse dictionary, adjugate over determinant (Box B06.A): \(P^{-1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt 5}\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -\psi \\ -1 & \varphi \end{bmatrix}\). (d) The table shape, by induction. At \(n = 1\): \(F^1 = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} F(2) & F(1) \\ F(1) & F(0) \end{bmatrix}\). If \(F^n = \begin{bmatrix} F(n+1) & F(n) \\ F(n) & F(n-1) \end{bmatrix}\), then

\[F^{n+1} \;=\; F^n F \;=\; \begin{bmatrix} F(n+1) + F(n) & F(n+1) \\ F(n) + F(n-1) & F(n) \end{bmatrix} \;=\; \begin{bmatrix} F(n+2) & F(n+1) \\ F(n+1) & F(n) \end{bmatrix},\]

the recurrence \(F(n+2) = F(n+1) + F(n)\) doing the climbing. So the \((1,2)\) entry of \(F^n\) is \(F(n)\) for every whole \(n\) — the school route. The harvest. Claim 2 gives \(F^n = P D^n P^{-1}\) with \(D = \begin{bmatrix} \varphi & 0 \\ 0 & \psi \end{bmatrix}\). The first row of \(P D^n\) is \((\varphi^{n+1},\; \psi^{n+1})\); the second column of \(P^{-1}\) is \(\frac{1}{\sqrt 5}\begin{bmatrix} -\psi \\ \varphi \end{bmatrix}\). So the \((1,2)\) entry is

\[\frac{-\varphi^{n+1}\psi \;+\; \psi^{n+1}\varphi}{\sqrt 5} \;=\; \frac{\varphi^n \, (-\varphi\psi) \;+\; \psi^n \, (\varphi\psi)}{\sqrt 5} \;=\; \frac{\varphi^n - \psi^n}{\sqrt 5},\]

because \(\varphi\psi = -1\): the \(-\varphi\psi\) turns the first term into \(+\varphi^n\) and the \(\varphi\psi\) turns the second into \(-\psi^n\). Equate the two routes and Vieta's product reading has bought a theorem:

\[F(n) \;=\; \frac{\varphi^n - \psi^n}{\sqrt 5} \qquad \text{for every whole } n \text{ at once}.\]

Binet's formula — the closed form the whole page was built to reap, and it is exact, not a float: the surds cancel to whole numbers for every \(n\), because the two routes computed the same entry of the same table. (e) Route 1, the ladder. Ten feeds of \((1,0)\): \((1,0) \to (1,1) \to (2,1) \to (3,2) \to (5,3) \to (8,5) \to (13,8) \to (21,13) \to (34,21) \to (55,34) \to (89,55)\). The tenth feed lands on the first column of \(F^{10}\), which by the table shape is \((F(11), F(10)) = (89, 55)\): \(F(10) = 55\), school arithmetic, no surd in sight. Route 2, the Binet float. \((\varphi^{10} - \psi^{10})/\sqrt 5\) on a calculator: \(55.000000000000014\) — fourteen right answers and then float noise; the formula is exact and the float is merely the float. Route 3, exact pairs. Write \(\varphi^n = \tfrac{a_n + b_n\sqrt 5}{2}\): \(\varphi^1 = \tfrac{1 + \sqrt 5}{2}\), and multiplying by \(\varphi\) again gives \(\varphi^{n+1} = \tfrac{(a_n + 5b_n) + (a_n + b_n)\sqrt 5}{4}\), so the pairs climb by \((a, b) \to \left(\tfrac{a + 5b}{2},\; \tfrac{a + b}{2}\right)\): \((1,1) \to (3,1) \to (4,2) \to (7,3) \to (11,5) \to (18,8) \to (29,13) \to (47,21) \to (76,34) \to (123,55)\). So \(\varphi^{10} = \tfrac{123 + 55\sqrt 5}{2}\), and \(\psi\) is the same expression with \(\sqrt 5\) negated — the conjugate — so \(\psi^{10} = \tfrac{123 - 55\sqrt 5}{2}\). The difference is \(55\sqrt 5\), and Binet divides by \(\sqrt 5\): exactly \(55\), with every surd gone. Three routes, one answer — and a closing glance the tilt taught you to take: \(\psi \approx -0.618\) has size below \(1\), so \(\psi^n\) dies out and \(F(n)\) grows like \(\varphi^n/\sqrt 5\) — the counts are the golden rail's shadow, the red rail's tilt worn as a number sequence.

Open exploration ◐

The growth detector, island edition. The harbourmaster pins B07's census grid to the desk — the 48 nonzero arrows with entries from \(-3\) to \(3\) — and asks a question with a brute-force answer and a closed-form proof. (a) Feed every arrow to \(M\) ten times over: which arrows are still inside the grid at the end? Brute-force it if you like — 48 arrows, ten feeds, honest bookkeeping — then prove the answer from Claim 4, in terms of red content. (b) What does the detector detect: say precisely what "stays in the window" measures about an arrow. (c) The twist: double the machine to \(2M = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 2 \\ 4 & 4 \end{bmatrix}\), hunt it in one line, and show that now nothing survives — every one of the 48 arrows escapes within a single feed.

Hint

(a) An arrow's red content is the second coordinate of its reading, \(\tfrac{2a + b}{3}\) — a fraction whose denominator is fixed, so a nonzero red content has a size it cannot shrink below. Claim 4 says the red piece multiplies by 4 every feed. (b) Compare the two rails' factors. (c) In-grid means both coordinates answer to \(-3\) to \(3\); \(4(a + b)\) and \(2(3a + b)\) are even numbers in that window — count what can fit.

Worked answer

(a) Exactly two arrows survive: the blue pair \(\pm(1,-2)\). Proof. Read an arrow \((a,b)\) on the rails: \(P^{-1}(a,b) = \left(\tfrac{a - b}{3},\; \tfrac{2a + b}{3}\right)\) — blue content \(\tfrac{a-b}{3}\), red content \(\tfrac{2a+b}{3}\). Claim 4 says the orbit is \(\text{blue} \cdot (1,-2) + 4^n \cdot \text{red} \cdot (1,1)\). If the red content is nonzero, then \(2a + b\) is a nonzero whole number, so \(|\text{red}| \ge \tfrac{1}{3}\); and on the grid \(|\text{blue}| = \tfrac{|a-b|}{3} \le 2\). The first coordinate after \(n\) feeds is \(\text{blue} + 4^n\,\text{red}\), so its size is at least \(4^n \cdot \tfrac{1}{3} - 2\). Staying in the window demands \(|x_n| \le 3\), so \(4^n \le 15\): true at \(n = 0, 1\) and false from \(n = 2\) on — every arrow with red content has left the grid by its second feed at the latest, and ten feeds is far past that. If the red content is zero then \(b = -2a\): the arrow sits on the blue rail, and the grid holds exactly \(\pm(1,-2)\) of those. The blue factor is \(1\), so \(M\) returns them unchanged, this feed and every feed. Survivors: exactly \(\pm(1,-2)\) — B07's two fixed arrows, now detected by growth instead of counted by hand. (b) The detector detects red content. "Stays in the window for ten feeds" and "red content zero" name the same arrows — bounded inside the grid exactly on the frozen rail — and the reason is the pair of factors: one factor equal to \(1\) keeps its content; any other factor, even \(4\), marches its content through every window in a couple of feeds. The detector is a growth meter, and it reads off the machine nothing but the stretch factors. (c) The hunt: \(\operatorname{tr}(2M) = 10\), \(\det(2M) = 16\), so \(\lambda^2 - 10\lambda + 16 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda - 8)\) — the same two rails (doubling a machine doubles every answer, so every factor doubles: \(1 \to 2\), \(4 \to 8\)), and now both factors are off \(1\): the detector predicts an empty grid, survivors or not, blue rail included. And the prediction holds faster than promised — one feed suffices. \(2M(a,b) = (6a + 2b,\; 4a + 4b)\); to stay in the window the second coordinate must satisfy \(|4(a+b)| \le 3\), and \(a + b\) is a whole number, so \(a + b = 0\). Then the first coordinate is \(6a + 2b = 4a\), and \(|4a| \le 3\) forces \(a = 0\), so \(b = 0\) as well: the only arrow whose feed stays in the grid is the zero arrow the census never counted. All 48 escape in one feed — the blue pair first of all, \(2M(1,-2) = (2,-4)\), one bound out of the window. A machine with every factor off \(1\) empties any bounded grid, and how fast it empties is the factors' own sizes — the growth detector, reading the stretch table off nothing but survival.
