The Patterns Atlas · P51 · the nineteenth move of tier 5

The Koszul dual swap

A quadratic algebra and its dual answer for one another: each side's hard questions about resolutions become the other side's multiplication table. The swap is not a metaphor — it is a construction with a certificate, and this page runs the certificate by machine.

New word: quadratic algebra. The letters of a vector space \(V\) make words; concatenating them is the tensor algebra \(T(V)\), graded by word length — \(T(V)_n\) is the span of the \(n\)-letter words. Impose relations that are combinations of two-letter words, \(R \subseteq V \otimes V\), and the quotient \(A = T(V)/(R)\) is a quadratic algebra. The polynomial room is the archetype: impose \(xy - yx\) and every word collapses to \(x^a y^b\). The floors multiply upward, \(A_m \cdot A_k \subseteq A_{m+k}\), so each algebra is a ladder of finite-dimensional rooms.
New word: orthogonal complement. Dual letters score letters: if \(\xi, \eta\) are dual to \(x, y\), the value \(\langle \xi \otimes \eta, x \otimes y \rangle = \xi(x)\eta(y)\) extends entry by entry to a pairing between \(V^{\ast} \otimes V^{\ast}\) and \(V \otimes V\). The dual tensors scoring zero on every relation form the orthogonal complement \(R^{\perp}\). The ledger is honest before any counting starts: \(\dim R + \dim R^{\perp} = (\dim V)^2\).
New word: Hilbert series. An algebra's growth ledger is its Hilbert series \(H_A(t) = \sum_n \dim(A_n)\, t^n\) — one coefficient per floor. The polynomial room's is \(1/(1-t)^2\), floor \(n\) holding the \(n+1\) words \(x^a y^b\); the exterior room's is \((1+t)^2\), because its floors stop at \(n = 2\). This page makes the census pass arithmetic: the two ledgers of a swapped pair will multiply to exactly \(1\), coefficient by coefficient, or the pairing has failed.

1 · Before we begin

This page is where those habits meet an unexpected customer: not a group action but an algebra presentation, and not a character census but a growth census — the Hilbert series — policed by the same bookkeeping instinct.

The reader arriving from P50 has already met the pairing style: two structures commuting inside one construction, each answering for the other, with the bookkeeping certified by machine. Here the two structures are algebras, the one construction is a differential built from a single element that lives in both at once, and the answers are literal: the dual algebra is the answer book to the first algebra's extension questions — a claim this page will make precise and then verify coefficient by coefficient.

2 · The encounter

Two rooms, both old friends. The polynomial room \(k[x,y]\): commutative, infinite, one more word per floor — dimensions \(1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\). The exterior room \(\wedge(V^{\ast})\): letters \(\xi, \eta\) with \(\xi^2 = \eta^2 = \xi\eta + \eta\xi = 0\), silent above floor two — dimensions \(1, 2, 1, 0, \ldots\). Nothing about the exterior room suggests it is holding the polynomial room's answer key. It is.

Here is the swap machine. Write the polynomial room as a quadratic algebra: \(A = T(V)/(R)\) with \(V = \mathrm{span}\{x, y\}\) and \(R = \mathrm{span}\{x \otimes y - y \otimes x\}\) — commutativity, said in two-letter words. The dual letters score the relation tensor: \(\langle \xi \otimes \eta, x \otimes y - y \otimes x \rangle = 1\), \(\langle \eta \otimes \xi, \cdot \rangle = -1\), and the diagonal tensors score \(0\). The dual tensors scoring zero on all of \(R\) are \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi \otimes \xi,\; \xi \otimes \eta + \eta \otimes \xi,\; \eta \otimes \eta\}\) — three relations, matching the ledger \(\dim R^{\perp} = (\dim V)^2 - \dim R = 3\). Impose them on the dual letters and the exterior room appears: \(A^{!} = T(V^{\ast})/(R^{\perp}) = \wedge(V^{\ast})\). One algebra's relations, run through the pairing, are the other algebra.

Now the single element that couples the two rooms: the Casimir \(c = x \otimes \xi + y \otimes \eta\), living in \(A \otimes A^{!}\). Square it, term by term:

\[ c^2 \; = \; x^2 \otimes \xi^2 \; + \; xy \otimes \xi\eta \; + \; yx \otimes \eta\xi \; + \; y^2 \otimes \eta^2 . \]

The end terms die first: \(\xi^2 = \eta^2 = 0\). The middle terms trade through each algebra's own relation — \(yx = xy\) in \(A\), \(\eta\xi = -\xi\eta\) in \(A^{!}\) — and cancel: \(xy \otimes \xi\eta + xy \otimes (-\xi\eta) = 0\). So \(c^2 = 0\): a two-line certificate that the pairing itself is doing the bookkeeping. The machine runs this certificate, for this pair and for a one-parameter family, with the arithmetic in integers.

And \(c^2 = 0\) is exactly what a differential is: left multiplication by \(c\) on the room of blocks \(A_m \otimes (A^{!})^{\ast}_k\) — the first algebra's floors against the second's — squares to zero, because each step applies \(c\) twice. The resulting complex is the Koszul complex, a staircase in which every diagonal \(m + k = N\) is a finite room. Two claims will decide everything:

  • The staircase resolves the trivial module. Over the polynomial room, the complex built from the exterior room's floors is exact — this is the oldest result in the subject: Hilbert counted these resolutions' syzygies in 1890, decades before anyone said Koszul. Exactness means the staircase is a free resolution of the ground field \(k\), the smallest module there is.
  • The dual is the answer book. When the staircase is exact, the extensions of the trivial module over \(A\) multiply exactly like \(A^{!}\): \(\mathrm{Ext}^{\ast}_A(k, k) \cong A^{!}\). Questions that stall inside \(A\) — what extends what, which maps close, what the resolution's next floor holds — become multiplication tables in the swap's mirror. Questions become answers, literally.

3 · The pattern

Pattern P51 — The Koszul dual swap. \[ A = T(V)/(R) \quad \longleftrightarrow \quad A^{!} = T(V^{\ast})/(R^{\perp}), \qquad c = \sum_i x_i \otimes \xi_i \in A \otimes A^{!}, \qquad c^2 = 0, \] and \(A\) is Koszul when the Koszul complex — the staircase of blocks \(A_m \otimes (A^{!})^{\ast}_k\) with differential \(d =\) left multiplication by \(c\) — is exact, resolving the trivial module \(k\). Then two receipts follow: the series gate \(H_A(t) \, H_{A^{!}}(-t) = 1\), and the answer book \(\mathrm{Ext}^{\ast}_A(k, k) \cong A^{!}\). Every clause is load-bearing: the dual is computed from the pairing, never guessed; \(c^2 = 0\) is a theorem of the pairing, checked in two lines; exactness is the definition of Koszul — the two receipts are consequences and gates, not the theorem itself.

Or, in words: impose relations, then hand the relations to the pairing machine — the orthogonal complement, imposed on dual letters, is a second algebra. One element built from the letters of both squares to zero, and its staircase — first algebra's floors against second algebra's floors — resolves the smallest module exactly when the second algebra is the first's answer book: every extension question over one side is a multiplication over the other. Two structures, one differential, each the other's resolution.

Box P51.A — the three obligations. Before any exactness question, three cheap checks police the swap, and each is a different kind of arithmetic. First, the pairing ledger: \(\dim R^{\perp} = (\dim V)^2 - \dim R\), checked the moment the dual's relations are written — a dual with the wrong number of relations was copied, not computed. Second, the Casimir gate: \(c^2 = 0\) in \(A \otimes A^{!}\), a finite computation on floor two, free of charge — if it fails, nothing downstream can be trusted. Third, the series gate: \(H_A(t) \, H_{A^{!}}(-t) = 1\), coefficient by coefficient — pure integer arithmetic on the two growth ledgers, no linear algebra at all, and it catches a wrong census instantly. Only then does exactness — the real theorem, linear algebra on the staircase — earn its run.

The staircase's bookkeeping deserves one more look, because it is where P36's promise gets kept. The complex is bigraded: block \((m, k)\) multiplies like the tensor product of the two floors, and the differential preserves each diagonal \(N = m + k\). On the shaded diagonal of the figure, the machine certifies exactness as rank arithmetic: a rank-\(1\) map into the \(4\)-dimensional block, a rank-\(3\) map out of it, homology \(4 - 1 - 3 = 0\). This is the character census one shelf up: the inner products became dimensions, the waves became floors, and the certificate is now a rank count instead of a trace count — graded traces, each wave keeping its amplitude book, exactly as the parent page said.

Diagnosis: the certified swap. Claim type: a structure theorem — an exact complex and an isomorphism of graded algebras, no error bars anywhere; its gates are integer arithmetic (pairing ledgers, series coefficients) and its certificate is linear algebra (ranks on the staircase). Problem shape: a quadratic presentation plus a question about resolutions or extensions. Tools at hand: build the dual from the pairing, run the three cheap gates, then rank arithmetic on the staircase — the question usually dissolves into multiplication in the mirror.

How you could have found it: the cheap gate comes first. Ask which two growth ledgers can multiply to \(1\), and the pairing forces the dual's relations on you before you know the algebra's name — \(R^{\perp}\) is computed, never guessed. Ledger, Casimir, series: three checks cheap enough to run on any quadratic presentation, and only when they pass does the staircase earn its linear algebra. That is the discovery order of every exemplar below — pairing, gates, staircase — and the order the lineage tells: count first, complex second, name last.

4 · Three exemplars

Exemplar 1 · The polynomial room and its exterior mirror: the whole certificate

Run every obligation of Box P51.A on the pair of §2, with nothing taken on faith. The pairing ledger: one relation in, three out, \(1 + 3 = (\dim V)^2 = 4\). The census: polynomial floors \(1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\), exterior floors \(1, 2, 1, 0, \ldots\), counted word by word. The Casimir gate: \(c^2 = 0\), the two-line cancellation of the encounter, verified by machine. The series gate: \(\frac{1}{(1-t)^2} \cdot (1-t)^2 = 1\), coefficient by coefficient — the machine multiplies the ledgers to order twelve and reads off zeros everywhere but the constant term. The exactness: on the staircase's \(N = 2\) diagonal, blocks \(3, 4, 1\) with ranks \(1\) and \(3\) — homology zero — and the same rank arithmetic runs one diagonal further, \(N = 3\): blocks \(4, 6, 2\), ranks \(2\) and \(4\), homology \(6 - 2 - 4 = 0\) again. What the certificate buys is the oldest answer in the subject: the resolution of \(k\) over the polynomial room is \(\;0 \to A(-2) \to A(-1)^{\oplus 2} \to A \to k \to 0\), Hilbert's syzygy ladder, and the exterior room's floors are its scaffolding. The mirror holds the answer key, and the arithmetic says so.

Exemplar 2 · The quantum plane, priced both ways

Deform the relation: \(A_q = k\langle x, y\rangle/(xy - q\,yx)\) with \(q \neq 0\) a parameter — the machine runs \(q = 2\) in integers, and the page's claim is the general \(q\). The census: every word sorts to \(x^a y^b\) (bubble \(x\)'s leftward, picking up \(q\)'s), so floors again run \(1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\) and \(H_{A_q}(t) = 1/(1-t)^2\). The pairing machine: a dual tensor \(\alpha \xi \otimes \eta + \beta \eta \otimes \xi\) scores \(\alpha - q\beta\) on the relation, so \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi \otimes \xi,\; \eta \otimes \eta,\; q\,\xi\eta + \eta\xi\}\) — three relations again, ledger intact. The dual \(A^{!}_q = k\langle \xi, \eta \rangle/(\xi^2, \eta^2, q\,\xi\eta + \eta\xi)\) has floors \(1, 2, 1, 0, \ldots\): the single word \(\xi\eta\) survives floor two. The Casimir: \(c^2 = xy \otimes \xi\eta + yx \otimes \eta\xi\); trade \(xy = q\,yx\) in \(A_q\) and \(\eta\xi = -q\,\xi\eta\) in \(A^{!}_q\), and the terms cancel for every \(q \neq 0\) — the pairing's cancellation is \(q\)-blind. The series gate holds, \(\frac{1}{(1-t)^2}(1-t)^2 = 1\), and \(A_q\) is Koszul for every \(q\) — the machine's integer certificate at \(q = 2\) is \(2\,c^2 = 2\,xy \otimes \xi\eta + 2\,yx \otimes \eta\xi = 4\,yx \otimes \xi\eta - 4\,yx \otimes \xi\eta = 0\), no fractions anywhere. The mirror's shape interpolates: \(q = 1\) is the encounter's pair (symmetric against exterior), and as \(q\) wanders the two rooms keep their ledgers while the multiplication bends — the swap survives the deformation.

Exemplar 3 · The universal envelope and its cochains: the showcase, honestly a sketch

The largest room the swap has ever furnished: for a Lie algebra \(\mathfrak{g}\), the universal envelope \(U(\mathfrak{g})\) — the associative room where \(\mathfrak{g}\)'s bracket becomes a commutator. The Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem says the envelope's graded shadow is the symmetric room, so the quadratic bookkeeping of this page applies to it. Its Koszul dual is the Chevalley–Eilenberg complex: the exterior room on \(\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\) with a differential built from the bracket, and the classical swap states the answer book exactly — \(\mathrm{Ext}^{\ast}_{U(\mathfrak{g})}(k, k) \cong H^{\ast}(\mathfrak{g})\), the cohomology of the cochains. Lie algebra cohomology, the workhorse of P33's bridge, is this page's swap run on the envelope. For \(\mathfrak{g}\) abelian the bracket is zero, the differential vanishes, and the swap degenerates to Exemplar 1's mirror; for \(\mathfrak{g}\) semisimple, the cochain room is finite-dimensional and Whitehead's vanishing prices it — the showcase is honest sketch territory, and this page does not pretend to verify it: the certificate here is the literature's, not the machine's.

5 · Three exhibits

Exhibit A · Cubic blindness: the swap reads only floor two

Take \(B = k[x,y]/(x^3)\) — commutative, one cubic relation. Its quadratic data is exactly the polynomial room's: commutativity lives on floor two, and \(x^3\) lives on floor three, where the pairing machine never looks. Run the exterior mirror's gate against \(B\)'s actual ledger — floors \(1, 2, 3, 3, 3, \ldots\) (words \(x^a y^b\) with \(a \leq 2\)) — and the arithmetic refuses: \(H_B(t) \, H_{\wedge}(-t) = 1 - t^3\), the failure coefficient by coefficient, and the machine reads the cubing relation's fingerprint off the \(-t^3\). The gate did not merely fail; it named the invisible relation. Mitigation: a nonquadratic algebra must first be replaced by its quadratic shadow, and the shadow's honesty is a separate question — here the shadow kept the rooms commutative and lost the cube. Price: relations above floor two are invisible to the swap, and shadow failures surface as series defects. Default: before quoting a dual, name the floor the presentation lives on.

Exhibit B · The false conviction: a memory that convicts

Take \(A = k\langle x, y\rangle/(xy, yx)\) — monomial, letters never mixing: floors \(1, 2, 2, 2, \ldots\) (the words \(x^n\) and \(y^n\)). The pairing machine gives \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi \otimes \xi, \eta \otimes \eta\}\), so the dual is \(A^{!} = k\langle \xi, \eta\rangle/(\xi^2, \eta^2)\) with floors \(1, 2, 2, 2, \ldots\) (alternating words) — and the series gate passes: \(\frac{1+t}{1-t} \cdot \frac{1-t}{1+t} = 1\), coefficient by coefficient, machine-checked. Here is the trap. A plausible memory convicts \(A\): the two-letter paths \(x \to y\) and \(y \to x\) are both forbidden, and they chain — \(x \to y \to x\) — into the composite hop \(x \to x\), which is alive (\(x^2 \neq 0\)); a half-remembered "transitivity criterion" pronounces the forbidden-pair family dishonest, not Koszul, homology on the staircase. The machine was built to certify that conviction, and it acquitted instead: the staircase is exact on every diagonal through \(N = 6\), \(d^2 = 0\) everywhere — no homology at all. Ring theory agrees: \(yx - xy\) lies in the ideal (both generators are in it), so \(A \cong k[x,y]/(xy)\), a quadric hypersurface, and quadratic hypersurfaces are Koszul by a theorem of Tate. And the acquittal was never in doubt: every quadratic monomial algebra is Koszul (Backelin's criterion; the census ran all sixteen two-letter monomials through gate and staircase alike, and every one was clean) — a monomial sinner for this exhibit never existed. Mitigation: a recalled criterion is a memory, not a certificate; the machine recomputes what memory misquotes. Price: the genuinely unforgivable algebras — gate passing to every finite order, homology alive underneath — do exist, but they live outside the monomial class, in Exhibit C's territory. Default: when a pattern convicts, run the machine before publishing the verdict.

Exhibit C · Necessary, not sufficient: the Roos gate

Exhibit B's gate passed, and its conviction dissolved; the genuinely dangerous phenomenon lives here. Lech's example convicts by arithmetic alone: a commutative quadratic algebra with an innocent, finite ledger — four variables, five quadrics, floors \(1, 4, 5, 0\), nothing negative in sight — yet the gate forces the would-be dual's series to \(1/(1 - 4t + 5t^2)\), which turns negative at degree 6 (\(-29\)); no dual's floors can be negative, so the algebra is not Koszul, convicted before any staircase was built — the first known non-Koszul quadratic algebra, and the gate's sharpest catch. Roos (1993) built commutative algebras whose resolutions stay linear to arbitrarily high order — every finite staircase check passes — yet which are not Koszul; his counterexamples price the series gate's insufficiency, and Positselskii (1995) proved the duals' Hilbert-series relation implies nothing. The series is genuinely necessary and genuinely insufficient, and no amount of coefficient checking upgrades it to a certificate. Mitigation: in specific classes the facts harden into theorems: every quadratic monomial algebra is Koszul (Backelin's criterion — Exhibit B could never have been a sinner), and PBW-type algebras (Priddy, 1970) are Koszul by construction. Price: outside those classes, Koszulity is a property to be proved, not tested — and proofs are scarce on the ground. Default: quote a dual as an answer book only with a certificate in hand: exactness computed, or membership in a certified class, or the literature's theorem — named, never implied.

6 · The doors this opens

The operad door. The swap's largest generalization trades whole species of algebraic structure: operads. The commutative operad's Koszul dual is the Lie operad — commutativity's mirror is the bracket — and the associative operad is its own dual, up to a degree shift. This is Ginzburg–Kapranov's extension of the machine (1994), and the page P63 · Operad algebra carries the door forward from here. Mitigation: operadic duals carry grading shifts that scalar duals do not; the ledger arithmetic of Box P51.A does not transplant unchanged. Price: beyond a handful of classical operads, Koszulity must be certified family by family. Default: name the structure's category before swapping it.

The geometry door. Beilinson–Ginzburg–Soergel (1996) ran the swap on entire categories of sheaves: on a flag variety, perverse sheaves and their graded mirrors exchange roles the way generators and relations do here, and the answer book becomes a graded algebra's module category. The road runs through P33's bridge, whose resolutions are the staircase's geometric descendants. Mitigation: the geometric swap is a statement of categories, with purity replacing exactness as the certificate. Price: the theorems live over algebraically closed fields and singular varieties complicate the ledger. Default: in geometry, quote the swap as category duality, never as a Hilbert-series identity.

The physics door. Gauge theories run this swap under another name: BRST cohomology pairs a gauge symmetry's algebra with a ghost algebra, the Casimir-style differential is the BRST operator, and the physical states are its homology — the questions (which configurations are physical) become answers (cohomology classes) by exactly this page's mechanism. The mathematics underneath is Exemplar 3's: the envelope–cochain swap with the gauge algebra in the driver's seat. Mitigation: physics normalizations (ghost number conventions, field-antifield statistics) sit on top of the split, not inside it. Price: the gauge-fixed room is a derived room; quoting its naive homology underquotes the physics. Default: count the cohomology floors first, decorate with conventions after.

7 · Lineage

The staircase was counted before it was named. Hilbert's syzygy theorem (1890) proves the polynomial room's resolutions are free, with floors matching the exterior room's — the first certificate, computed by hand in Königsberg. Koszul (1950) built the complex that carries his name, for the symmetric/exterior pairing and for the Lie cohomology of Exemplar 3, in a paper whose title says Lie algebras and whose substance is this page's machinery. Priddy (1970) isolated the property — algebras whose staircase is exact he called Koszul — and proved the PBW class Koszul, giving Exhibit C its first certified class. Backelin (1981) gave the criterion under which every quadratic monomial algebra certifies Koszul — Exhibit B's acquittal rests on it — Roos (1993) priced the series gate's insufficiency with counterexamples whose resolutions stay linear to arbitrarily high order, and Positselskii (1995) proved the duals' passing series implies nothing. The swap scaled up twice at the century's end: Ginzburg–Kapranov (1994) traded operads, and Beilinson–Ginzburg–Soergel (1996) carried the pattern into representation theory, where it now organizes territory from flag varieties to the physics door's BRST bookkeeping.

Exercise 1 · The smallest honest swap ●○○
One letter: \(V = k \cdot x\), so \(R = 0\) and \(A = k[x]\). (a) Compute \(A^{!}\) from the pairing — the ledger says \(\dim R^{\perp} = 1\). (b) Square the Casimir \(c = x \otimes \xi\). (c) Run the series gate. (d) Write the staircase's resolution of \(k\) and check exactness by eye.

Hint

(a) With no relations, every dual tensor scores zero on \(R\). (d) Two rooms and one map — what does multiplication by \(x\) do to constants?

Worked answer

(a) \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi \otimes \xi\}\) — the whole of \(V^{\ast} \otimes V^{\ast}\), one relation, ledger intact — so \(A^{!} = k[\xi]/(\xi^2)\): floors \(1, 1, 0, \ldots\). (b) \(c^2 = x^2 \otimes \xi^2 = 0\) because \(\xi^2 = 0\). (c) \(H_A = \frac{1}{1-t}\), \(H_{A^{!}} = 1 + t\), and \(\frac{1}{1-t}(1 - t) = 1\) ✓. (d) \(0 \to A(-1) \xrightarrow{\,\cdot x\,} A \to k \to 0\): multiplication by \(x\) is injective on polynomials and surjective onto the positive floors, so the complex is exact — the tiniest staircase, resolved in one line.

Exercise 2 · The monomial swap, priced ●●○
Let \(A = k\langle x, y\rangle/(xy)\). (a) Count the floors: which words survive, what are the dimensions? (b) Run the pairing machine: compute \(R^{\perp}\) and \(A^{!}\), and count its floors. (c) Verify the series gate. (d) Why is this algebra Koszul — which obligation of Box P51.A does the exactness certificate reduce to, and why is it vacuous here?

Hint

(a) Once a \(y\) appears, no \(x\) may follow. (b) The dual forbids everything except \(\xi \otimes \eta\). (d) Which two-letter paths are forbidden, and can they ever chain? Why was Koszulity never in doubt for this algebra?

Worked answer

(a) Surviving words are \(y^a x^b\): floors \(1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\), so \(H_A = \frac{1}{(1-t)^2}\). (b) \(R = \mathrm{span}\{x \otimes y\}\); a dual tensor \(\alpha \xi\xi + \beta \xi\eta + \gamma \eta\xi + \delta \eta\eta\) scores \(\beta\), so \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi\xi, \eta\xi, \eta\eta\}\) and \(A^{!} = k\langle \xi, \eta\rangle/(\xi^2, \eta\xi, \eta^2)\): floors \(1, 2, 1, 0, \ldots\) (the words \(1\), \(\xi\), \(\eta\), \(\xi\eta\)). (c) \(\frac{1}{(1-t)^2}(1-t)^2 = 1\), coefficient by coefficient ✓. (d) Only \(x \to y\) is forbidden; a chain would need another forbidden pair to follow it, but no word starting with \(y\) is forbidden, so the chaining pattern never appears. The staircase is exact — and that is no accident: every quadratic monomial algebra is Koszul (Backelin's criterion), so the gate's success was guaranteed by class membership; the honest work was checking it anyway.

Exercise 3 · The quantum plane's mirror ●●●
Let \(A_q = k\langle x, y \rangle/(xy - q\,yx)\) with \(q \neq 0\). (a) Compute \(R^{\perp}\) and \(A^{!}_q\) from the pairing. (b) Prove \(c^2 = 0\) for every \(q\), in two lines. (c) Count both floors and run the series gate. (d) What happens at \(q = 1\) and at \(q = -1\) — which shadows do the two rooms become?

Hint

(a) Score \(\alpha \xi \otimes \eta + \beta \eta \otimes \xi\) against the relation. (b) Each algebra trades one product for the other. (d) At \(q = -1\), what does \(xy = -yx\) say about the letters themselves?

Worked answer

(a) The tensor scores \(\alpha - q\beta\), so \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi\xi,\; \eta\eta,\; q\,\xi\eta + \eta\xi\}\) and \(A^{!}_q = k\langle \xi, \eta \rangle/(\xi^2, \eta^2, q\,\xi\eta + \eta\xi)\). (b) \(c^2 = xy \otimes \xi\eta + yx \otimes \eta\xi\); trading \(xy = q\,yx\) and \(\eta\xi = -q\,\xi\eta\) gives \(q\,yx \otimes \xi\eta - q\,yx \otimes \xi\eta = 0\) — \(q\)-blind. (c) Both rooms sort to \(x^a y^b\) and \(\xi\eta\)-type words: floors \(1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\) and \(1, 2, 1, 0, \ldots\); the gate reads \(\frac{1}{(1-t)^2}(1-t)^2 = 1\) ✓. (d) \(q = 1\): the encounter's symmetric/exterior mirror. \(q = -1\): the letters anticommute, \(xy = -yx\) — a Clifford-type shadow — and the dual's floors stay \(1, 2, 1\): the swap survives, only the multiplication bends.

Exercise 4 · The false conviction, priced ◆
Let \(A = k\langle x, y \rangle/(xy, yx)\). (a) Count the floors of \(A\). (b) Compute \(A^{!}\) and its floors. (c) Run the series gate — what does it say? (d) Now the trap: which two-letter paths are forbidden, what composite path do they chain, and what would a half-remembered criterion conclude? Run the staircase — what does the machine find, and why does the isomorphism \(A \cong k[x,y]/(xy)\) settle the question? What does the false conviction teach about certificates?

Hint

(a) Letters never mix. (b) The dual forbids repetition, not mixing. (c) Compute \(\frac{1+t}{1-t} \cdot \frac{1-t}{1+t}\) before trusting your instincts.

Worked answer

(a) Surviving words: \(x^n\) and \(y^n\) — floors \(1, 2, 2, 2, \ldots\), \(H_A = \frac{1+t}{1-t}\). (b) \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi\xi, \eta\eta\}\), so \(A^{!} = k\langle \xi, \eta\rangle/(\xi^2, \eta^2)\) with alternating words: floors \(1, 2, 2, 2, \ldots\). (c) The gate passes: \(\frac{1+t}{1-t} \cdot \frac{1-t}{1+t} = 1\) ✓ — the series forgives this algebra entirely. (d) The chaining pattern is real: the forbidden paths \(x \to y\) and \(y \to x\) chain into the composite \(x \to x\), which stays alive (\(x^2 \neq 0\)). But the conviction it once earned is false: the machine runs the staircase and finds every diagonal through \(N = 6\) exact, \(d^2 = 0\) everywhere — no homology at all. Ring theory agrees: \(yx - xy\) lies in the ideal (both generators are in it), so \(A \cong k[x,y]/(xy)\), a quadric hypersurface — and quadratic hypersurfaces are Koszul by a theorem of Tate. Deeper still, no quadratic monomial algebra could have been the sinner: all are Koszul (Backelin's criterion). The honest certificate is exactness, and here it acquits; the lesson is that a recalled pattern is a hypothesis, not a verdict — and the genuinely unforgivable algebras live outside the monomial class, in Exhibit C's territory.

Exercise 5 · The certified class, priced ◆
Let \(A = k\langle x, y \rangle/(yx - xy - x^2)\) — the Jordan plane, one twist short of commutativity. (a) Compute \(R^{\perp}\) from the pairing. (b) Count both rooms' floors and run the series gate. (c) The relation rewrites every \(yx\) as \(xy + x^2\), sorting each word into \(x^a y^b\) form — a PBW re-ordering. Why does Priddy's theorem then certify Koszulity without running the staircase — and what does Exercise 4's price say about running it anyway?

Hint

(a) Score \(\alpha \xi \otimes \xi + \beta \xi \otimes \eta + \gamma \eta \otimes \xi + \delta \eta \otimes \eta\) against the relation. (b) The dual's three relations leave a single two-letter word standing. (c) What does a PBW basis force the floors to be — and what did trusting a memory cost in Exercise 4?

Worked answer

(a) The relation \(yx - xy - x^2\) scores \(-\alpha - \beta + \gamma\) on the dual tensor, so \(R^{\perp} = \mathrm{span}\{\xi\xi + \eta\xi,\; \xi\eta + \eta\xi,\; \eta\eta\}\). (b) The re-ordering \(yx \mapsto xy + x^2\) sorts every word to \(x^a y^b\): floors \(1, 2, 3, 4, \ldots\), \(H_A = \frac{1}{(1-t)^2}\). The dual kills \(\eta\eta\) and folds \(\xi\xi\) and \(\xi\eta\) into \(\eta\xi\): every word of degree three or more dies, floors \(1, 2, 1, 0, \ldots\), \(H_{A^{!}} = (1+t)^2\). The gate reads \(\frac{1}{(1-t)^2} \cdot (1-t)^2 = 1\) ✓. (c) Priddy's theorem certifies what the re-ordering implies: a PBW algebra's staircase is exact because the re-ordering and the dual's bookkeeping are compatible — Koszulity by class membership, no staircase run. Exercise 4's price runs it anyway: every diagonal exact through \(N = 4\), \(d^2 = 0\) everywhere — no homology at all. The pass was never in doubt; the audit was free.

Exercise 6 · Cubic fingerprints, and the frontier ◐
(a) Why does \(B = k[x,y]/(x^3)\) have the same quadratic shadow as the polynomial room? (b) Count \(B\)'s floors. (c) Run the exterior mirror's gate against \(B\) and read the failure. (d) Show the fingerprint generalizes: for \(B_d = k[x,y]/(x^d)\), the same computation gives \(1 - t^d\) — which coefficient carries the signature, and why? (e) Exhibit C says the gate is necessary, not sufficient. What certificate settles Koszulity in general, and why is Exhibit C's Roos family a stronger witness than any series computation could catch?

Hint

(b) \(x^a y^b\) with \(a \leq 2\). (d) Compute the \(d\)-th coefficient: which floors changed shape, and where does the corner sit?

Worked answer

(a) The pairing machine reads only floor two: commutativity is the quadratic data, and \(x^3\) lives above its sightline. (b) Floors \(1, 2, 3, 3, 3, \ldots\) — words \(x^a y^b\) with \(a \leq 2\). (c) \(H_B \cdot H_{\wedge}(-t) = 1 - t^3\): every coefficient vanishes except the third, \(-1\), where the flat floors first fail the exterior mirror's demand — the invisible cubing relation, named by its exponent. (d) For \(B_d\), floors run \(1, 2, \ldots, d-1, d, d, \ldots\); the \(d\)-th coefficient of the product is \(h_d - 2h_{d-1} + h_{d-2} = d - 2d + (d-1) = -1\), and all other coefficients telescope to zero: the failure is \(1 - t^d\), the relation's fingerprint in its own exponent. (e) Exactness of the staircase — or membership in a certified class (PBW-type; quadratic monomial, always Koszul by Backelin's criterion), or the literature's theorem, named. Exhibit C carries the sharpest known form: Roos' algebras stay linear to arbitrarily high order — every finite check passes — yet are not Koszul, so no series computation, run to any length, could ever have caught them.
