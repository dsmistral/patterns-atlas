The Patterns Atlas · P33 · the second move of tier 3

The sheaf cohomology bridge

Local answers that fail to glue are not dead ends: the refusals themselves form a space, and that space — the cohomology — is the obstruction, computed, named, and counted.

New word: sheaf. A sheaf is a rule for hanging local data on the pieces of a space: to each patch it assigns the things that live on that patch — numbers on a town, temperatures on a room, functions on an interval — and whenever one patch sits inside a bigger one, the data restricts, the way a weather report for a country specializes to a weather report for your district. That is the whole definition: data per patch, restriction between patches, and one honesty clause, gluing: if two patches carry data that agrees on their overlap, the union patch carries exactly one piece of data restricting back to both. Sheaves are what "local" means when it is said carefully. This page never leaves the definition's front door: its question is what to do when the gluing clause is the one that fails.

1 · Before we begin

P12 taught the move of naming an obstruction: impossibility is information, and the information is a number that refuses to change. P31 taught the discipline of jobs: say what the maps must do before touching any construction. P69, with B11, taught the cancellations of differences: take differences of a sequence and middle terms die — and differences of differences die entirely. And the bridge pages B09–B10 built the concrete machine this page inherits: numbers on towns, rises on roads, and the loop test that decides which rise tables come from a potential column.

Here is the tension this page resolves. B10 ended with a verdict and a count: a rise table either passes every loop test — and then a potential column exists — or it refuses somewhere; and \(\dim\) independent loop tests, \(E - V + 1\) of them, are enough to decide. But when the answer is no, B10's story stops. The refusal is reported, the table is repaired, and the obstruction is thrown away. That is honest arithmetic and wasted mathematics. A refusal that survives every repair, that adds and scales like a number, that every two refusals can be combined and compared — that is not debris. It is an object, it lives in a space, and the dimension of that space counts exactly the holes in the landscape. This page builds that space, first on graphs — Kirchhoff's voltage law is the machine's first published customer — and then on the patches of a shape, where the data is no longer numbers on towns but readings on overlapping patches, and the same three-step machine counts the holes of the shape itself.

2 · Meet the problem

Three surveyors must map a triangular mountain, and they never stand on two corners at once. Each pair measures the altitude difference between their two corners, twice, walking it in both directions — and the two walkings agree as they must, sign flipped: the climb from \(A\) up to \(B\) is the descent from \(B\) down to \(A\), negated. Their field book, oriented \(A \to B\), \(B \to C\), \(C \to A\):

legmeasured risewalked back
\(A \to B\)\(+40\)\(B \to A\): \(-40\) ✓
\(B \to C\)\(+60\)\(C \to B\): \(-60\) ✓
\(C \to A\)\(-90\)\(A \to C\): \(+90\) ✓

Every one of the six walkings is consistent with its partner. So: what are the altitudes? Pin it the honest way — P01 would be proud. An altitude column \((h_A, h_B, h_C)\) must satisfy the rise relations: \(h_B - h_A = 40\), \(h_C - h_B = 60\), \(h_A - h_C = -90\). Add all three: the left sides telescope to \(0\) — P69's cancellation, the only computation in sight — so the right sides must total \(0\) too. They total \(40 + 60 - 90 = +10\). There is no altitude column. Six honest pairwise measurements, zero global answer.

You have seen this shape of failure: it is B10's loop test refusing. And you have seen the repair: change one entry, \(C \to A\) from \(-90\) to \(-100\), and the table glues. But suppose the measurements are not negotiable — they are data, the census was honest, the mountain is what it is. Then three questions appear that B10 never asked, and they are the questions of this page.

How many refusals can a landscape hold? The triangle can refuse in exactly one independent way — every refusal on it is a multiple of the \((+1, +1, +1)\)-shaped one. The square-with-shortcut of B10 can refuse in two independent ways. A landscape of \(V\) towns and \(E\) roads holds exactly \(E - V + 1\) independent refusals — one per independent loop, the same count B10 certified as the number of enough tests. Refusals are not accidents; they come in a fixed supply, and the supply is computable in advance.

Can refusals be compared, combined, repaired into each other? Add two refusing tables entry by entry: the loop sums add, so the refusal adds — refusals scale too. Repair one by any potential column: the loop sum never changes. So each table carries one number no repair can touch — its loop sum, \(+10\) for the surveyors — and two tables are repair-equivalent exactly when their loop sums agree. The refusals form a space; its classes are the invariants P09 taught you to hunt for; and the space is one-dimensional for the triangle, two for the square-with-shortcut, \(E - V + 1\) in general.

And when the patches are not towns but overlaps? Here is where the story grows past B10. Three cameras photograph a circular room, each covering a third, each pair sharing one arc of overlap; a stitching program matches the photos on the overlaps and reports, per overlap, one mismatch number. When does a global photo exist? Walk the question cold and you see it is the surveyors again — patches for towns, overlaps for roads — except the pieces now tile a shape, a circle, and the refusals, if any, must say something about the shape itself: the circle has a hole; the triangle of towns has exactly one loop; these are not coincidences. Local data, honest pairwise, a global answer that may refuse — and the refusal measuring the room.

The move this page names is the deliberate promotion of refusals into mathematics: gather the local data into cochains, take their disagreements with one difference operator \(\delta\), use the always-true telescoping law \(\delta \circ \delta = 0\) to split data into repairable and irreparable, and keep the irreparable part — organized, added, scaled — as the cohomology, the obstruction space whose dimension is the shape's honest hole count.

3 · Name the move

§2 took a refused table and asked what survives every repair. The move, named:

Pattern P33 — The sheaf cohomology bridge. When a question is answered locally — one answer per patch, honest and computable — and the local answers must assemble into one global answer, do not attempt the assembly and do not mourn its failure. Instead: (1) collect the local data and the pairwise disagreement data into cochains; (2) compute the disagreements of the disagreements with the difference operator \(\delta\), whose always-true law \(\delta \circ \delta = 0\) — a telescoping, every time — certifies that repairable data is automatically disagreement-free; (3) form the quotient: all local data, modulo the repairable. This quotient is the cohomology \(H\). If \(H = 0\), the local answers glue — this is a theorem, not a hope. If \(H \neq 0\), each of its elements is a named, checkable, addable obstruction, and \(\dim H\) counts the independent holes. The bridge runs both ways: a shape's holes produce refusals, and refusals, organized, measure the shape.

Or, in words: when local answers refuse to agree, stop repairing and start bookkeeping. Every refusal belongs to a class that no repair can touch; collect the classes; they form a space; its dimension is how many independent ways the world can say no. Zero means yes — local honestly implies global — and every nonzero answer is a hole with a name.

The move applies when three keys line up, and each key names its own jam-mode:

Key 1 — the claim is local-to-global. The sentence has the shape "each patch has an answer; when does one global answer exist?" — or its contrapositive, "this global answer cannot exist", offered without a reason. Jam-mode: the question is genuinely global with no local structure — then there is no \(\delta\) to build, and P02's tables or P25's census own the problem.

Key 2 — the data restricts. Bigger patches inherit data from the space they sit inside (a reading on a whole room restricts to a reading on a corner); overlaps carry comparisons. Without restriction there are no sheaves — see the on-ramp — and no overlaps means no loop tests; the story is over before it starts. Jam-mode: the patches overlap in uncontrolled, un-listable ways — then first draw the overlap pattern, which is B09's graph-drawing work, not this page's.

Key 3 — the repairs are honest. "Adjust the local answers so they agree" must mean something exact: adjustments that change nothing measurable on overlaps (adding a potential column to town altitudes, a constant to a room's temperatures). The repairable data must form its own collection, or the quotient has no meaning. Jam-mode: the allowed repairs scramble the data instead of shifting it — then no invariant survives, and the obstruction hunt of P12 needs a new invariant before any cohomology can be built.

Box P33.A — the machine, in three obligations. Every instance of the pattern — graphs, covers, calculus, physics — runs the same three steps, and every step is checkable by hand on small cases:

1 · Cochains. Fix the patches and their overlaps. A cochain is the assignment of one piece of data per patch — altitudes per town, readings per camera — and the comparison data per overlap: one level of data per level of overlap. Level 0: data on patches. Level 1: data on pairwise overlaps. Level 2: data on triple overlaps, if any. Write \(C^0, C^1\) for the collections; each is a space in its own right — add entry-wise, scale entry-wise.

2 · The difference operator \(\delta\), and the law that never fails. \(\delta\) takes level-\(k\) data to level-\((k+1)\) data by telescoping subtraction: from patch readings \(g\), the overlap comparison is \((\delta g)_{ij} = g_j - g_i\); from overlap comparisons \(m\), the triple-overlap check is \((\delta m)_{ijk} = m_{ij} - m_{ik} + m_{jk}\). And always \(\delta \circ \delta = 0\): \((g_j - g_i) - (g_k - g_i) + (g_k - g_j)\) cancels to \(0\), every middle term dying in pairs — P69's cancellation, running as a law of nature. This law is what makes repairable data automatically triple-consistent: no honest repair can ever manufacture a triple-overlap contradiction.

3 · The classes. Call the repairable data \(B = \operatorname{im} \delta\) — the coboundaries. Call the triple-consistent data \(Z = \ker \delta\) — the cocycles. The law \(\delta^2 = 0\) says \(B \subseteq Z\), and the cohomology is the quotient \(H = Z / B\): consistent data, modulo repairable. Two data-sets are the same class exactly when a repair bridges them; \(H = 0\) means every consistent datum is repairable — gluing succeeds; \(\dim H\) counts the independent survivals. On a graph there are no triple overlaps, so \(Z = C^1\) is everything and \(H^1 = C^1/B^1\): exactly B10's tables modulo potential-differences, and the loop test is the membership test for \(B^1\).

Each obligation names its own proof duty: the cochain spaces must be honestly organized (level by level); the law \(\delta^2 = 0\) must be proved, telescoped, never waved; and the quotient must be shown well-defined — which the law has already done, since \(B \subseteq Z\) is exactly the statement that repairable data never leaves the consistent world.

Box P33.B — where the move appears. Graphs and circuits — Kirchhoff's voltage law, the worked application of §4 and the machine's oldest customer (1845). Covers of shapes — camera stitches, the circle of §4 counted from three arcs. Calculus — antiderivatives on a loop, the de Rham door: every function integrates locally, and the obstruction to a global antiderivative is one integral around the circle. Physics — potentials from field measurements, wave functions glued around a flux (the Aharonov–Bohm shape). Data practice — distributed databases reconciled on overlaps; sensor networks; the stitching of any survey from local views. The three-key signature: a local-to-global claim (build \(\delta\), count the classes); an "impossible" verdict without a name (the name is a class); a shape suspected of holes (make the local data refuse, and read off the dimension). The failure signatures are §5's: tests you did not run, repairs that only move the refusal, and data whose twist lives in the comparison, not the values.

4 · The move at work — three encounters

Three worked examples, one per homeland of Box P33.B. The first is the machine's oldest customer and this pattern's mandated application — circuits, where Kirchhoff stated the loop test in 1845 and where every number below can be checked by hand. The second moves from graphs to covers: a shape's hole, counted from the local data of three cameras. The third crosses into calculus, where the same three obligations run on functions and integrals. The exhibits of §5 then break the move on purpose.

Example 1 · Graphs and numbers — Kirchhoff's loop obstruction

Pin down the problem (P01, again). Three towns \(A, B, C\) at the corners of a mountain; a surveyor measures the rise along each road: \(A \to B\): \(+40\), \(B \to C\): \(+60\), \(C \to A\): \(-90\). The table came back refused. B10 threw the verdict away — the table is wrong — and P12 would hunt the obstruction: the loop sum \(40 + 60 - 90 = +10\), a quantity no repair touches. This page asks the promoted question: what is the collection of all such refusals, and can it be organized into a space?

The data restricts. A road's rise restricts to nothing smaller — roads are the smallest patches — but towns are level-0 patches and roads are level-1 overlaps, exactly the two-level picture of Box P33.A. A candidate global answer is a choice of altitude per town, \(g = (g_A, g_B, g_C)\), and it predicts the rises: \(g_B - g_A\) along \(A \to B\), and so on — potential-differences, exactly \(\delta g\). The tables that glue are precisely the ones in the image \(B^1\), and the refusal's home is the quotient \(H^1 = C^1/B^1\) of Box P33.A's graph case — one independent class per loop of the graph.

Run the machine. Level 1: \(C^1\) is all edge-data \((t_{AB}, t_{BC}, t_{CA})\), a 3-dimensional space. Level 0: \(C^0\) is all altitude choices, 3-dimensional; its image \(B^1 = \{(g_B-g_A,\; g_C-g_B,\; g_A-g_C)\}\) is the plane \(x + y + z = 0\), 2-dimensional. No triple overlaps exist on a graph, so every table is triple-consistent: \(Z^1 = C^1\). The cohomology is 3-space modulo the plane: \(H^1\) is a line, \(\dim H^1 = E - V + c = 3 - 3 + 1 = 1\) for a connected graph — one class per loop. The class map is B10's loop sum itself: \(t \mapsto t_{AB} + t_{BC} + t_{CA}\), and the refused table \(+10\) is the point where the surveyors' data sits off the repairable plane.

The edit and the test — do not confuse them. B10's surveyor "fixes" the table by changing the reading \(t_{CA}\) to \(-100\): loop sum \(0\), altitudes \(g_A = 0, g_B = 40, g_C = 100\), refusal gone. But a reading is a measurement — the edit is a forgery, and it moved the class from \(+10\) to \(0\). The honest move does not touch the table at all: it asks a membership question — does some altitude column have these exact rises, i.e. does the table lie in \(B^1\)? — and the loop sum answers it, because \(t \mapsto t_{AB} + t_{BC} + t_{CA}\) vanishes on \(B^1\) and on nothing else in this graph. What the quotient prices is the distance from the table to the repairable plane; §5's Exhibit B returns to the forger.

A hole in the census. The square-with-shortcut of B10 — roads \(AB: 4\), \(BC: -9\), \(CD: +7\), \(DA: -2\), plus the shortcut \(AC: -6\) — ran the big loop first: \(AB + BC + CD + DA = 4 - 9 + 7 - 2 = 0\). Passed. But the shortcut created a second loop, and the triangle tests fail on both sides of it: the low triangle \(AB + BC + CA = 4 - 9 + 6 = +1\), the high triangle \(AC + CD + DA = -6 + 7 - 2 = -1\). One passed test out of three certified nothing — the graph's \(\dim H^1 = 2\), two independent classes, and a repair that zeroes the big loop's class leaves both triangle classes untouched. One class, one test, no substitutes; §5's Exhibit A makes this refusal of the single-test habit the centerpiece.

Example 2 · Geometry and topology — the hole, counted from a cover

Pin down the problem. §2's third question: three cameras photograph a circular room, each covering a bit more than a third of the wall, so every stretch of wall appears in at least one photo — and no spot appears in all three. Overlaps: \(O_{12}\) (cameras 1 & 2), \(O_{23}\), \(O_{31}\) — three pairwise overlaps, no triple overlap. The local answers: each camera's photo, plus one comparison per overlap — how far to slide camera \(j\)'s photo to line up with camera \(i\)'s on the stretch they share. The global question: does one panorama exist — three shifts \(g_1, g_2, g_3\), one per camera, reproducing the comparisons exactly?

Run the machine — Box P33.A's three obligations, in order. Level 0: \(C^0\) is all per-camera shifts \((g_1, g_2, g_3)\), dimension 3. Level 1: \(C^1\) is all per-overlap comparisons \((m_{12}, m_{23}, m_{31})\), dimension 3. There is no level 2 — no triple overlap — so \(Z^1 = C^1\): with no triple check to fail, every table is triple-consistent. The repairable tables are the images of shifts: \(\delta g = (g_2 - g_1,\; g_3 - g_2,\; g_1 - g_3)\), and these always satisfy \(m_{12} + m_{23} + m_{31} = 0\) — the sum telescopes to nothing, B11's discrete derivative around a loop, P69 again. So \(B^1\) is the plane \(m_{12} + m_{23} + m_{31} = 0\), dimension 2 (its preimage's kernel — the shifts with all comparisons zero — is the constant shifts \((g,g,g)\), dimension 1). The quotient: \(H^1 = C^1/B^1\), dimension \(3 - 2 = 1\). One class. The room has exactly one hole, and the cover counted it — without ever seeing the interior, without any altitude column, from comparison data alone.

The winding table. Take the comparison table \(m_{12} = m_{23} = m_{31} = +1\): each photo must slide one unit against the next, all the way around. Its sum is \(+3\), not \(0\) — a nonzero class, and no triple of shifts produces it. This is not a failed repair; it is a measurement: the wall's pattern advances by three units per lap... or, if the units are honest, by one — the class records the winding, P12's named obstruction, now an element of a space that can be added and scaled. Twice around the room: table \((+2,+2,+2)\), sum 6, exactly twice the generator. The classes are the room's lap-counting arithmetic.

Close the trap and the class dies. Suppose the room were a triangular hall with one corner spot visible to all three cameras — a triple overlap \(O_{123}\). Level 2 exists now, one number \(m\)-check per triple: \((\delta m)_{123} = m_{12} - m_{13} + m_{23}\), and the winding table fails it: with \(m_{13} = -m_{31} = -1\), the check reads \(1 - (-1) + 1 = 3 \neq 0\). The table that survived on the circle is exposed as inconsistent, not merely unrepairable — it never enters \(Z^1\) at all. And for consistent tables the quotient collapses: \(Z^1 = \ker \delta^1\) drops to the plane, \(H^1 = 0\), every consistent panorama stitches. Fill the hole and the obstruction has nowhere to live — the same census, read as Euler's \(V - E + F\): three patches, three overlaps, one triple: \(3 - 3 + 1 = 1 = \dim H^0 - \dim H^1 + \dim H^2\), with \(\dim H^0 = 1\) (constant shifts) and \(\dim H^1 = 0\).

Moves used: pin the problem (P01), obstruction with a name (P12), the discrete derivative on a loop (B11, B10), telescoping (P69).

Example 3 · Calculus — the antiderivative on a loop

Pin down the problem. A smooth signal on a circular dial, \(f(\theta) = a + b\cos\theta + c\sin\theta\), read around the full turn \(0 \le \theta < 2\pi\). The local question answers itself: on any small arc, an antiderivative exists — \(F(\theta) = a\theta + b\sin\theta - c\cos\theta\) works there, by the fundamental theorem. The global question: does a periodic antiderivative exist, one \(F\) with \(F' = f\) and \(F(\theta + 2\pi) = F(\theta)\), a single potential for the whole dial? This is the surveyors' problem with the continuum of towns: the signal is rise data, the antiderivative is the altitude column.

Run the machine. The patches are the arcs of the dial, and they overlap. Level 0: the local antiderivatives \(F\), one per arc — always existing, honest. The repairs: adding a constant to one arc's \(F\) changes nothing measurable (the derivative never sees it) — the honest repairs are exactly the per-patch constants, the continuous avatar of the town-altitude column. Level 1: the comparison data is the derivative itself — \(f = F'\) is the rise operator of B09 in its continuous form, and \(\frac{d}{d\theta}\) plays \(\delta\). The law \(\delta \circ \delta = 0\) is the oldest telescoping of all: the second difference of a function cancels in pairs — mixed differences commute, every middle term dies — P69 in continuum clothes.

The classes. The repairable data — the \(f\) that equal \(F'\) for a periodic \(F\) — form \(B\). The consistent data is everything: a 1-dimensional dial has no triple-overlap check for a function to fail, so \(Z\) is all smooth \(f\), and the quotient \(H = Z/B\) is the functions modulo the derivatives of periodic functions. One invariant decides membership, and it is B10's loop sum made continuous: the class map is the integral around the circle, \[[f] \longmapsto \int_0^{2\pi} f(\theta)\, d\theta.\] A periodic \(F\) returns to itself after a lap, so its rise integrates to \(0\): every repairable \(f\) has \(\int_0^{2\pi} f = 0\). And conversely — the converse is the whole theorem — a function with zero integral is the derivative of a periodic potential, built by integrating from a basepoint. So \(H\) is one-dimensional: the class of \(f\) is its integral, and nothing else.

Receipts. For \(f(\theta) = a + b\cos\theta + c\sin\theta\), the oscillating parts pay for themselves — \(\int_0^{2\pi} \cos\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \sin\theta = 0\) — and the obstruction is pure offset: \(\int_0^{2\pi} f = 2\pi a\). The verdict: a periodic antiderivative exists exactly when \(a = 0\), however large \(b\) and \(c\) rage. Numbers: \(a = 2, b = 3, c = 1\) gives \(\int_0^{2\pi} f = 4\pi \approx 12.5664\) — a nonzero class, and no repair touches it; drop the offset to \(a = 0\) and \(F = 3\sin\theta - \cos\theta\) closes the loop, class \(0\). The offset \(a\) is the dial's winding datum of Example 2 — the same generator, wearing calculus clothes.

The door this opens. Everything above survives with the dial replaced by any shape: patches, forms \(f\,dx + g\,dy\), the operator \(d\), the law \(d \circ d = 0\), classes measured by integrals around loops. That is de Rham cohomology — calculus discovering the holes of a shape with its own hands, no graphs required. The pattern's obligations are unchanged; only the sizes of the cochains grow. P47 chases classes through maps; P46 assembles them page by page.

Moves used: pin the problem (P01), rise data (B09), loop sums (B10), telescoping (P69), receipts on numbers (P25).

5 · The move under fire — three exhibits

Three failure exhibits, each produced by breaking one of the three keys. The pattern survives them — but only because the keys were named in §3, and each exhibit names the key it broke.

Exhibit A · The missed test

An engineer inherits the square-with-shortcut table of Example 1 — roads \(AB: 4\), \(BC: -9\), \(CD: +7\), \(DA: -2\), shortcut \(AC: -6\) — runs the test the manual taught, the perimeter loop \(AB + BC + CD + DA = 0\), and ships the verdict: consistent, panorama approved. The verdict is false. The shortcut created a second loop, and neither triangle was on the test sheet: the low triangle reads \(AB + BC + CA = +1\), the high triangle \(AC + CD + DA = -1\). Two independent classes (\(\dim H^1 = E - V + c = 2\)), one tested, both failures invisible to the test that ran.

What broke: Key 3's honesty, and with it the census discipline of P25. The failure signature is a consistency verdict issued from fewer tests than the class count — "it glues" claimed without its census. Mitigation: count the classes first — \(E - V + c\) takes a moment — then demand one passing receipt per independent class. Price: the test suite grows with the loop count; on a twenty-town lattice the single perimeter test becomes dozens, and none of them is optional. Default habit: every "it glues" claim ships with its class dimension. A verdict without its census is this exhibit, waiting to happen.

Exhibit B · The repair that never lands

Two faces, one moral. Face one — the forger. The surveyors of Example 1, refused, do not report the refusal; they edit a reading — \(t_{CA} = -90\) becomes \(-100\) — and the loop sum vanishes. The edit did not repair the data; it replaced the data, and the class moved with it: \(+10\) to \(0\), a real obstruction erased by a fake measurement. Face two — the honest repairer. The cameras of Example 2 refuse to stitch, and the operator keeps adjusting: nudge each photo's shift \(g_i\), try again. Honest repairs — but every one of them moves the comparison table by \(\delta g\), and the loop sum of \(\delta g\) telescopes to zero (P69). The class is P09's invariant, upgraded to a space: no sequence of honest repairs, however long, lands a nonzero class at zero. The operator can adjust until the heat death of the room.

What broke: nothing in the machine — the machine is exactly what the repairer never consulted. The failure signature is effort spent inside a class: a repair loop that "almost works" every time and never finishes. Mitigation: compute the class before the first repair. Price: when the class is nonzero, the honest options are expensive — add a measurement, change the shape, or redesign — and knowing this early is the entire value. Default habit: a refusal gets named and measured (which class? how big?), not adjusted. The repair that never lands is the adjustment that never should have started.

Exhibit C · The twisted data

Cut a paper strip, give it a half-twist, tape the ends: the Möbius band. Cover it with three patches \(U_1, U_2, U_3\) running around the loop, overlapping pairwise. Each patch can be given a handedness — an arrow perpendicular to the band, drawn on the patch. The local question is trivial: every patch carries a handedness. The global question is the pattern's question: does one handedness exist for the whole band?

The twist lives in the comparisons. On each overlap the two patches' arrows must agree up to a sign \(\varepsilon\): \(g_i = \varepsilon_{ij}\, g_j\) with \(\varepsilon_{ij} \in \{+1, -1\}\). On an ordinary band every \(\varepsilon = +1\). On the Möbius band, one trip around the loop flips the arrow: one overlap carries \(\varepsilon = -1\), and the product around the loop is \(\varepsilon_{12}\,\varepsilon_{23}\,\varepsilon_{31} = -1\). Chain the three comparisons — \(g_1 = \varepsilon_{12}\varepsilon_{23}\varepsilon_{31}\, g_1\) — and the arithmetic detonates: \(g_1 = -g_1\), so \(g_1 = 0\), and the only global handedness is none. (Not \(+1\) followed by \(-1\); the census below is exact.)

The census. Allow each patch's arrow the values \(\{-1, 0, +1\}\): \(3^3 = 27\) assignments to test. Untwisted band (\(\varepsilon_{12} = \varepsilon_{23} = \varepsilon_{31} = +1\)): the gluing conditions \(g_1 = g_2 = g_3\) pass for exactly 3 assignments — \((0,0,0)\), \((1,1,1)\), \((-1,-1,-1)\) — three global handednesses. Twisted band (one \(\varepsilon = -1\)): the 27 assignments yield exactly one passing, \((0,0,0)\), the empty answer. The twist did not touch a single local value — every patch still carries its arrow — it rewired the comparisons, and the global count collapsed from 3 to 0. This is P12's obstruction wearing the pattern's clothes: the refusal has a name (the sign product \(-1\)), a checkable test (chain the loop), and a census.

What broke: nothing visible — that is the exhibit's point. Box P33's Key 2 — "the data restricts" — came with fine print: the restriction rules are part of the data, and the rules themselves can carry a twist. The failure signature is a refusal whose cause is not in any local value but in the overlap signs. Mitigation: audit the comparison system, not just the values — multiply the signs around every loop. Price: coefficient systems double the bookkeeping, and the honest census must range over them. Default habit: when local answers all exist and global ones do not, read the gluing rules twice — the twist is hiding there.

Scope, honestly. Four limits of this page's promises. Gluing is not uniqueness: \(H^1 = 0\) says a global answer exists; uniqueness is \(H^0\)'s business, and answers are unique only up to the repair group — the panorama stitches, and still slides. Covers must be good: every cover here had honest, fully-tested overlaps; a cover with a defective overlap can lie, and the refinement theorem that repairs bad covers is deferred — this page's covers were good by construction. \(H^1\) is not the whole story: shapes carry higher obstructions — \(H^2\) counts enclosed cavities, and a sphere's \(H^1 = 0\) while its \(H^2 \neq 0\); those doors are P47 and P46. Coefficients matter: Exhibit C computed with a sign-system; change the coefficients and the same shape counts differently. The machine runs on any honest coefficient system — but the census must say which one it used.

Moves used: named obstruction (P12), invariant (P09), census (P25), telescoping (P69), loop sums (B10).

6 · The doors this opens

Every pattern page ends by pointing at the doors it passes without opening. Four here, at honest distances.

The nearest door — P47 · Diagram chase. This page computed classes by hand, one shape at a time. The moment classes must travel — a map between shapes drags cohomology along with it — the bookkeeping becomes arrows between the cochain spaces, and the chase begins. P47 is the nearest door because this page already built everything it chases through: the spaces \(C^0, C^1\), the operator \(\delta\), the law \(\delta^2 = 0\). Distance: one move. What it carries: exactness arguments — why "\(H^1 = 0\) here" can be inherited from "\(H^1 = 0\) there", without recomputing anything.

The far door — P46 · Spectral sequences. This page's covers had two, maybe three levels. When a shape's data comes pre-organized in layers — a filtration — the machine must be run in stages, page by page, each stage's output feeding the next. That staged machine is the spectral sequence, and it is the far door because its first honest example already outruns this page's notation. Distance: far, and worth the walk. What it carries: the census of obstructions for shapes too complicated for a single cover.

The widening door — P62 · Sheafify on site. This page treated "the data that restricts" as a given. P62 asks what data deserves to restrict — the sheaf axioms as a design discipline, and the site as the general home of covers. Distance: medium. What it carries: the right to say "cohomology" about data that is not functions on a shape at all.

The moving door — P53 · Deformation cohomology. Here the shape stood still and the data refused. When the shape itself flexes, obstructions organize into \(H^1\)'s that govern moves and \(H^2\)'s that wall them off — the same machine, run on a family of shapes instead of one. Distance: medium-far. What it carries: the grammar of what can be deformed into what. (P52, P54, and P65 wait behind these, each a customer of the same cochain bookkeeping.)

Default habit: when a local-to-global question survives this page's keys, walk through the doors in this order — chase first (cheap, inherits answers), sheafify second (clarifies the data), stage third (P46, when the layers are real), and let the moving shapes (P53) come to you.

7 · Where the move came from

Non-load-bearing, and honest about it: the page above works if this section is skipped. But the move has a documented history, and it is stranger than the mathematics suggests.

The machine's first customer was a circuit. Kirchhoff, stating the voltage law in 1845 and publishing it in 1847, wrote down the loop test in its physical form: potentials around a loop sum to zero. The general shape behind it waited half a century. Poincaré's Analysis situs (1895) built the census of holes — the Betti numbers — by hand, combinatorially. de Rham (1931) proved that calculus computes the same census: the integrals of Example 3, promoted to a theorem. Čech (1932) moved the computation onto covers, Example 2's camera-stitching arithmetic.

Then the war. Leray, a prisoner in Oflag XVII-A from 1940, taught mathematics in the camp and — avoiding topics he feared might be weaponized, by his own account — built a new, deliberately abstract machine: the sheaf, published in 1946. The idea that "the data that restricts" deserves its own object was born in a prison notebook. Cartan's seminar (1950–51) rebuilt it for complex-analysis customers; Serre's FAC (1955) ran it on algebraic varieties; Grothendieck's Tôhoku paper (1957) re-founded the whole bookkeeping on the law \(\delta^2 = 0\) itself, the axiom this page computed with; Godement (1958) wrote it all down as a reference. From a circuit rule to a prisoner's abstraction to the grammar of modern geometry: the move's lineage is part of its meaning.

8 · Exercises

Five exercises, moons for difficulty: ●○○ read-and-compute, ●●○ multi-step, ●●● full argument, ◆ diagnose a pattern-less situation, ◐ open exploration. Hints first; worked answers after each.

Exercise 1 ●○○ · Loop sums, classes, and a census

(a) A triangle's rise table reads \(t_{AB} = +5\), \(t_{BC} = -2\), \(t_{CA} = -1\). Compute its class. Can any altitude column produce this table?
(b) A square's table reads \(t_{AB} = 2\), \(t_{BC} = 3\), \(t_{CD} = -4\), \(t_{DA} = -1\). Compute its class; if the class is \(0\), produce an altitude column.
(c) Census: among all \(3^3 = 27\) triangle tables with entries in \(\{-1, 0, +1\}\), how many glue? How many carry a refusal, and what are the possible class values?

Hint

The class map is the loop sum; on the triangle, gluing means membership in \(B^1\), the plane \(t_{AB} + t_{BC} + t_{CA} = 0\). For the square, altitudes can be built edge by edge: set \(g_A = 0\) and walk.

Worked answer

(a) Loop sum \(5 - 2 - 1 = +2\): a nonzero class, and no altitude column exists — the table sits \(2\) units off the repairable plane. (b) Loop sum \(2 + 3 - 4 - 1 = 0\): class \(0\). Walk: \(g_A = 0\), \(g_B = 2\), \(g_C = 2 + 3 = 5\), \(g_D = g_C - 4 = 1\); check the last road: \(g_A - g_D = 0 - 1 = -1 = t_{DA}\) ✓. (c) Gluing tables are the triples with sum \(0\): \((0,0,0)\) and the six permutations of \((+1, 0, -1)\) — exactly \(7\) of \(27\). The other \(20\) carry refusals, with class values \(-3, -2, -1, +1, +2, +3\). One census line, one cohomology line: \(\dim H^1 = 1\).

Exercise 2 ●●○ · The camera cover, by rank

Return to Example 2's cover: three arcs, three pairwise overlaps, no triple overlap. (a) Verify the law \(\delta \circ \delta = 0\) by hand: expand \((g_2 - g_1) + (g_3 - g_2) + (g_1 - g_3)\) and name the pairs that cancel. (b) Compute \(\dim H^1\) by rank: \(\dim \ker \delta^0\), \(\dim \operatorname{im} \delta^0\), \(\dim Z^1\), and the quotient. (c) Where exactly did "no triple overlap" enter the computation?

Hint

For (b), \(Z^1 = \ker \delta^1\), but \(\delta^1\) maps into the triple-overlap data — which is the zero space here. Rank–nullity on \(\delta^0\) gives the image's dimension.

Worked answer

(a) The middle terms cancel in pairs: \(g_2\) dies against \(-g_2\), \(g_3\) against \(-g_3\), \(g_1\) against \(-g_1\) — a pure telescoping, every interior term appearing once with each sign. (b) \(\ker \delta^0\) = constant shifts \((g,g,g)\), dimension 1; \(\dim C^0 = 3\), so \(\dim \operatorname{im} \delta^0 = 3 - 1 = 2\), and the image is the plane \(m_{12} + m_{23} + m_{31} = 0\). With no triple overlap, \(C^2 = 0\), so every table is triple-consistent: \(Z^1 = C^1\), dimension 3. Then \(\dim H^1 = 3 - 2 = 1\) — the room's one hole, computed from ranks alone. (c) At \(Z^1\): a triple overlap would install a level-2 check \((\delta m)_{123} = m_{12} + m_{23} + m_{31}\), cutting \(Z^1\) down to the same plane and killing the quotient to \(0\) — the "close the trap" paragraph of Example 2.

Exercise 3 ●●● · The dial, fully

Prove Example 3's theorem completely. (a) Show that \(f(\theta) = a + b\cos\theta + c\sin\theta\) has a periodic antiderivative if and only if \(a = 0\), and that the obstruction is exactly \(\int_0^{2\pi} f = 2\pi a\). (b) Now the converse for general smooth \(f\): show that any smooth periodic \(f\) with \(\int_0^{2\pi} f = 0\) has a periodic antiderivative — build it, and check periodicity honestly. (c) Conclude that the class map \([f] \mapsto \int_0^{2\pi} f\) is an isomorphism onto \(\mathbb{R}\): cohomology \(H\) is one-dimensional, with explicit inverse.

Hint

For (a), integrate each term over the full period. For (b), try \(F(\theta) = \int_0^\theta f\); compute \(F(\theta + 2\pi) - F(\theta)\) and use the periodicity of \(f\) to identify the integral over one full turn.

Worked answer

(a) \(\int_0^{2\pi} \cos\theta\, d\theta = \int_0^{2\pi} \sin\theta\, d\theta = 0\), so the obstruction is \(2\pi a\). If \(a = 0\): \(F = b\sin\theta - c\cos\theta\) is periodic with \(F' = f\). If \(a \ne 0\): any antiderivative is \(a\theta + b\sin\theta - c\cos\theta\) plus a constant, and the \(a\theta\) term shifts by \(2\pi a \neq 0\) per lap — no periodic choice exists. (b) Define \(F(\theta) = \int_0^\theta f(t)\, dt\). Then \(F(\theta + 2\pi) - F(\theta) = \int_\theta^{\theta + 2\pi} f = \int_0^{2\pi} f\), because \(f\) is periodic — the integral over any window of length \(2\pi\) is the full-turn integral. If that integral is \(0\), then \(F(\theta + 2\pi) = F(\theta)\): periodic, and \(F' = f\) by the fundamental theorem. (c) Every class is determined by its integral, every real number is \(2\pi a\) for \(f = a\) alone: the map \([f] \mapsto \int_0^{2\pi} f\) is well-defined (zero on \(B\), by the lap argument), linear, surjective, and injective — if two classes' integrals agree, their difference has zero integral, lies in \(B\), and the classes are equal. One dimension, generator \([1]\), obstruction receipt \(4\pi \approx 12.5664\) when \(a = 2\).

Exercise 4 ◆ · The consultant's table

A surveyor hands you a report on five towns and six roads, with the rise table: \(t_{AB} = 3\), \(t_{BC} = -1\), \(t_{CA} = -2\) (the first triangle), \(t_{CD} = 2\), \(t_{DE} = 1\), \(t_{EC} = -1\) (the second). The towns form two triangles sharing town \(C\); the roads are exactly the six triangle edges. The report's verdict: "triangle check passed — the table is consistent." You have this page. What do you check, in what order, and what is the truth?

Hint

Count loops before trusting any verdict: \(E - V + c\). A consistency claim needs one receipt per independent class.

Worked answer

First, the class count: \(E - V + c = 6 - 5 + 1 = 2\) — two independent loops, so one passing test certifies nothing (Exhibit A's signature, arriving as a document). Second, find a loop basis: triangle \(ABC\) and triangle \(CDE\) — the shared town does not share a class (the census table of Example 1 said so: two triangles sharing a vertex carry \(\dim H^1 = 2\)). Third, run both tests. Triangle \(ABC\): \(3 + (-1) + (-2) = 0\) — passes, and indeed altitudes \(g_A = 0, g_B = 3, g_C = 2\) fit it. Triangle \(CDE\): \(2 + 1 + (-1) = +2\) — a hidden refusal, class \(+2\), invisible to the first test. No altitude column for all six roads exists. The verdict should read: "one of two classes tested; second fails; table refused." The consultant ran one test on a two-class graph — and the missing test was the entire story.

Exercise 5 ◐ · The other cohomology — and Euler's echo

This page computed \(H^1\); the same machine also produces \(H^0\). (a) Show that \(\ker \delta^0\) — the assignments \(g\) whose every comparison vanishes — are exactly the locally-constant assignments, and that \(\dim H^0\) counts the graph's connected components \(c\). (b) Combine with \(\dim H^1 = E - V + c\) to prove the echo: \(\dim H^0 - \dim H^1 = V - E\). Verify it on the family of Example 1's census table — the triangle, the tree, the two islands. (c) Open end: compute \(\dim H^0 - \dim H^1\) for the complete graph \(K_n\) and find the closed form; then hunt for a graph family where the echo is easier to compute than either term alone.

Hint

(a) If \(g_j = g_i\) on every road, the value cannot change along any walk — and walks reach everything in a component. (c) \(K_n\) has \(E = n(n-1)/2\); simplify \(V - E\).

Worked answer

(a) \((\delta g)_{ij} = g_j - g_i = 0\) on every road means \(g\) is constant along edges, hence constant on each connected component: the locally-constant assignments, one free value per island, \(\dim H^0 = c\). (b) \(\dim H^0 - \dim H^1 = c - (E - V + c) = V - E\). Triangle: \(1 - 1 = 0 = 3 - 3\) ✓. Six-town tree: \(1 - 0 = 1 = 6 - 5\) ✓. Two islands of one triangle each: \(2 - 2 = 0 = 6 - 6\) ✓. The census table's last column was this echo all along. (c) \(K_n\): \(\dim H^0 = 1\), \(\dim H^1 = \frac{n(n-1)}{2} - n + 1 = \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}\); the echo reads \(1 - \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2} = n - \frac{n(n-1)}{2} = V - E\) ✓ — and the echo is one arithmetic step while \(\dim H^1\) is a quotient, so for hand computation the echo is the cheap side of the ledger. The open end: the echo persists for covers and shapes — it is Euler characteristic in cohomological clothing, and Example 2's \(3 - 3 + 1\) was its first appearance.
