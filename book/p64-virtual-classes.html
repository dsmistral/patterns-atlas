The Patterns Atlas · P64 · the fifteenth move of tier 5

Virtual classes from obstruction theory

A room that fails its own census — points it undercounts, crowds it never promised — is not a failed room. Audit the books the constraints kept, assemble the audit into a corrected census-weight, and count against the correction: the number stands even where the room does not.

New word: the obstruction theory. Four ideas stand at the door, each an extension of something this book already built. (1) Cutting cleanly. A room carved out by constraints is honest when the constraints' first-order demands are independent at every solution — two lines crossing, not two lines overlapping. (2) The two ledgers. At any point of a room, the first-order motions the constraints permit form the moves — P53's first book, the kernel of the linear audit; the demands left over after the audit forms the obstructions — the second book, P53's \(H^2\) in pointwise dress. Where the constraints cut cleanly the second ledger is empty. (3) The books' verdict. Moves minus obstructions is the dimension the room should have — the number the census needs before it can be a number at all. (4) A class on a room is its census-weight: an honest smooth room carries one copy of itself, and counting means adding weights against it. This page's trade is the case where the room cannot carry its own weight — and the two ledgers, not the room, supply a corrected one.

1 · Before we begin

Two doors lead here, and both said what this page would have to do. P53 closed its ledger with the commission: "the customer this page's counts were made for. When a moduli space has walls, the obstruction count certifies an error term, and integration against it is P64's trade — this page's \(H^2\) is P64's starting ledger." And P54 named the stage from its own boundary: "P64's whole trade is integrating over rooms that do not quite exist, certified by exactly the obstruction bookkeeping of P53. P53's \(H^2\) is P64's starting ledger; this page's boundary is P64's stage: every virtual count is a count over a compactified census whose degenerations were named first." This page is the errand both commissions describe: the obstruction bookkeeping, run on a compactified room, turned into a count.

Here is the tension this page resolves. P54 built rooms that end — the family's escapes admitted as named boundary points — and counted on them. But a census on a room needs the room to carry a weight, and P54's honest note already confessed the limit: for the plane, its stable-map counts were honest because the room happened to be well-behaved; "for general targets the same census runs on rooms whose books do not quite balance, and repairing them is P64's virtual audit." Rooms misbehave in two directions at once. A room can undercount: the census of its points reads one where the honest number is two. A room can overcount: promised a finite census, it presents an entire curve of solutions. And at research scale the failure is structural: P54's stable-map rooms carry components of the wrong dimension, tangent books that swell, obstruction books that never close. The naive census on such a room is not merely wrong — it is undefined.

The move of this page: do not repair the room — audit it. The constraints that carved the room kept books; read them at every point; assemble the books into a corrected weight, the room's virtual class; and take every count against the correction. The room keeps its geometry, honest and misbehaving. The count keeps its integrity, certified by the audit. The two together are what the research frontier calls enumerative geometry — and the certificate is the whole point.

2 · Meet the problem

Count the solutions of two equations in two unknowns: \[ u^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}, \qquad v = 0. \] Nothing could be simpler: \(u = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\), so exactly two solutions — \((\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\) and \((-\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\). Every algebra class in the world agrees.

Now slide the constant to zero and count again: \[ u^2 = 0, \qquad v = 0. \] The room now holds one point: \((0,0)\). One equation collapsed from two crossings to a single tangency, and the census halved. But nothing about the problem changed shape — the room did not grow a family, no solution escaped to infinity (P54's failure modes are all absent); the census simply stopped telling the truth. Run the perturbation again at \(\tfrac{1}{9}\): two solutions, \((\pm\tfrac{1}{3}, 0)\). At \(\tfrac{1}{16}\): two, \((\pm\tfrac{1}{4}, 0)\). The count of the perturbed rooms is stubbornly \(2\); the room at \(\varepsilon = 0\) reports \(1\). Which number is the answer to "how many"?

Here is the second artifact, the failure in the other direction. Pose one honest census: count the points where the line \(L\) meets the line \(L\) — the same line, the doubled census. The solutions of \(y = 0\) and \(y = 0\) are every point of \(L\): a whole infinite line where a finite count was promised. A census machine handed this room would count forever. Yet the demand is meaningful — a line in the plane "ought" to meet a nearby line once — and the doubled room is simply refusing to pay it.

Three questions, and none is answerable by counting the room's points.

Q1 — what is the honest count when the room undercounts? The tangency room shows one point and hints at two. Is the honest count always the perturbed count? And is there a census that reads \(2\) off the unperturbed room itself, without reaching for a perturbation we must then argue was "generic"?

Q2 — what is the honest count when the room overcounts? The doubled line offers infinitely many points. Where would a finite answer even live — and what does the answer have to do with the boundary points P54 spent its whole page learning to add?

Q3 — what certifies any of this? Suppose we agree on a corrected count. Why believe it? A number invented to fix an embarrassment is a wish; a number is honest when some audit forces it — when changing the number would require changing books the room itself keeps.

The answers, in short. Q1: \(2\) — read three independent ways: the perturbation census, the monomial census (the room's own algebra, which keeps a two-dimensional book \(\{1, u\}\) at the tangency), and the two-ledger audit that predicts the corrected census has the right dimension to be a number at all. Q2: \(1\) — stored, as it turns out, exactly at the boundary point P54's compactification added; the affine census honestly reads \(0\), and the difference between \(0\) and \(1\) is the boundary's work. Q3: the books — the two ledgers of the obstruction theory, assembled into a class; the count is certified because every perturbation meets it, whatever the perturbation's own algebra class in school might have been. The rest of the page earns those three answers.

3 · Name the move

Virtual classes from obstruction theory. When a room is carved by constraints that do not cut cleanly — points undercounted, crowds overcounted, dimensions that disagree from component to component — do not repair the room. Audit it: at every point, read the two ledgers the constraints kept (the moves the linear audit permits; the demands it leaves unpaid), and check that the books balance to one constant verdict, the predicted dimension. Then assemble the ledgers into a corrected census-weight — the room's virtual class, of the predicted dimension — and take every count against the correction, not against the room.

In words: the room is allowed to be wrong; the count is not. A census needs a weight to add against, and a misbehaving room cannot carry its own — so build the weight from the room's audit. The audit is not an opinion: the moves ledger is a kernel, the obstruction ledger a cokernel, both computed exactly, both stable under every honest relabeling (the P16 habit, load-bearing as always). The class they assemble is the room's census with the error certified: perturb the constraints, and the perturbed census lands on the same number; read the room's own algebra, and the same number is sitting there as multiplicity. What the move promises is not that the room behaves — it promises that the count behaves, and it shows the books that force it.

Three-key diagnosis. Claim type: an enumerative claim — a number asked of a room — made honest by a certificate, in the service of a count you refuse to fudge. Problem shape: a room defined by constraints whose first-order demands are not independent at the solutions: tangencies (the room undercounts), doubled or excess demands (the room overcounts), books that differ from point to point (the room cannot be one room of one dimension). Tools at hand: P53's two ledgers (moves and obstructions, read pointwise), P54's compactified rooms (the stage the audited counts happen on), and the one genuinely new tool — the assembly step that turns ledgers into a class, which is the research content of the move and this page's §4, built in miniature and named at full size.

4 · The move at work

Exemplar 1 · The room that undercounts — one point on the books, two in the census

Take the tangency room of §2 as a machine-verified case, every number a fraction. The room: solutions of \(f_1 = u^2 = 0\) and \(f_2 = v = 0\) — the point \((0,0)\). Run the audit. The first-order demands at \((0,0)\): a motion \((\delta u, \delta v)\) must change neither constraint, so \(2u\,\delta u = 0\) and \(\delta v = 0\); at \(u = 0\) the first demand is vacuous and the ledger reads: moves \(=\) the whole \(u\)-axis, dimension \(1\). The demands left unpaid: the first constraint's row vanished (its demand \(2u\,\delta u\) carries no force at \(u = 0\)), so one full direction of demand — the first coordinate — was never collected: obstructions \(= 1\). The books balance to \(1 - 1 = 0\): the corrected census should be a number of points. P53's collinear triangle taught this exact ledger-reading — its second-order residue \(s^2\) was the same phenomenon, a quadratic term the linear audit could not see; there the residue stopped a move. Here the same quadratic term weights a count. Same books, different errand.

Read the corrected count three ways. (1) Perturbation census: nudge the constant to \(\tfrac{1}{4}\), \(\tfrac{1}{9}\), \(\tfrac{1}{16}\) — the zeros \((\pm\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\), \((\pm\tfrac{1}{3}, 0)\), \((\pm\tfrac{1}{4}, 0)\): two, every time, at every rational strength (Fig P64.1). (2) Monomial census: the room's own algebra keeps books at the tangency — the polynomials in \(u, v\) modulo the constraints \(u^2 = 0, v = 0\), and a basis of what survives is \(\{1, u\}\): dimension \(2\). The room's algebra remembers the crossing the eye forgot. (3) Consistency: the audit said the corrected census should be zero-dimensional, and both censuses return a finite number — of the same size. The virtual class of this room is \(2\cdot[\text{point}]\): the eye saw one, the books say two, and the books are right.

One honest note, and it is the move's deepest point: the first-order audit alone is blind here. The rooms \(u^2 = 0\) and \(u^3 = 0\) have identical linear books at the origin (both derivatives vanish), so the two-ledger reading cannot tell them apart — yet their corrected counts are \(2\) and \(3\) (Exercise 2 runs both machines). The virtual class is therefore not the linear audit repackaged: the assembly step reads the constraints to all orders — the full local shape, called the cone at research scale — and the ledgers certify the dimension while the cone supplies the weight. That division of labor is the pattern's signature.

Exemplar 2 · The doubled line — a whole room where one count was promised

The overcounting failure, in the other direction. In the affine plane, pose the doubled census of §2: solutions of \(y = 0\) and \(y = 0\) — every point of the line \(L\). Run the audit. At a point of \(L\), the first constraint permits motion along \(L\) and forbids motion off it: moves \(= 1\) (the line's own direction). Both constraints make the same demand — "do not leave \(L\)" — so the second constraint collects nothing new: the unpaid ledger holds exactly \(1\) demand (the duplicated demand collapses to one book, the leftover direction of the plane's two). The books balance to \(1 - 1 = 0\): the doubled census, corrected, should be a number. The room presents an infinite line instead; the books and the room disagree by exactly the duplication.

Read the corrected count by census, in both rooms. In the affine plane, perturb honestly: replace the second copy of the line by a nearby parallel line \(y = \delta\), and count meetings with \(L: y = 0\), at \(\delta = \tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{3}, \tfrac{1}{4}\): zero, every time — parallel lines never meet, and the affine doubled census honestly reads \(0\). Now apply P54's move — this is the step the whole exemplar turns on — and work in the projective plane, the plane with its boundary points added, where every line ends in the one boundary point of its own direction. The doubled census in the completed room: perturb to the parallel line \(y = \delta\) and count projective meetings: the two lines share exactly the boundary point \([1 : 0 : 0]\), the point at infinity in the \(x\)-direction — one meeting, at every \(\delta\), always the same point, and always a boundary point (Fig P64.2). The count \(1\) the doubled room refused to pay is stored at exactly the boundary P54's compactification added. The census at \(\delta \to 0\) does not drift, does not split, does not wobble: the corrected count is pinned.

The books certify the reconciliation. The excess direction — the demand the duplicated constraint left unpaid — is a one-dimensional book along the whole line \(L\), and a one-dimensional book along a completed line carries a twist: in the research vocabulary, the corrected count is the first Chern class of the excess book read along \(L\), and on the projective line that twist has degree \(1\) (on the affine line, degree \(0\) — which is exactly why the affine census reads \(0\)). No new machinery is needed to believe the numbers here: the two censuses are exact and runnable. What the vocabulary adds is the name of the phenomenon: the excess is not discarded but charged, and the charge is what the compactified boundary carries. This is the intrinsic normal cone construction in embryo — at research scale it is the assembly step of §3, due to Behrend and Fantechi, and every virtual class in the literature is built this way: the room's excess, intersected against the obstruction books, pushed down to the room.

Exemplar 2b · The count read straight from the books — when no perturbation exists

The reconciliation above worked because an honest perturbation was available: nearby parallel lines, countable meetings. The same doubled census, run where no perturbation exists, is where the books stop assisting and start deciding. A line \(L\) in the projective plane, doubled against itself: the excess book along \(L\) is its normal book, of degree \(+1\), and the corrected count is that degree — \(1\), matching the perturbation census (any nearby line meets \(L\) once). Now take the line a blow-up adds where a point was removed — the exceptional line \(E\), cousin of P54's blown-up corners, whose normal book has degree \(-1\). The perturbation census is unavailable: a negative-degree book has no honest sections, so the line admits no nearby neighbor at all. The books still return the corrected count: \(-1\). Same points, same doubled demand — and a negative count, a debt the room's geometry carries where no naive census could ever run. That is the move's deepest reach: not a bookkeeping trick for rooms that miscount, but a count that exists — and is forced — exactly where no countable census can.

Exemplar 3 · A count computed from the books alone — the \(27\) lines on a cubic surface

The censuses above run on rooms small enough to solve by hand. The research-scale version of the same move is different only in size: the room is far too large to solve in, but the books still return the count exactly. The classical receipt: how many straight lines lie on a smooth cubic surface in projective three-space?

Frame the room, count the books honestly. The room: all lines in projective three-space, one point per line. Count its parameters: a line is a 2-plane through the origin in four-space, and a generic such plane is spanned by vectors starting \((1, 0, \ldots)\) and \((0, 1, \ldots)\) — four free coordinates, a four-parameter room. The constraints: the surface's cubic equation, restricted to a candidate line, becomes a polynomial of degree \(3\) in the line's two own variables — \(3 + 1 = 4\) coefficients — and the line lies on the surface exactly when all four vanish. The books: ambient \(4\), constraints \(4\), verdict \(4 - 4 = 0\). The corrected census is a number, and for once not even a perturbation is in sight.

Run the census from the books. The four coefficients are not four independent demands; they vary with the line, assembling into one book of rank \(4\) over the room (the degree-3 coefficient book on the moving line). The corrected count is that book's top Chern class \(c_4\) — the \(c_1\) charge of the reconciliation above, four times over — integrated over the room. Give the tautological book the two formal roots \(\alpha, \beta\); the four coefficients carry the weights \(3\alpha\), \(2\alpha + \beta\), \(\alpha + 2\beta\), \(3\beta\); their product, in the elementary books \(s_1 = \alpha + \beta\) and \(s_2 = \alpha\beta\), is \[ (3\alpha)(3\beta)(2\alpha+\beta)(\alpha+2\beta) \;=\; 9\,s_2\bigl(2s_1^2 + s_2\bigr) \;=\; 18\,s_1^2 s_2 + 9\,s_2^2. \] Against the room's own classical intersection census — \(\int s_1^2 s_2 = 1\), \(\int s_2^2 = 1\), Schubert's receipts, cited here as the room's given data — the corrected count is \(18 + 9 = 27\). A smooth cubic surface carries exactly \(27\) lines. And the deeper receipt is what the count survives: as the surface degenerates — nodes, then three flat pieces, where the room of lines-on-the-surface explodes into whole two-dimensional sheets, §2's overcount returning at scale — the corrected count stays \(27\), because the books, not the room, carry it.

Exemplar 4 · The stage and the certified count — why P54's recursion was allowed to work

Now the stage both doors pointed at: the stable-map rooms of P54's Exemplar 5. A stable map is a curve plus a map to a target; the room of maps is compactified by stable degenerations; the count P54 computed by Kontsevich's recursion — \(N = 1, 1, 12, 620, 87{,}304\) for rational plane curves of degree \(1\) through \(5\) — was honest, P54 confessed, "because the compactified room is so well-behaved." This exemplar audits that confession: the books, the dimension, and the certificate.

The books of a stable-map room, by honest parameter arithmetic. A degree-\(d\) map from a line to the projective plane is a triple of degree-\(d\) polynomials in two source variables, not all vanishing: \(3(d+1)\) coefficients; divide by the target's overall scaling (\(1\)) and by the source's reparametrizations (\(3\), the projective motions of a line); add \(n\) marked points, each a free source position: the room of maps has \[ 3(d+1) - 1 - 3 + n \;=\; 3d + n - 1 \] parameters. The census P54's recursion ran needs \(3d - 1\) marked points, each pinned to a general target point — and each pin is two conditions (a plane point is two coordinates). Check the books at \(d = 1, \ldots, 5\): the unpinned room has \(3d + (3d-1) - 1 = 6d - 2\) parameters — \(4, 10, 16, 22, 28\) — and the \(3d - 1\) pins cut \(2 \cdot (3d-1) = 6d - 2\) — the same \(4, 10, 16, 22, 28\). The books balance to exactly zero at every degree: the corrected census is a number, the same number the recursion produced, degree by degree, on rooms of predicted dimension \(0\). This is the certificate's arithmetic skeleton, and every number in it is machine-checked.

The honest gap between arithmetic and geometry. The parameter count predicts; it does not construct. At research scale the construction is the obstruction theory: the moves ledger of a stable-map room is the space of first-order deformations of the map (the map wiggles, the curve bubbles, the marks slide), and the obstruction ledger is its cohomology one degree up — P53's machine at full size, run on the map rather than the framework. For genus-zero maps into a projective space, the obstruction ledger reads zero at every point — the books close everywhere, the room is "virtually smooth," and the virtual class is the honest class P54's census already integrated against. That is precisely why P54's confession held for the plane. Sketch, with the gap named: the vanishing is a splitting computation on each component of the source — the target's pulled-back directions decompose into pieces of degree \(\geq -1\), and none of those contributes a wall — and the splitting computation is a door this page does not build. The generic quintic threefold, by contrast — the room of maps into the degree-\(5\) surface in projective four-space, the Calabi–Yau case — has obstruction books that read nonzero at every point and components of too many dimensions: the naive census is not merely hard there, it is undefined. The virtual class still exists, of predicted dimension \(0\), and the counts it certifies are the famous receipts of enumerative geometry: \(2875\) lines (the classical Schubert census — earned by hand in Exercise 6), \(609{,}250\) conics (Katz's 1986 census, which the mirror-symmetry prediction later matched, as the \(317{,}206{,}375\) twisted cubics were verified by Ellingsrud and Strømme's Hilbert-scheme census) — numbers that made the virtual class the frontier's accounting standard. Finiteness per degree — the premise that each census is a number at all — is Clemens's conjecture, still open in general. Each is stated here as a sketch with its gap named: the construction of the class, the localization machines that compute it, and the mirror theorems that first predicted the counts are doors, listed in §6 and §7, not built on this page.

Exemplar 5 · Multiple covers — the ledger's honest fractions

One more room where the books alone return a verdict: a rigid curve \(C\) in a Calabi–Yau threefold, carrying normal books \(\mathcal{O}(-1) \oplus \mathcal{O}(-1)\). A degree-\(k\) cover of \(C\) is \(k\) copies of the same map — the parameter book counts \(2(k+1) - 1 - 3 = 2k - 2\) motions — and the obstruction book is the pulled-back \(\mathcal{O}(-k)^{\oplus 2}\), whose sections mirror, by Serre duality, the sections of \(\mathcal{O}(k-2)^{\oplus 2}\): there are \(2(k-1) = 2k-2\) of them. Motions balance obstructions exactly; the verdict is \(0\) for every \(k\), and the honest count of covers is the fraction \(\tfrac{1}{k^3}\) — the deck motions of the cover, all \(k\) of them, each carry weight \(\tfrac{1}{k}\). The ledger, for the first few covers: \(k = 1, 2, 3, 4\) contributes \(1, \tfrac{1}{8}, \tfrac{1}{27}, \tfrac{1}{64}\).

These fractions are not a defect to be argued away; they are the ledger telling the truth about what was counted. The divisor-wise total \(\sum_{k \ge 1} \tfrac{1}{k^3} q^k\) recombines — this is the Aspinwall–Morrison evaluation, a named door this page does not build — into integer Gopakumar–Vafa counts, one rigid curve per integer. The census machinery of this page earns the fractions; the recombination belongs to the frontier.

What this page earns by counting is the integer-scale dictionary: multiplicity censuses \(\{1,u\} \to 2\), perturbation censuses at rational strengths, the doubled-line reconciliation \(0\) affine versus \(1\) projective (with the exceptional line's \(-1\): a count no perturbation could reach), the Schubert census of \(27\) lines computed by hand, the multiple-cover ledger and its \(1/k^3\) table, and the parameter books \(6d - 2 = 2(3d-1)\) that frame P54's recursion. The research-scale theorem is Behrend and Fantechi's: a room equipped with a two-term obstruction theory — the ledgers, assembled — carries a canonical virtual class of dimension moves-minus-obstructions, and Gromov–Witten invariants, Donaldson–Thomas invariants, and the mirror-symmetry counts are all integrals against it. The named gaps, none built here: the intrinsic normal cone construction itself (the assembly step, named in Exemplar 2 where its embryo is computed by hand), the cohomology machine at full size (P33's ladder, climbed), the splitting vanishing that makes projective-space rooms virtually smooth, the localization and mirror theorems that turn the certified class into a computable number, and the Aspinwall–Morrison evaluation of the cover census (Exemplar 5's table is its receipt, not the computation). The sketch is a promise about where the certificate's engine lives; nothing on this page proves the general case.

5 · When the move fails

Every pattern page owes an honest list of failure modes. The virtual-class move fails in five distinct ways, and each has a repair, a price, and a default.

Exhibit A — the room alone does not remember its audit. The virtual class is a class of the room plus its obstruction theory, not of the room's point set. The line \(L\) cut by one constraint carries its honest weight \([L]\); the same line cut by the doubled constraint carries a corrected zero-dimensional count of mass \(1\). Same points, different books, different classes — and both are right, because they answer different censuses. A student who computes "the virtual class of the line" has not stated a question yet. Jam-mode: any claim of the form "the virtual count of this room is \(k\)" that never names the constraints or the ledgers is not a claim; the certificate is the claim.

Mitigation: state every virtual count as "the room with these books carries corrected mass \(k\)" — the books travel with the count or the count is not certified.

Price: two rooms with the same points may disagree, and the disagreement is information (Exemplar 2 is the disagreement, exhibited and reconciled), not noise.

Default: when handed a room and a number, first ask: whose books? Then check the books balance (Exhibit B) before believing the number.

Exhibit B — the books must balance to one verdict. The assembly step needs the ledger arithmetic to return the same predicted dimension at every point; books that read \(0\) on one component and \(2\) on another cannot be one class of one dimension, and no certificate exists for "the" count. The verdict can be brutally honest in the other direction too: predicted dimension below zero. The room \(u^2 = 0, \; uv = 0, \; v^2 = 0\) is a single point (the eye sees one solution); its books read moves \(= 2\), obstructions \(= 3\), verdict \(= -1\) — and the corrected count is zero: perturb by honest constants (\(u^2 = 1, uv = 2, v^2 = 1\)) and no solutions remain, at any rational strength. The monomial census still reads \(3\) (the basis \(\{1, u, v\}\) survives), so the room's algebra "remembers" a mass no honest census can collect — multiplicity without a count is exactly what a negative verdict means. The virtual class vanishes; that is a result, not a failure. Jam-mode: a room whose components carry different verdicts, or whose verdict is negative, cannot be counted as one number — component-wise censuses or an honest "zero" are the only certified answers.

Mitigation: read the verdict before the count — the audit is one exact elimination and it settles what kind of answer is even possible.

Price: "the count is zero" is a weaker receipt than "the count is \(620\)", and the census-taker must say which verdict produced it.

Default: report predicted dimension with every virtual count; a number without its verdict is un-audited.

Exhibit C — the class corrects the count, not the room. The virtual class is not a repair of the room's geometry: the tangency room still has one point, the doubled census still presents an infinite line, the stable-map rooms still carry their swollen tangent books. The move's promise is that the census is honest — not that the room became smooth, or that its points were renamed, or that the singularities were resolved. A question about the room itself — what are its components, where is it singular, what are its symmetries — needs the room, uncorrected; a question about the count needs the class. Confusing the two produces the classic misuse: quoting a virtual count as if it described the room's actual points (as if \(2\) solutions stood where the eye sees \(1\)). They do not; the corrected census is a weighting of the audit, and the room keeps the right to look however it looks. Jam-mode: any argument that starts "since the virtual dimension is \(0\), the room is finite" — the room may be infinite, impure, or worse; only the corrected count is a number.

Mitigation: keep the two vocabularies separate in every claim — the room's geometry in geometric words, the count in certified words, and never let one stand for the other.

Price: some questions about the room (its honest components, its resolutions) need other tools entirely — the virtual class will not answer them, and pretending it will is how the move gets misquoted.

Default: virtual counts answer enumerative questions only; state geometric questions as geometric questions and route them to the rooms themselves.

Exhibit D — the books must close after two ledgers. The two-term obstruction theory is a budget with exactly two lines — motions, obstructions — and the verdict is their difference. Rooms exist where the obstruction story does not fit two ledgers: obstructions in a third degree stack walls behind the walls, and the dimension formula loses its meaning before any count is attempted. On Calabi–Yau fourfolds the books do close, but the assembly that cancels the orientations is subtler (Borisov–Joyce's and Oh–Thomas's \(\sqrt{e}\) construction) — a named door, not built here. Jam-mode: reading a verdict off a room whose obstructions occupy more than two degrees, as if the budget had been issued.

Mitigation: audit which degrees the obstructions occupy before trusting any verdict; if they do not fit two ledgers, the count is not slow — it is uncertified.

Price: the honest count, where it exists at all, carries weights the simple ledger cannot express.

Default: two-term rooms for the dictionary; higher-term rooms are frontier, stated as doors.

Exhibit E — the room must end. Run the perturbation census on the open affine line: the equation \(\varepsilon x - 1 = 0\) has one solution, \(x = \tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\) — at \(\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{2}\) it sits at \(x = 2\), at \(\varepsilon = \tfrac{1}{3}\) at \(x = 3\) — and as the perturbation dies the solution walks off, until at \(\varepsilon = 0\) nothing remains to count. A census on an open room can lose its own count. The reconciliation is the move this page's neighbor taught: compactify, add the boundary the family was missing, and the zero that walked off waits there — in the projective line, the limit point \([1:0]\) collects it. Jam-mode: counting on an open room without asking where the solutions go as the perturbation dies.

Mitigation: close the room before counting — the boundary is not decoration, it is storage.

Price: the boundary brings its own books: P54's degenerate maps, and the exceptional line's \(-1\) of Exemplar 2b — counts no perturbation inside the room could reach.

Default: compactify first, then audit — the order is the move.

Scope, honestly. The audited counts on this page run on rooms small enough for exact fractions: the tangency audit (moves \(1\), obstructions \(1\), verdict \(0\)), perturbation censuses at \(\varepsilon \in \{\tfrac{1}{4}, \tfrac{1}{9}, \tfrac{1}{16}\}\) and \(\delta \in \{\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{3}, \tfrac{1}{4}\}\), monomial censuses \(\{1,u\}\) and \(\{1,u,v\}\), the doubled-line reconciliation in both affine and projective rooms with the meeting point \([1:0:0]\) pinned at every perturbation, the negative-verdict room audited and perturbed, the \(27\)-line Schubert census (its two intersection numbers classical and cited; its algebra exact), the multiple-cover ledger with its \(1/k^3\) table (the evaluation itself a named door), and the parameter books \(6d - 2 = 2(3d-1)\) checked at \(d = 1, \ldots, 5\) against P54's recursion counts. 7 · Lineage

The move's content is the oldest bookkeeping in algebra — Schubert and Severi's conservation of number, counts trusted to survive degeneration before anyone could certify why; its assembly is 1990s geometry; its working life is the frontier's accounting standard.

  • 1779 — Bézout. The multiplicity census in its first clothing: curves meet in degree-times-degree points, counted with multiplicity, tangencies included. The monomial book \(\{1, u\}\) of Exemplar 1 is Bézout's count, kept by the room's own algebra — the corrected census is older than the rooms that needed naming.
  • 1984 — Fulton. Intersection Theory: refined intersections — excess charged, multiplicities constructed from the normal cone, censuses certified even where rooms misbehave. Exemplar 2's reconciliation is Fulton's excess formula, run by hand at integer scale.
  • 1991 — Candelas, de la Ossa, Green, Parkes. Mirror symmetry's predictions: the counts of rational curves on the generic quintic, read off a physics duality before any geometry could certify them — the receivable that made the accounting standard urgent.
  • 1995 — Kontsevich. Stable maps (the idea proposed around 1992, the enumeration published in 1995): the compactified rooms whose censuses need exactly this page's certificate — P54's stage, built.
  • 1997 — Behrend–Fantechi. The intrinsic normal cone: a two-term obstruction theory assembled into a canonical virtual class. The assembly step of §3 — the move itself, named and constructed.
  • 1998 — Li–Tian; 1999 — Graber–Pandharipande. The same classes built symplectically, then made computable: localization of virtual classes turned the frontier's receipts from predictions into numbers. The certificate acquired its machines.
  • 1999 — Fukaya–Ono; Ruan. The symplectic construction, run on Kuranishi charts with honest multi-sections (and Ruan's virtual neighborhoods): the analysis-side assembly, where P65's genericity and this page's ledgers turn out to be one machine — polyfolds (Hofer, Wysocki, and Zehnder, later) are its modern house.
  • 1996 — Givental. The mirror theorems proved: the predictions of 1991 certified by the classes of 1997 (with Lian, Liu, and Yau's independent proof the same seasons). The ledger closes; the books balance — the frontier's census, paid.

None of this history is load-bearing for the pattern; the counts above are internal to this page, and the lineage is a map of doors, not a proof.

8 · Exercises

Exercise 1 ●○○ — Audit the tangency room. Take the room \(u^2 = 0, v = 0\). (a) Run the two-ledger audit at \((0,0)\): write the first-order demands, find the moves (kernel), the unpaid demands (cokernel), and the verdict. (b) Perturb to \(u^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\): find every solution exactly, and check each is transverse (its own first-order audit reads clean). (c) Run the monomial census: list a basis of the polynomials in \(u, v\) modulo the constraints, and read the corrected count.

Hint

For (a), a motion \((\delta u, \delta v)\) must keep both constraints zero to first order. For (c), a polynomial survives if no term of it is divisible by \(u^2\) or by \(v\).

Worked answer

(a) Demands: \(2u\,\delta u = 0\) and \(\delta v = 0\); at \((0,0)\) the first is vacuous, so the moves are \((\delta u, 0)\) — the \(u\)-axis, dimension \(1\). The first constraint's demand carries no force, so one direction of demand (the first coordinate) is uncollected: obstructions \(= 1\). Verdict: \(1 - 1 = 0\). (b) \(u = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\), \(v = 0\): two solutions, \((\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\) and \((-\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\). Transversality at each: the first constraint's demand is \(2u\,\delta u = \pm\delta u\), which now carries full force — together with \(\delta v = 0\) it pins the motion completely: clean books at both solutions. (c) Modulo \(u^2\) and \(v\), the surviving monomials are \(1\) and \(u\) (every \(u^a v^b\) with \(a \geq 2\) or \(b \geq 1\) is killed): basis \(\{1, u\}\), dimension \(2\). Three censuses, one number: \(2\).

Exercise 2 ●●○ — The audit is blind; the class is not. Compare the two rooms \(u^2 = 0, v = 0\) and \(u^3 = 0, v = 0\). (a) Show their first-order audits at the origin are identical: same moves, same obstructions, same verdict. (b) Run each monomial census and read the two corrected counts. (c) Run each perturbation census honestly: perturb the first room by the constant \(-\tfrac{1}{4}\) and the second by the linear term \(-\tfrac{1}{4}u\) (so the rooms read \(u^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\) and \(u^3 = \tfrac{u}{4}\)); find every solution of each, exactly, and count. (d) The two rooms share books but not counts — say precisely which part of the pattern supplies the difference.

Hint

For (b): a monomial survives modulo \(u^3\) and \(v\) if it is not divisible by either. For (c), \(u^3 = \tfrac{u}{4}\) rearranges to \(u\left(u^2 - \tfrac{1}{4}\right) = 0\).

Worked answer

(a) Both first constraints have vanishing derivative at \(u = 0\) (the derivative is \(2u\), resp. \(3u^2\)): both audits read moves \(=\) the \(u\)-axis (dimension \(1\)), obstructions \(= 1\), verdict \(0\). The linear books cannot tell the rooms apart. (b) Modulo \(u^2, v\): basis \(\{1, u\}\), count \(2\). Modulo \(u^3, v\): basis \(\{1, u, u^2\}\), count \(3\). (c) \(u^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\): \(u = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\), two solutions \((\pm\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\). \(u^3 = \tfrac{u}{4}\): \(u(u^2 - \tfrac{1}{4}) = 0\), so \(u \in \{0, \tfrac{1}{2}, -\tfrac{1}{2}\}\), three solutions \((0,0), (\pm\tfrac{1}{2}, 0)\) — and each is transverse (the demand \(3u^2 - \tfrac{1}{4}\) at \(u = 0\) is \(-\tfrac{1}{4} \neq 0\), at \(u = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\) is \(\tfrac{1}{2} \neq 0\)). (d) The difference is supplied by the assembly step: the ledgers certify the dimension (verdict \(0\) in both rooms), but the weight comes from the constraints' full local shape — the cone of Exemplar 1's honest note — which reads the quadratic term one way and the cubic term another. The audit frames the census; the class fills it.

Exercise 3 ●●● — The doubled census, both rooms. Work the line \(L: y = 0\) against a perturbed copy of itself. (a) Affine census: show the rooms \(y = 0\) and \(y = \delta\) share no solution, at \(\delta \in \{\tfrac{1}{2}, \tfrac{1}{3}, \tfrac{1}{4}\}\) and in fact at every \(\delta \neq 0\); report the corrected affine count. (b) Projective census: homogenize to \(Y = 0\) and \(Y = \delta Z\); solve exactly, and show the two lines share one projective point — the same point at every \(\delta \neq 0\). Name it. (c) Audit the doubled room: moves along \(L\), the collapsed duplicated demand, the verdict — and reconcile the two counts: where is the difference between \(0\) and \(1\) stored, and which earlier page's move put it there?

Hint

For (b), a projective point \([X : Y : Z]\) with \(Y = 0\) and \(\delta Z = 0\), \(\delta \neq 0\): what does the second equation force on \(Z\)? Then what survives?

Worked answer

(a) A common solution would need \(y = 0\) and \(y = \delta\) at once: impossible for \(\delta \neq 0\). The affine doubled census honestly reads \(0\) — at the three strengths and at every strength. (b) \(Y = 0\) and \(Y = \delta Z\) force \(Z = 0\) (as \(\delta \neq 0\)), so the point is \([X : 0 : 0] = [1 : 0 : 0]\) — the boundary point in the \(x\)-direction, the same point at every \(\delta\): the perturbed meeting never drifts. Projective count: \(1\). (c) Moves along \(L\): dimension \(1\); the two constraints make one identical demand, so the unpaid ledger holds \(1\); verdict \(1 - 1 = 0\). The reconciliation: the excess book along the completed line carries a twist of degree \(1\) — its charge is the count — while the affine line's excess book is untwisted, degree \(0\), count \(0\). The difference between the two counts is stored exactly at the boundary point \([1:0:0]\): the point P54's compactification added is where the doubled room keeps its missing count. Compactify first, then audit: the two moves are one machine.

Exercise 4 ◆ — Three claims about corrected counts, no safety net. Diagnose each — true, false, or true only under a condition — and cite the exact audit of this page that settles it. (a) "The room \(y = x^2\) meeting \(y = 0\) contains one point, so the census of that meeting is \(1\)." (b) "Two rooms with the same solution points have the same virtual class." (c) "A negative verdict means the pattern has failed: nothing can be certified about such a room."

Worked answer

(a) False, twice over. The room's audit: at the meeting point the curve's demand and the line's demand coincide (the parabola's tangent is the line) — moves \(1\), obstructions \(1\), verdict \(0\), the same books as Exemplar 1 (substitute \(u = x\), \(v = y - x^2\) to see it is the very same room). The corrected count: perturbation census (\(y = \tfrac{1}{4}\) meets the parabola at \(x = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\): two), monomial census (modulo \(y\) and \(y - x^2\), i.e. modulo \(x^2, y\): basis \(\{1, x\}\): two). The census is \(2\), not \(1\). (b) False: the certificate travels with the books, not the points. The line cut by one constraint carries its honest weight \([L]\); the same line cut by the doubled constraint carries a corrected zero-dimensional count of mass \(1\) — same points, different classes, both certified (Exhibit A). A virtual class is a class of the room plus its audit. (c) False: a negative verdict is a certified count of zero, not a failure. The room \(u^2 = uv = v^2 = 0\) reads moves \(2\), obstructions \(3\), verdict \(-1\): the corrected class vanishes, and the perturbation census confirms it — perturbed by honest constants, no solutions remain. The monomial census still reads \(3\), which is exactly the point: a remembered mass that no honest census can collect is what "verdict below zero" means. The pattern certifies "zero"; it does not fail.

Exercise 5 ◐ — When does "multiplicity equals nearby count" survive? The page used two censuses: the monomial census (the room's own remembered mass) and the perturbation census (masses an honest perturbation can actually collect). (a) For the room \(u^2 = v^2 = 0\): run the monomial census, then perturb to \(u^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\), \(v^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\) and count solutions exactly. (b) For the room \(u^2 = uv = v^2 = 0\): run the monomial census, then perturb to \(u^2 = 1\), \(uv = 2\), \(v^2 = 1\) and count. (c) Open end: state the law these two rooms suggest — when does remembered mass equal collectable mass? Test your law on a fresh room, say \(u^2 = v^3 = 0\), before reading §5's Exhibit B; then say what the law's failure at negative verdicts teaches about what a "count" is.

Hint

For (a), the perturbed room's four solutions have independent coordinates — count them per coordinate and multiply. For (b), solve the last two equations for \(uv\) and check consistency with the first two.

Worked answer

(a) Monomial census: modulo \(u^2, v^2\) the basis is \(\{1, u, v, uv\}\): mass \(4\). Perturbed: \(u = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\) and \(v = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\) independently: \((\pm\tfrac{1}{2}, \pm\tfrac{1}{2})\), four solutions, each transverse. Mass \(4\), count \(4\): the law holds. (b) Monomial census: modulo \(u^2, uv, v^2\) the basis is \(\{1, u, v\}\): mass \(3\). Perturbed: \(u = \pm 1\), \(v = \pm 1\), and \(uv = 2\) — but the four candidates give \(uv \in \{1, -1\}\), never \(2\): zero solutions. Mass \(3\), count \(0\): the law fails. (c) The law: remembered mass equals collectable mass when the books stay non-negative (verdict \(\geq 0\)) and the perturbation is honest — each failure room on this page has a negative verdict. Test: \(u^2 = v^3 = 0\): books \(2 - 2 = 0\); monomial census \(\{u^i v^j : i < 2, j < 3\}\): mass \(6\); perturb honestly to \(u^2 = \tfrac{1}{4}\), \(v^3 = \tfrac{v}{4}\), i.e. \(v(v^2 - \tfrac{1}{4}) = 0\): \(u = \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\), \(v \in \{0, \pm\tfrac{1}{2}\}\): six solutions, each transverse. Mass \(6\), count \(6\): the law holds again. The lesson of the failures: a "count" is not what the room remembers — it is what an honest perturbation can collect, certified by books that stay non-negative; remembered mass without a collectable census is a room keeping score for a game no longer being played.

Exercise 6 ◐ — Earn the \(2875\). Exemplar 3 computed the \(27\) by hand and cited two intersection numbers as given data; §4 quoted the frontier's first receipt, \(2875\) lines on the generic quintic threefold, as Schubert's classical census. Earn it. The room: lines in projective four-space, the Grassmannian \(G(2,5)\) — count its parameters the way Exemplar 3 counted \(G(2,4)\)'s. The constraint: the quintic's degree-\(5\) equation restricted to a candidate line, \(5 + 1 = 6\) coefficients, assembling into one book of rank \(6\) over the room. Given data, the room's own classical intersection census: \(\int s_1^4 s_2 = 2\), \(\int s_1^2 s_2^2 = 1\), \(\int s_2^3 = 1\). (a) Read the books and the verdict. (b) Weight the six coefficients by contact degree in the formal roots \(\alpha, \beta\) of the tautological book — \(5\alpha\), \(4\alpha + \beta\), \(3\alpha + 2\beta\), \(2\alpha + 3\beta\), \(\alpha + 4\beta\), \(5\beta\) — pair them symmetrically, and multiply out in \(s_1\) and \(s_2\). (c) Integrate against the given data and report the count.

Hint

For (b), pair first with last: \((5\alpha)(5\beta)\) is pure \(s_2\); each of the other two pairs expands in \(s_1^2\) and \(s_2\) via \(\alpha^2 + \beta^2 = s_1^2 - 2s_2\).

Worked answer

(a) A 2-plane in five-space, spanned by vectors starting \((1, 0, \ldots)\) and \((0, 1, \ldots)\): six free coordinates, a six-parameter room; the book has rank \(6\); verdict \(6 - 6 = 0\) — the corrected census is a number, and no perturbation is in sight. (b) \((5\alpha)(5\beta) = 25\,s_2\); \((4\alpha + \beta)(\alpha + 4\beta) = 4\,s_1^2 + 9\,s_2\); \((3\alpha + 2\beta)(2\alpha + 3\beta) = 6\,s_1^2 + s_2\). The product: \(25\,s_2\bigl(4\,s_1^2 + 9\,s_2\bigr)\bigl(6\,s_1^2 + s_2\bigr) = 600\,s_1^4 s_2 + 1450\,s_1^2 s_2^2 + 225\,s_2^3\). (c) Against the given data: \(600 \cdot 2 + 1450 \cdot 1 + 225 \cdot 1 = 1200 + 1450 + 225 = 2875\). The generic quintic threefold carries exactly \(2875\) lines — Schubert's census, earned — and the count survives the degenerations for the same reason the \(27\) did: the books, not the room, carry it.
