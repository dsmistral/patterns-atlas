The Patterns Atlas · P57 · the sixteenth move of tier 5

Nash–Moser fast Newton

The tangent step squares the error — until the step itself eats the smoothness it needs to exist. This is the orientation page for the repair: cap every step at a scale, grow the scale geometrically, and let the accuracy compound below the cap while the deferred scales wait their turn.

New word: smoothing operator. A smoothing operator is a machine that caps detail: \(S_\theta\) keeps what a window of size \(1/\theta\) can see and deletes the rest — concretely, on the mode dictionary this page uses, it keeps the first \(\theta\) coefficients and zeroes the tail. It is called "smoothing" because a truncated function is trivially smooth — smoother than its source, at the price of the detail it dropped. Nothing else is needed: a way to count smoothness (the rooms of §2), a machine that caps it, and — this page's real subject — the discipline of paying for each step of an iteration in the smoothness it spends.

1 · Before we begin

P22 taught the receipt that makes iteration honest: name the room the map returns into, prove the pull — each hit drawn toward the last by a fixed factor — and price the error before the first hit lands. Its sharpest case is the tangent step: near a root the error does not merely shrink, it squares, and one hit buys twice the digits of the last. P26 supplied the terrain where that receipt gets stressed: a nonlinear differential equation, where the unknown is a whole function and the step must be computed in whatever smoothness the equation leaves behind.

P22 closed with a commission, quoted verbatim: "And at the research frontier, P57's Nash–Moser fast Newton, built directly on this page." And it named the terrain honestly: "P57 faces problems where no single room works: the map loses derivatives as the iteration proceeds, and the recipe must re-smooth its own steps as it goes — Newton's method where the ground keeps changing under the tangent."

This page keeps both promises, and the keeping costs something. On P26's terrain there are equations whose tangent step destroys a unit of the very smoothness the step needs to be defined: each round of the iteration is charged rent, in derivatives, and the rent is not refundable. The receipt's room — the fixed room P22 names once and reuses forever — dissolves a floor at a time. The question the single-lens pages never had to face: what can you still certify when each step spends the very room it is computed in?

2 · The encounter

Two currencies are in play, and the second needs a dictionary. The first currency is accuracy, and it is on display in the smallest honest census.

The step at home. Take \(x^2 = 2\), start at \(x_0 = 1\), and run the tangent step \(x^+ = \tfrac12(x + 2/x)\). Every hit is a fraction; every error is exact (Fig P57.1):

The error's identity is \(e^+ = e^2/2x\): the new error is the square of the old. Digits double per hit — the receipt of §1 at full price, no caveats. This is what the step does in a room it owns.

The dictionary of rooms. The second currency is smoothness, and the census needs a way to count it. Work in modes: a function on a circle is the sequence of its coefficients \((u_n)\), and a room is a decay class — \(u\) lives in room \(s\) when its mode-\(n\) mass decays like \(n^{-(s+1)}\), one power faster than the count. Room 3 is three derivatives of smoothness. Room 0 — decay barely faster than \(1/n\) — is the last room in which the arithmetic even works: one floor below it the sums diverge, and nothing certifiable lives there. (The census above never needed the dictionary: a single number is automatically in every room.)

The rent. Now charge the same step in the second currency, on the smallest model that tells the truth. The essential nonlinearity of P26's terrain is a product with a gradient — \(u \cdot \partial u\), the transport term — and products, on the dictionary, are convolutions: \((u \cdot \partial u)_n = \sum_m u_m \, (n-m)\,u_{n-m}\). Take the honest census function \(u_n = n^{-3}\) — a room-2 sequence — and compute the product exactly at small modes (with \(u_0 = 0\), mean zero):

mode \(n\)\((u\cdot\partial u)_n\)exactroom factor \(n^3 (u\cdot\partial u)_n\)

The product decays like \(n^{-2}\), not \(n^{-3}\): one unit of smoothness gone, exactly, in one multiplication by a gradient. Its mass at mode \(n\) exceeds the room-2 allowance by the room factor — a factor that grows in proportion to \(n\) (like \(\zeta(3)\,n\) as \(n\) grows, where \(\zeta(3) = \sum_m 1/m^3 = 1.2021\)). The table's column — 8.0, 10.1, 11.5, 12.7 — is the rent, priced at census scale.

The crisis. Watch the round trip. The iterate \(u\) is room \(s\); its residual — built from products with gradients — is room \(s-1\); the correction that answers to the residual is room \(s-1\) too: one floor down per round trip, and the next trip starts from there. Room 3 goes 3 → 2 → 1 → 0, and the trip after that cannot be computed: from room \(s\), plain iteration buys exactly \(s\) round trips of quadratic accuracy and then stops — not because it fails to converge, but because there is nothing left to compute the next step with. Quadratic digits, finitely many of them, and then out of the building.

The repair. The move is almost embarrassingly small: cap the correction. Apply the step only below a scale \(\theta\) — keep the correction's first \(\theta\) modes, delete the rest. A truncated sequence has finite support, so it lives in every room at once: the capped correction cannot be evicted, because it never owed rent. The price is honesty itself: whatever the correction owed above \(\theta\) is deferred, not paid, and the deferred scales converge at the cap's pace, not the square's. And the schedule is the whole trick: grow the cap, \(\theta_k = 2^k\), so each round pays only the scales it uses, the deferrals compound to a finite total, and the smoothness bill for the entire run stays bounded. The capped step is no longer the home-room step — its error is quadratic below the cap and merely geometric above it — but it never leaves the building, and on terrain whose rooms dissolve, that is the only receipt on offer.

3 · The pattern

Here is the whole move, stated once. An equation \(\Phi(u) = 0\) whose step charges rent: the residual \(\Phi(u)\) lives one room below the iterate, so the correction does too, and plain iteration walks down the staircase of rooms until the step is undefined. The repair runs the step inside a cap, and grows the cap:

Pattern P57 · Cap the step, then grow the cap.

\[ u_{k+1} \;=\; u_k \;-\; S_{\theta_k}\, D\Phi(u_k)^{-1}\,\Phi(u_k), \qquad \theta_{k+1} = 2\,\theta_k, \]

with the two certificates that make the schedule honest — quadratic below the cap, geometric above it:

\[ \underbrace{\|e_{k+1}\|_s}_{\text{on modes} \le \theta_k} \;\le\; C\,\|e_k\|_s^{2} \;+\; \underbrace{C\,\theta_k^{-(\bar{s}-s)}\,\|e_k\|_{\bar{s}}}_{\text{the deferred scales}}, \]

and the budget line that makes the whole run affordable:

\[ \sum_k \; \theta_k^{-(\bar{s}-s)} \;=\; \sum_k \; 2^{-k(\bar{s}-s)} \;<\; \infty. \]

Or, in words: cap each step at a scale; grow the scale geometrically; let the accuracy compound below the cap while the deferred scales wait their turn. The squaring is not lost — it is rationed: below the cap the error still squares; above it the error decays at the cap's fixed discount. Each round pays rent only on the scales it actually uses, a geometric schedule makes the total rent finite, and the answer is bought twice over: once for its accuracy, once for the smoothness the buying spends. This is the pattern's name — Nash–Moser, after the two who ran capped tangent steps on terrain whose rooms dissolve — and every piece of it is inherited: the room, the pull, and the price are P22's receipt; the deferred-scales estimate is P15's squeeze run between two rooms; the doubling schedule is P42's scale ladder, with the bill paid shell by shell.

4 · Three exemplars

The pattern earns its keep three times over: the rent priced exactly, the cap's price priced exactly, and the showcase — labeled honestly as what it is.

Exemplar 1 · The rent, priced exactly

The census of §2, extended. The room factor — how many times the room-2 allowance the product exceeds at mode \(n\) — was \(8.0, 10.1, 11.5, 12.7\) at \(n = 2,3,4,5\); at \(n = 10\) it is \(18.3\), and the growth continues in proportion to \(n\) (Fig P57.2). Two exact facts hold for every \(n\): the room factor is at least \(n\) — Exercise 2's one-term bound — and asymptotically \(n^2 (u\cdot\partial u)_n\) tends to \(\zeta(3) = 1.2021\), the small-\(m\) side of the convolution sum. The rent is not an artifact of small modes: it is a rate — one derivative per product with a gradient — and the census prices it.

Exemplar 2 · The cap's price, priced exactly

On the same sequence, the cap \(S_\theta\) keeps modes \(n \le \theta\) and defers the rest. The deferred mass, counted in room 1 (decay \(n^{-2}\)), is \(\sup_{n > \theta} n^2 \cdot n^{-3} = 1/(\theta+1)\): at \(\theta = 2, 4, 8, 16\), exactly \(1/3, 1/5, 1/9, 1/17\). Each doubling of the cap halves the deferral, to within the slack of the bound \(\theta^{-1}\). This is the cap's whole price line: the deferred mass is a power of the cap — \(\theta^{-(s-s_0)}\) between rooms \(s_0\) and \(s\) — and a geometric schedule pays a geometrically shrinking bill whose total is finite (in this model, exactly one unit; Exercise 5's ledger). Pay double, get double: the price of never leaving the building.

Exemplar 3 · The showcase, honestly a sketch

Here is the terrain's flagship statement, labeled exactly as the tier-5 charter demands: sketch, not proof. Any smooth way of measuring distances on a shape — a smooth Riemannian metric — is realized by an embedding, a copy of the shape in a slightly higher-dimensional flat space, that preserves lengths exactly; and the embedding can be genuinely crumpled, with detail at every scale. The classical construction splits into two regimes. The flexible one, where the step is unproblematic, is a door this page does not open (convex integration). The rigid one is where the rent appears: the linearized problem — nudge the embedding to fix the metric error — is first-order, and its right inverse loses a derivative. The rent is priced by a four-line trick. Force the nudge \(h\) to point purely normal to the embedded copy — \(\langle \partial_i u, h \rangle = 0\) — and differentiate the constraint: the crossed term of the metric-error equation rewrites as \(\langle \partial_i u, \partial_j h \rangle = -\langle \partial_{ij}^2 u, h \rangle\), and the linearized differential equation collapses to a pointwise algebraic system, one matrix per point, invertible wherever the second derivatives' normal parts are independent — a free immersion. The price hides in plain sight: the matrix contains \(\partial_{ij}^2 u\), so every derivative demanded of the nudge is a derivative demanded of the iterate through it — rent again, charged per derivative, and the capped step is what pays it. The repair that made the theorem was the capped step: corrections below a scale, the scale doubled each round, convergence certified by the two displays of §3. This page prices the mechanism; the theorem itself stays a door with its name on it.

5 · Three exhibits

Exhibit A · The rent-free room. P26's two balances make the contrast concrete. The rod's heat equation, linearized in modes, has symbol \(n^2\): solving \(-n^2 \hat u_n = \hat f_n\) hands back \(\hat u_n = -\hat f_n/n^2\) — the correction is two derivatives smoother than the residual. The staircase reverses: each round trip goes up a floor, and the plain receipt works unmolested.

Mitigation: none needed — when the linearized solve gains derivatives, pay nothing and run the plain step; the cap would only slow it down.

Price: the rent-free diagnosis holds only while the nonlinear products stay subordinate; a quasilinear coefficient reopens the staircase (Exercise 4's diagnosis).

Default: on genuinely first-order terrain — the transport and wave rooms — default to the capped step of §3.

Exhibit B · The smoothing price. The cap is not free, and its ledger has two lines. Accuracy: below the cap the error squares; above it the error only defers, at the cap's fixed discount — the scheme's honest convergence rate is the cap's geometric rate (Exercise 6's ledger: about three-tenths of a digit per round, forever), not the home room's doubling. Budget: the certificates close only when the smoothness reserve \(\sigma = \bar{s} - s\) exceeds the rent the map charges per round trip — the reserve requirement — and the initial error must be small in the top room (the smallness hypothesis, the machine's entry fee). One shortcut from the home receipt does not survive the move: the inverse cannot be frozen at the entry point and reused. Re-inverting at the current iterate is what makes the mismatch square with the error; a frozen inverse leaves a mismatch that no longer shrinks, and a cap that grows fast enough to be useful eventually amplifies it past the squaring. That is the fast in fast Newton: the terrain is re-read every round.

Mitigation: grow the cap geometrically, so the bill shrinks geometrically and totals finitely.

Price: geometric convergence where the home room would double digits — and a reserve of smoothness sitting idle until the schedule calls for it.

Default: if the reserve cannot exceed the rent, no schedule saves the run: the terrain is beyond this page's machine, and the obstruction, not the recipe, gets the name.

Exhibit C · The resonant door. One honest floor under everything above: the caps work because the linearized solves below the cap are tame — amplification bounded, the rent a clean one derivative. On some terrain that fails: when the linearized solve divides by quantities that nearly vanish on resonant modes — \(1 - e^{2\pi i n \omega}\) for an irrational rotation \(\omega\) — the amplification spikes without bound on resonances, and no scale-blind cap tames it; the arithmetic of \(\omega\) itself must enter the schedule. The arithmetic that enters: a Diophantine rotation, whose resonances stay a fixed power \(c/|n|^{\tau}\) away from the integers, keeps the amplification polynomial, and a big enough budget still balances it; a Liouville rotation, which rationals approximate faster than every fixed power, pushes its worst resonances past every polynomial, and no fixed budget pays. The golden rotation is the borderline tenant: its worst resonances — Fibonacci modes — decay at exactly the slowest rate the Diophantine ceiling permits. That world — invariant tori, the Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser small-divisor machine — is the frontier this page faces but does not cross.

Mitigation: beyond this page: resonance-aware schedules and cancellations — the door's own machinery.

Price: crossing it means paying in number-theoretic information about \(\omega\), not just in smoothness.

Default: name the obstruction and stop: a map whose linearized solves are resonantly unbounded does not yield to scale-blind caps.

Honest scope of this page. The census rooms are a model — a decay dictionary, priced exactly but only there; the real machine runs on Sobolev-type scales, with constants this page never earns. The certificates are stated and machine-checkable in the model; the general theorem, with its tameness conditions and honest constants, is a door with its name on it. So is the showcase — the crumpled embeddings of Exemplar 3 — and so is the resonant world of Exhibit C. None of them is built here; each is a door with its name on it. What this page builds: the rent census, the cap, the doubling schedule, and the two certificates — all at census scale, all priced exactly.

6 · The doors this opens

The receipt's own doors first. P22 · Iterate to a fixed point owns the room, the pull, and the price — this page only learned to cap them. P15 · Squeeze it owns the room-to-room trade: the deferred-scales certificate is its squeeze run sideways between rooms. P42 · Dyadic decomposition owns the scale ladder the schedule climbs — the bill is paid shell by shell. And the terrain is P26 · Model it with an equation, whose balances supplied both the rent-free rod and the rented transport.

Farther doors, named only: the same frontier's wave-set is P52 · Microlocal propagation — where the roughness itself is tracked along rays, and the rooms of this page become microlocal; the ergodic side of the frontier — where the small divisors of Exhibit C live — is ergodic correspondence (P58, planned); and the flexible counterpoint to the showcase, convex integration, is a research door without a page yet. A quieter escape deserves its name too: Günther's reformulation of the showcase — trade the lossy pointwise solve for an elliptic decomposition — retires the rent altogether, and the plain receipt of §1 returns; sometimes the rent was a property of the coordinates, not the terrain. Each is a name on a door, not a tool in the kit.

7 · Lineage

  • The step (1669, 1690). The tangent iteration runs in the method of fluxions; Raphson publishes the loop as an algorithm. For two centuries nobody asks which rooms the receipt is allowed to live in.
  • The rent-free receipt (1948–49). Kantorovich proves the iteration converges in abstract rooms — Banach spaces — under room, pull, and price: P22's receipt in full generality, valid exactly where the step pays no rent.
  • The rented terrain (1954–1956). Nash's embedding theorems carry the capped step into the literature, on a problem whose linearized solves genuinely lose derivatives — the sketch of Exemplar 3, with the smoothing schedule at the machine's heart.
  • The technique abstracted (1961–1966). Moser isolates caps, schedule, and certificates as a lemma in its own right, in the small-divisor setting — and the move takes the name it carries here.
  • The machine formalized (1982). Hamilton states the general theorem on scales of spaces with tameness conditions: the rent, the reserve, and the cap as budget lines in one accounting system — the door as it stands today.

Exercise 1 · The doubling identity ●○○
(a) Show that for the step \(x^+ = \tfrac12(x + 2/x)\) on \(x^2 = 2\), the new error is exactly the square of the old: \(e^+ = e^2/2x\), where \(e = x - \sqrt{2}\). (b) From hit 1's error \(e_1 = 3/2 - \sqrt{2} \approx 8.6 \times 10^{-2}\), predict the digit counts of hits 2 and 3. (c) How many correct digits does hit 6 carry?

Hint

For (a), put \(e = x - \sqrt{2}\) into \(x^+ - \sqrt{2} = (x^2 + 2 - 2x\sqrt{2})/2x\) and recognize the numerator. For (c), each hit squares a number near \(10^{-24}\).

Worked answer

(a) \(x^+ - \sqrt{2} = (x^2 + 2 - 2x\sqrt{2})/2x = (x - \sqrt{2})^2/2x = e^2/2x\): the new error is the square of the old, divided by a factor near \(2\sqrt{2}\). (b) \(e_2 = e_1^2/(2 \cdot \tfrac{3}{2}) = (0.0858)^2/3 = 2.45 \times 10^{-3}\), two digits; \(e_3 = e_2^2/(2 \cdot \tfrac{17}{12}) = 2.1 \times 10^{-6}\), five digits — the doubling of the table. (c) \(e_4 \approx 1.6 \times 10^{-12}\) (eleven), \(e_5 = e_4^2/2.83 \approx 9.0 \times 10^{-25}\) (twenty-four), \(e_6 \approx (9 \times 10^{-25})^2/2.83 \approx 2.9 \times 10^{-49}\): forty-eight digits. P22's level-6 Pell pair checks it in whole numbers.

Exercise 2 · The rent's exact fractions ●●○
(a) Compute \((u \cdot \partial u)_4\) and \((u \cdot \partial u)_5\) exactly for \(u_n = n^{-3}\) (with \(u_0 = 0\)), and confirm \(155/864\) and \(175/1728\). (b) Compute the room factors \(n^3 (u\cdot\partial u)_n\) for \(n = 2, 3, 4, 5\), and check they grow monotonically. (c) Show \(n^3 (u\cdot\partial u)_n \ge n\) for every \(n \ge 2\): from a single term of the sum, the loss is at least one derivative.

Hint

(a) Three terms, then four; use the common denominator. (c) The term \(m = 1\) of the convolution, alone.

Worked answer

(a) \((u\cdot\partial u)_4 = \tfrac{1}{9} + \tfrac{1}{32} + \tfrac{1}{27} = \tfrac{288+81+96}{2592} = \tfrac{155}{864}\); \((u\cdot\partial u)_5 = \tfrac{1}{16} + \tfrac{1}{72} + \tfrac{1}{108} + \tfrac{1}{64} = \tfrac{108+24+16+27}{1728} = \tfrac{175}{1728}\). (b) \(8,\; 10.125,\; 11.481,\; 12.659\): monotone. (c) The \(m = 1\) term is \(u_1 \cdot (n-1)u_{n-1} = 1 \cdot (n-1)^{-2}\), so \(n^3 (u\cdot\partial u)_n \ge n^3/(n-1)^2 \ge n\) for every \(n \ge 2\). One term already prices the rent: linear growth in \(n\), one derivative of room.

Exercise 3 · The round trip ●●●
(a) Show: if \(u_n\) decays like \(n^{-(s+1)}\), then \((u\cdot\partial u)_n\) decays like \(n^{-s}\) — the product is exactly one room down, in both directions (\(\le C n^{-s}\) and \(\ge c\, n^{-s}\)). (b) Deduce the staircase: from room \(s\), the \(k\)-th correction lives in room \(s - k\). (c) Why does no finite starting room save the plain step?

Hint

(a) For the upper bound, split the convolution sum at \(m = n/2\) and bound each side by a convergent tail; for the lower bound, the \(m = 1\) term again.

Worked answer

(a) Upper: for \(m \le n/2\), \((n-m)^{-s} \le (n/2)^{-s}\) and \(\sum m^{-(s+1)}\) converges — this side is \(O(n^{-s})\); for \(m > n/2\), \(m^{-(s+1)} \le (n/2)^{-(s+1)}\) and \(\sum_{j < n/2} j^{-s}\) is at most a constant (for \(s > 1\)) or \(O(n^{1-s})\) (for \(s \le 1\)) — this side is \(O(n^{-(s+1)} \cdot n^{\max(0,1-s)})\), which is \(O(n^{-s})\) or better. Lower: the \(m = 1\) term alone is \((n-1)^{-s}\), within a constant of \(n^{-s}\). So the product decays like \(n^{-s}\) exactly: room \(s\) in, room \(s-1\) out. (b) By induction: the residual of a room-\(r\) iterate is room \(r-1\), and the correction answers to the residual. (c) Room \(s\) buys \(s\) round trips; convergence is a limit, an infinite run of trips; and below room 0 the arithmetic itself diverges (\(u_n \sim 1/n\) makes \(\sum_m m^{-1}\) grow like \(\log n\)). No finite \(s\) pays an infinite rent.

Exercise 4 · Diagnosis: the symbol census ◆
For a linear problem with mode symbol \(a_n = n^{\alpha}\) (solving \(a_n \hat u_n = \hat f_n\)), compute the correction's room relative to the residual's, for \(\alpha = 0, 1, 2\). Which \(\alpha\) are rent-free? Then the diagnosis: P26's rod balance has symbol \(n^2\); its traffic balance is first-order. Explain why the naive symbol count ("first order gains one derivative, the nonlinear term charges one — net zero") still fails on the quasilinear side, and where the census of §2 actually charges its rent.

Worked answer

\(\alpha = 2\): \(\hat u_n = \hat f_n/n^2\), the correction two rooms above the residual — rent-free, and this is the elliptic rod. \(\alpha = 1\): one room above — also rent-free as a linear problem. \(\alpha = 0\): the correction inherits the residual's room: the staircase. The naive count fails because the quasilinear coefficient \(a(u)\) puts the iterate's own gradient into the linearized operator as a multiplier: the term the census prices — a correction times \(\partial u\), a product with a gradient — is exactly §2's rent, and no linear symbol sees it. Diagnosis: read the rent from the nonlinear products, not from the linear symbol; the transport room is rented not because its symbol fails to gain, but because its linearization multiplies by \(\partial u\).

Exercise 5 · The cap's ledger ◐
On \(u_n = n^{-3}\) again, the cap keeps modes \(n \le \theta\) and defers the rest. (a) Compute the deferred room-1 mass \(\sup_{n > \theta} n^2 |u_n|\) exactly for \(\theta = 2, 4, 8, 16\), and confirm \(1/3, 1/5, 1/9, 1/17\). (b) Show it is at most \(\theta^{-1}\): the cap's price is a power of the cap. (c) The schedule's total bill \(\sum_k \theta_k^{-1}\) with \(\theta_k = 2^{k+1}\): compute it exactly.

Hint

(a) The supremum of \(1/n\) strictly past \(\theta\). (c) A geometric series with ratio \(1/2\).

Worked answer

(a) \(\sup_{n > \theta} 1/n = 1/(\theta+1)\): at \(\theta = 2, 4, 8, 16\) this is \(1/3, 1/5, 1/9, 1/17\), each a little over half the last, closing on half. (b) \(1/(\theta+1) \le 1/\theta\), and between rooms \(s_0\) and \(s\) the same argument gives \(\theta^{-(s-s_0)}\): the deferred mass is a power of the cap. (c) \(\sum_{k \ge 0} 2^{-(k+1)} = 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + \cdots = 1\): the deferred bill, in this model, is exactly one unit — finite, payable, and independent of how far the run goes. That is the whole point of the doubling schedule: the total rent is a geometric series, and geometric series converge.

Exercise 6 · Calibration: the certificate's ledger ◐
Run the two-certificate bound \(e_{k+1} \le e_k^2 + 2^{-(k+1)} E\) — with \(E\) the initial error's top-room norm — for six rounds, starting from \(e_0 = E = 10^{-1}\). (a) Fill the ledger through \(e_6\). (b) Identify the convergence law once the deferred term dominates. (c) Compare with the home room: plain doubling reaches \(10^{-12}\) in four hits, then stops existing. What does "fast" honestly mean on this terrain?

Hint

(a) \(e_1 \le 0.01 + 0.05 = 0.06\). (b) Once \(e_k^2\) is negligible, \(e_{k+1} \approx E\theta_k^{-1}\).

Worked answer

(a) \(e_1 \le 0.01 + 0.05 = 0.06\); \(e_2 \le 0.0036 + 0.025 = 0.0286\); \(e_3 \le 0.00082 + 0.0125 = 0.0133\); \(e_4 \le 0.00018 + 0.00625 = 0.0064\); \(e_5 \le 0.000041 + 0.003125 = 0.0032\); \(e_6 \le 0.00001 + 0.0015625 = 0.0016\) — sums rounded to four decimal places. (b) The deferred term dominates from round 2 on: the error halves per round — geometric convergence at exactly the cap's rate, about three-tenths of a digit per round, forever. (c) The home room buys twelve digits in four hits and then cannot take a fifth step (Exercise 3's staircase); the capped scheme buys digits slowly but always: from any starting room, to any accuracy, with a certificate. "Fast" on this terrain means: converges at all, permanently, from everywhere the equation lives — not the home room's doubling. The caps buy existence and permanence; the doubling buys digits where the room allows.

Exercise 7 · How fast should the cap grow? ◐
The doubling schedule of §3 prices the budget first: its bill is geometric and totals exactly one (Exercise 5). The speed-optimal schedule balances the two certificates instead. Model the round honestly: a cap at \(\theta\) passes the correction through the rented inverse, so the quadratic term itself carries the cap's amplification — \(e_{k+1} \le \theta^d\, e_k^2 + \theta^{-\sigma} E\), with \(d\) the rent, \(\sigma\) the reserve, and \(E\) the entry-fee norm. (a) Balance the two terms — find the cap \(\theta_k\) that makes them equal — and show the balanced round gives \(e_{k+1} \le 2 E^{d/(\sigma+d)} e_k^{\kappa}\) with \(\kappa = 2\sigma/(\sigma+d)\), superlinear exactly when \(\sigma > d\). (b) Run the idealized ledger (constants set to one) with reserve triple the rent, \(\sigma = 3d\), from \(e_0 = 10^{-2}\): the rounds go \(10^{-3}\), \(3.2 \times 10^{-5}\), \(1.8 \times 10^{-7}\), \(7.5 \times 10^{-11}\). (c) Compare with the doubling ledger of Exercise 6: which schedule converges faster, which one is priced exactly in this page's census, and what does each assume you can afford?

Hint

(a) Set \(\theta^d e_k^2 = \theta^{-\sigma} E\) and solve for \(\theta\); substitute back into either term. (c) The balanced cap grows on its own each round; the doubling ladder is fixed in advance.

Worked answer

(a) \(\theta^{\sigma+d} = E/e_k^2\), so \(\theta_k = (E/e_k^2)^{1/(\sigma+d)}\); each term then equals \(E^{d/(\sigma+d)} e_k^{2\sigma/(\sigma+d)}\), and the balanced round gives \(e_{k+1} \le 2 E^{d/(\sigma+d)} e_k^{\kappa}\) with \(\kappa = \frac{2\sigma}{\sigma+d}\), which lands in \((1,2)\) exactly when the reserve beats the rent, \(\sigma > d\): superlinear — faster than any geometric rate, slower than the home room's squaring. (b) At \(\sigma = 3d\) — reserve triple the rent — \(\kappa = 3/2\), and the chain from \(10^{-2}\) goes \(10^{-3}\), \(3.16 \times 10^{-5}\), \(1.78 \times 10^{-7}\), \(7.5 \times 10^{-11}\) — digit counts three, four and a half, six and three-quarters, ten and an eighth, each round multiplying the last by three-halves. (c) The balanced schedule is far faster — ten digits in four rounds, where Exercise 6's doubling ledger sits short of four digits after six rounds — but its cap must be recomputed from the current error each round and grows doubly exponentially, assuming the census can be re-priced on demand; the doubling ladder is fixed in advance, priced exactly at census scale, and its bill is exactly one.
