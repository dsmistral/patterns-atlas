The Patterns Atlas · P62 · the tenth move of tier 5

Sheafify on a site

Local answers that agree on every overlap and still refuse to assemble are not lying — the world hosting them is too small. The move is to build the world the data was asking for: a repair that adds no facts and invents nothing, runs the same two steps every time, and ends in a proof that every other repair passes through it. The data glues at last, because gluing is what the new world's records are.

New word: the site. A site is a world of regions together with a notion of cover: a rule saying which families of regions jointly count as covering one region — a country is covered by its provinces, a wall by three photos that overlap on the seams. A presheaf is an assignment of data to every region, with restriction maps along inclusions that compose honestly: data that restricts, with nothing forced to agree. A sheaf — P33 defined it on this book's on-ramp — is a presheaf that passes the gluing clause: sections on covering pieces that agree pairwise come from exactly one section on the covered region. The site is the honest abstraction of "space": the regions need not be subsets of anything, and the covers need not come from open sets — any world with regions and covers counts, and this page's fourth section runs the whole machine on a world of four abstract regions that has never been near a map.

1 · Before we begin

P33 taught what to do when local answers fail to glue and the data is immovable: measure the refusal. The refusals form a space — the cohomology — and its dimension counts the ways the world can say no. P60 taught the sibling move in one place: when a world's arithmetic cannot hear your questions, complete the world, and the completion is forced on you by a universal property — P31's discipline, define by the job, delivering the verdict. Both moves were honest about their limits: P33's obstruction measures but repairs nothing; P60's completion completed one monoid, in one place.

Here is the tension this page resolves. P33's surveyors stood on the mountain and refused to edit their readings — the data was the data, and its refusal was a fact about the shape. But the data is not always immovable, and it is not always honest to treat it as if it were. Sometimes the "data" is your own bookkeeping — a rule you wrote down region by region, restriction maps included, and never checked the gluing clause at all. Then the refusal is not a fact about the world; it is a defect of the hosting structure, and the honest question changes shape. Not how much does the data refuse? — P33's question, answered by cohomology — but what is the least world in which the data stops refusing? The answer must add no facts, invent no choices, and repair every presheaf at once, or it is not a move but a habit. This page builds that repair, proves it is forced, and — because no repair is free — measures exactly what it erases.

2 · Meet the problem

The almanac that cannot say "cold here, warm there". A country of two islands, East and West, with no shared shore — the two-island world, in the notation this page keeps: opens \\(E\\), \\(W\\), the empty stretch \\(\\varnothing\\), and the whole country \\(X = E \\sqcup W\\). The almanac office keeps temperature records, and its bookkeeping is the simplest honest rule imaginable: every inhabited region carries the same two official readings, \\(0\\) (cold) and \\(1\\) (warm), restriction does nothing between them — a reading of the country is a reading of any island — and the empty stretch, where nothing happens, keeps its one empty record, which every reading restricts to. Formally: \\(P(E) = P(W) = P(X) = \\{0, 1\\}\\) with \\(P(\\varnothing) = \\{\\ast\\}\\), restrictions between inhabited regions the identity, the empty record receiving everyone. This is a presheaf. It is even separated: two different world-records already differ on each island, and the empty stretch keeps exactly one record — the \\((1, 2, 2, 2)\\) shape that the census below counts among the separated.

The islands report. East: \\(0\\). West: \\(1\\). The report is consistent — matched on overlaps to the letter, and the only overlap is the empty stretch, whose one record every reading restricts to, so the matching is free. Then the question that breaks the bookkeeping: which world-record do the islands' readings come from? A world-record is an element of \\(P(X) = \\{0, 1\\}\\), and restriction is the identity — the record \\(0\\) restricts to \\(0\\) on East but to \\(0\\) on West too, and the islands' \\(1\\) is lost; the record \\(1\\) fails East the same way. No world-record witnesses the report. The data agrees everywhere it must, and the assembly still refuses.

Notice what has not happened. This is not honest disagreement: if the islands shared a shore and their readings clashed on it, the matching condition itself would fail — inconsistent data, and no repair is deserved; P33's cohomology is the honest bookkeeping for that case, counting the clash. Here the data is consistent and the world is too small: it owns records for "cold everywhere" and "warm everywhere", and no record for the perfectly real situation cold here, warm there. The distinction is the whole diagnosis, and it has a name for each failure mode: a presheaf fails gluing when a consistent local report has no global witness — the almanac's case — and fails locality when two global records agree on every piece without being equal, which needs overlap-blind bookkeeping. The repair, done by hand before any machinery: the honest world-records are the locally constant ones — a reading for East and a reading for West, \\(2 \\times 2 = 4\\) records where the office kept \\(2\\). The two constants survive among the four, as the constant records; what is new is exactly what the data demanded.

The hand repair generalizes, and the generalization is this page. But a move needs receipts, so the page runs the two-island world to exhaustion — every presheaf on it with values of size at most \\(2\\), checked against every cover. The census, machine-verified and re-verified for this page's lab: \\(458\\) raw assignments of data and restrictions; \\(190\\) of them respect composition and earn the name presheaf; and of those \\(190\\), exactly \\(5\\) are sheaves — one with singleton records everywhere, two shaped \\((1, 1, 2, 2)\\) — a one-record East and a two-record West forcing a two-record country, \\(s_X = s_E \\cdot s_W = 2\\) — and two shaped \\((1, 2, 1, 2)\\). (The shapes are recorded exactly: \\((s_\\varnothing, s_E, s_W, s_X)\\) with \\(s_X = s_E \\cdot s_W\\) — the sheaves are exactly the locally constant bookkeepings.) The failure census is just as sharp: \\(156\\) of the \\(190\\) fail instantly, on the empty stretch — the empty cover of \\(\\varnothing\\) has exactly one consistent report, so an honest world carries exactly one record on \\(\\varnothing\\), and \\(156\\) presheaves carry two; \\(25\\) pass locality — \\(5\\) of them are the sheaves, \\(20\\) fail gluing exactly as the almanac does; \\(9\\) fail locality. Every failure has a name, and the repair will have to answer all of them at once.

3 · The pattern, named

Pattern P62 — Sheafify on a site. Three steps, each a construction with a universality proof for its verdict. (1) Name the site. Say what the regions are and which families count as covers of what — opens of a space, patches of a cover, an abstract poset with a declared covering rule. Say what the data is: a set (or group, or ring) per region, restriction maps along inclusions, composition respected — a presheaf, with nothing forced to agree. Then ask the only diagnostic question: is there local data, honest on every overlap, that no global section witnesses? (2) Plus twice. Repair by decree: declare the consistent local reports of a region \\(U\\) — matching families on covers of \\(U\\), modulo agreement on every overlap — to be the new records \\(F^+(U)\\). One round forces locality; a second round forces gluing — on this page's finite worlds, two rounds always suffice, and the lab proves it by exhaustion; on general sites the same decree iterates further. The alternative route runs pointwise: take stalks (the germs, all local readings at a point), build the étalé space over them, and take its continuous sections. (3) Factor everything. The presheaf enters its repair by \\(\\eta\\), the class of the trivial report; and any map from \\(F\\) into a sheaf that respects restriction must factor through the repair, uniquely: \\(\\alpha = \\bar{\\alpha} \\circ \\eta\\). The repair is the least honest enlargement — and "least" is not praise but a theorem.

Or, in words: do not argue with the data, and do not mourn the world — build the world the data was asking for. Matching families are records-in-waiting; the repair is the bookkeeping that admits them; and the proof that nothing smaller or freer would do is the universal property, P31's discipline applied to whole spaces of data at once. The move's strength is what it refuses to promise: a repair that erases nothing is only possible for presheaves that already separated — \\(\\eta\\) can merge distinct records, and when it does, the merge is a verdict (the data disagreed in a way no honest world could host), measured in §5, not mourned.

The move applies when three keys line up, and each key names its own jam-mode:

Key 1 — the data must genuinely restrict. Regions, inclusions, and restriction maps that compose: \\(F(V \\cap W)\\) receives from both sides, and \\(F(W)\\) receives from \\(F(U)\\) whenever \\(W \\subseteq U\\). Jam-mode: tables of local data with no overlap bookkeeping at all — a database per island and no shared keys. Then "consistent" is not checkable, no matching family can be formed, and there is nothing to repair: the honest move is to build the overlap record first, which is data modeling, not this page's machine.

Key 2 — the site must be honest. The covers are part of the structure, and they decide everything: the same category of regions carries different sheaves under different covering rules — §4's diamond world is exactly this receipt, all \\(190\\) presheaves sheaves under the trivial site, only \\(47\\) under one extra cover. Jam-mode: too few covers, and every presheaf is a "sheaf" vacuously — the gluing law has nothing to bite, and the repair is the identity; too many covers (or covers the data cannot meet), and the repair erases records wholesale. Declare the covers, and let the lab check the census both ways.

Key 3 — the repair's price must be faced. \\(\\eta\\) embeds \\(F\\) into its repair exactly when \\(F\\) already separated; a presheaf that failed locality gets merged, and the merge is information destroyed: \\(F(X) = 2\\) records both restricting to the same island-readings become \\(1\\) record after repair — §5's Exhibit A. Jam-mode: applying sheafification as routine "cleanup" to data that was never separated — the repair silently equates distinct records, and the users of the data never learn what died. Run the census first: locality failures are a diagnosis, not a stain.

Box P62.A — the machine: the equalizer test, the plus construction, and the universality proof. The sheaf condition on a cover \\(U = \\bigcup_i U_i\\) is an equalizer diagram: the map \\(F(U) \\to \\prod_i F(U_i)\\), sending a section to its restrictions, must identify sections that agree everywhere (locality, injectivity) and hit exactly the matching families — those families \\((s_i)\\) whose members agree on every pairwise overlap, i.e. that equalize the two maps \\(\\prod_i F(U_i) \\rightrightarrows \\prod_{i,j} F(U_i \\cap U_j)\\). Two failures, two names: locality fails when the left map is not injective; gluing fails when a matching family is missed. The repair fixes both by decree. Define \\(F^+(U)\\) as the set of matching families \\((C, f)\\) — a cover \\(C\\) of \\(U\\), a reading \\(f_V \\in F(V)\\) per member, agreeing pairwise on overlaps — modulo the equivalence

\\[ (C, f) \\sim (D, g) \\quad\\text{iff}\\quad f_V|_{V \\cap W} \\;=\\; g_W|_{V \\cap W} \\quad\\text{for all } V \\in C,\\; W \\in D. \\]

Restrict a class to a smaller region by restricting each reading. The receipts, machine-checked over every presheaf of the two-island census (all \\(190\\), values of size at most \\(2\\)): one round makes every presheaf separated; a second round makes every one a sheaf; a third changes nothing; and the rounds census is honest — \\(5\\) presheaves needed no round at all (they were sheaves), \\(97\\) were repaired by one, \\(88\\) needed both. The empty stretch's law falls out of the same decree: the empty cover of \\(\\varnothing\\) has exactly one matching family (the empty one), so \\(F^+(\\varnothing)\\) is a one-point set, always — which is why \\(156\\) of the \\(190\\) fail instantly. The entry map \\(\\eta: F \\to F^{++}\\) sends a reading \\(s \\in F(U)\\) to the class of the trivial report \\((\\{U\\}, s)\\) — in two steps, one per round. And the universal property, the "least" made into a theorem: for every one of the \\(2{,}974\\) restriction-respecting maps from the \\(190\\) presheaves into the \\(5\\) sheaves, there exists exactly one map from the repair making the triangle commute — existence and uniqueness both verified by exhaustion over the whole census. Nothing about the two-step construction is arbitrary: any repair with this universal property is the same repair up to unique translation, which is P48's reading of P31.

Box P62.B — where the move lives. The construction is older than its name. Godement's 1958 reference book Topologie algébrique et théorie des faisceaux systematized the sheaf bookkeeping, including the canonical map to the étalé sheaf of germs — the "flèche" — which is this page's \\(\\eta\\) by another route. SGA 4 — Artin, Grothendieck, Verdier, seminars of 1963–64, published as Springer Lecture Notes in 1972–73 — moved the whole story to the site: regions and covers abstracted away from spaces, with sheafification defined and constructed there, and the sheaf category proved to be a topos. Giraud (1971) iterated the move one level up — gluing categories, not sets, which is the stack condition, quoted here as consistent with the literature. Lawvere–Tierney (early 1970s) re-founded the elementary topos with sheafification as an intrinsic closure operation — same quote status. The move's customers are everywhere data meets overlap: gauge physics glues field potentials on patches (the transition functions are matching-family data); databases reconcile local tables on shared keys; and every cohomology computation — P33's machine included — quietly sheafifies first, because cohomology is only defined on data that glues.

4 · Three encounters

Encounter 1 — the two-island census, run to exhaustion. The almanac of §2 was one presheaf; the receipt a page owes is the whole census, so the lab enumerates every presheaf on the two-island world with values of size at most \\(2\\): \\(458\\) raw assignments, \\(190\\) respecting composition, and every one checked against every cover — the world owns \\(2\\) covers of the empty stretch, \\(2\\) of each island, \\(10\\) of the country. Five sheaves, all locally constant: shapes \\((1,1,1,1)\\), \\((1,1,2,2)\\) twice, \\((1,2,1,2)\\) twice — in each, \\(s_X = s_E \\cdot s_W\\) exactly. The failures name themselves: \\(156\\) die on the empty stretch (two records where the empty cover demands one); \\(9\\) fail locality (two world-records agreeing on both islands); \\(20\\) fail gluing with locality intact — the almanac's failure, consistent reports no record witnesses. The repair census: plus once separates all \\(190\\); plus twice sheafifies all \\(190\\); \\(5\\) needed nothing, \\(97\\) one round, \\(88\\) two. And the constant presheaf — the almanac itself, \\((1,2,2,2)\\) — repairs to \\((1,2,2,4)\\): two constant readings grow to four locally constant records, \\(\\eta\\) embedding the constants as \\((0,0)\\) and \\((1,1)\\). Universality, the last gate: every restriction-respecting map from any of the \\(190\\) into any of the \\(5\\) sheaves — \\(2{,}974\\) of them — extends along \\(\\eta\\), uniquely, with not one exception. The repair is not a choice; it is the only answer the census permits.

Encounter 2 — the diamond world: same data, two sites. Regions need not sit in a space: take four abstract regions \\(a, b, c, m\\) with meets — \\(a \\wedge b = b\\), \\(a \\wedge c = c\\), \\(b \\wedge c = m\\), and \\(m\\) beneath all — the diamond. The census of set-valued presheaves with values of size at most \\(2\\) is again \\(190\\) — and that is law, not coincidence: the diamond is the two-island world's own poset in different clothes, \\(m\\) the empty stretch, \\(b\\) and \\(c\\) the islands, \\(a\\) the country, so the raw census was always going to repeat; what changes is the site. First topology, the trivial site \\(J_0\\): the only cover of a region is itself, so locality and gluing are vacuous — all \\(190\\) are sheaves. Second topology, \\(J_1\\): declare one non-trivial cover, \\(b\\) and \\(c\\) jointly covering \\(a\\). The sheaf census collapses: \\(47\\) sheaves, \\(84\\) separated-but-not-gluing, \\(59\\) failing locality outright — one declared cover destroyed \\(143\\) verdicts. The repair census is different too: on the diamond, one round of plus always suffices — \\(47\\) sheaves outright, \\(143\\) repaired by a single round, none needing two — because the only non-trivial cover is at the top; the two-island world needed two rounds because its covers live below the top too, and a first-round repair at \\(X\\) can create fresh gluing work at the islands. Two receipts, small enough to hold: the presheaf with one record at \\(a\\), two at \\(b\\), two at \\(c\\), one at \\(m\\) — every pair of island-readings consistent, since \\(m\\) carries a single record — glues nothing at \\(a\\); its repair carries \\(4\\) records at \\(a\\), the three patch-reports the trivial bookkeeping had no home for. And the mirror: two records at \\(a\\), one at \\(b\\), one at \\(c\\), both restricting to the same readings — the repair merges them into one. The site decided everything; the data never changed.

Encounter 3 — the étalé route: repair by moving to the points. The plus construction repairs by decree at every region; the étalé route repairs at every point. The stalk at a point \\(p\\) is the germs of all readings at all neighborhoods of \\(p\\), two readings equal when they agree on some smaller neighborhood — the almanac's East-stalk is the set of possible East-readings, because East is its own smallest neighborhood. String the stalks over their points and a section is a continuous choice of germ, one per point; the sheaf of sections over each region \\(U\\) is exactly the honest records on \\(U\\). On the two-island world the receipt is clean: stalks are \\(F(E)\\) and \\(F(W)\\), and a continuous section over the country is any pair of germs — \\(s_E \\cdot s_W\\) of them, machine-checked for all \\(190\\) presheaves, which is \\(F^{++}(X)\\) exactly. The subtle world is the Sierpiński one: two points \\(o\\) (open) and \\(c\\) (closed), opens \\(\\varnothing, \\{o\\}, X\\). The closed point's only neighborhood is \\(X\\) itself, so its stalk is \\(F(X)\\), and continuity forces any section over \\(X\\) to read \\(\\sigma(c)\\)'s germ at \\(o\\): sections over \\(X\\) biject with \\(F(X)\\). The census: \\(36\\) presheaves with values of size at most \\(2\\); the \\(28\\) carrying two records on the empty stretch fail the empty cover instantly; the \\(8\\) honest ones are all sheaves — every cover of \\(X\\) contains \\(X\\) itself, the closed point's doing, so the gluing law has nothing to bite. A whole page of §2-style failures, erased by one closed point: the site, again, deciding everything.

Three worlds — a country, a diamond, a two-point space — and one job description behind all of them: name the site, plus twice, factor everything. The pattern box said it as a construction; the encounters say it as a census: the site decides which data is honest, the repair is forced by the numbers, and the universal property is checked, not asserted. The remaining question is what the repair costs — which is §5's subject, and the honest half of this move.

SKETCH — the research theorems behind this page: on general sites, the plus construction can need a transfinite iteration to converge, and for large sites the sheaf category may only exist under set-theoretic smallness conditions (a universe); the adjoint-functor-theorem route to sheafification, and Giraud's stack gluing one level up, are quoted as consistent with the literature, not source-verified. The parts this page deliberately does not build: the transfinite construction, the universe hypotheses, the topos-theoretic closure operator of Lawvere–Tierney. What this page verified directly: the two-island census to exhaustion (\\(458 \\to 190 \\to 5\\) sheaves, with the full failure census), the two-round convergence theorem on that world, the universal property over all \\(2{,}974\\) maps of the finite model, the diamond world's two topologies (\\(190\\) sheaves under \\(J_0\\), \\(47\\) under \\(J_1\\), one round always sufficing), and the Sierpiński census (\\(36\\) presheaves, \\(8\\) sheaves, sections over \\(X\\) in bijection with \\(F(X)\\)).

5 · Exhibits: when the repair forgets

Every pattern earns its keep by failing. Three exhibits — a repair that merges records the data never distinguished locally, a demand so small it is easy to miss, and an iteration that stops at two here but is not promised to — with the mitigation, the price, and the default.

Exhibit A — the repair that merges. A country office keeps one record on the empty stretch, one on each island, and two on the country — the presheaf \\((1,1,1,2)\\) — with both country-records restricting to the same island-reading. The office insists the two records are different: different files, different fortunes, different theories. The repair answers: not locally. Two records of a sheaf are equal exactly when they agree on every cover — and these agree everywhere they restrict. The repair \\((1,1,1,2) \\to (1,1,1,1)\\) merges them, machine-checked: the two become one, Fig P62.3. Universality delivers the same verdict independently, and this is the receipt to remember: any restriction-respecting map from this presheaf into any sheaf must already identify the two records — their images agree on the islands, and a sheaf's records are determined by their local behavior — so no honest enlargement could ever have kept them apart. The merge is not the construction's clumsiness; it is the data's own verdict about itself.

Mitigation: run locality before repairing — it is a finite check on covers (two records of \\(F(U)\\) agreeing on every member of some cover of \\(U\\)), and in the two-island census it catches \\(9\\) presheaves with \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) honest, before any machinery runs.

Price: the merge is information destroyed, and if the merged records carried meaning — two theories, two claims, two owners — that meaning was never local, and the repair is the wrong tool: the honest fix is better data, not a bigger world.

Default: treat a locality failure as a diagnosis, not a stain. The data disagreed with its own restrictions; find out which side was lying before building anything.

Exhibit B — the empty set's demand. The smallest cover in the world is the empty family covering the empty region, and it makes the loudest demand: exactly one matching family exists on it (the empty one), so honest records on \\(\\varnothing\\) are exactly one — \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) must be a single point, always. In the two-island census, \\(156\\) of \\(190\\) presheaves die on this alone: two records on the empty stretch, a bookkeeping fiction the empty cover does not recognize. The demand is not pedantry; it is the gluing law's anchor. Gluing over the empty cover says every element of \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) comes from the (unique) empty family; locality says at most one does; together: exactly one. And the anchor generalizes — gluing over disconnected covers is what forces records on a region to be families of records on its pieces, which is the entire construction.

Mitigation: normalize first — check \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) before anything else; it is the cheapest check in the machine, and in this page's census it resolves \\(156\\) verdicts instantly.

Price: none. The demand is free, and presheaves that fail it were never honest candidates.

Default: the empty-set law is the pre-flight check of the whole move; the Sierpiński world of §4 shows its reach — there, even with every cover of the country containing the country itself, the \\(28\\) presheaves carrying two empty-stretch records still fail, all of them, on this one demand.

Exhibit C — the iteration that stops here. On the two-island world, two rounds of plus provably suffice: the lab checks a third round against the second for all \\(190\\) presheaves and finds no change — sizes, restrictions, everything stable. On the diamond world with one declared cover, one round suffices, checked the same way. It is tempting to read "plus twice" as a theorem. It is not. On general sites the plus construction is iterated — finitely where fortune allows, transfinitely where it does not, taking a merged record at limit stages — and the proof of convergence is part of the general construction, not a remark. Worse, on sites too large to be sets of records in a fixed universe, the sheaf category itself can require set-theoretic hygiene (a Grothendieck universe) to exist. This page's receipts are exhaustion receipts of finite worlds, honestly labeled as such; the general theorem is named, not proved, and the scope box holds the debt.

Mitigation: on finite sites, iterate until stable and prove stability by exhaustion — the lab's third-round check is the receipt; never claim a round count you have not checked or cited.

Price: on general sites there is no finite census to run, and the honest statement carries its hypotheses (smallness, universes) openly.

Default: "plus twice" is this page's receipt, not the world's theorem. Say which world you are in, and check.

6 · Doors out

  • Door — straighten and unstraighten (P61): the nearest door, and this page's footer. This page built one repair and read it two ways — matching families at every region, germs at every point — and the two readings were the same sheaf. P61 makes that duality a discipline: compute in the straightened world, interpret in the curved one, and the invariant is what survives the round trip. The étalé route of §4 is P61's method arriving early.
  • Door — the sheaf cohomology bridge (P33): the prerequisite, read backward. P33 measured the refusal — data immovable, the obstruction a fact about the shape; this page repaired the world — data yours, the refusal a defect of the hosting. The two moves are one toolkit: measure first; repair only what was bookkeeping to begin with. A cohomology class on a repaired presheaf is a confession that the repair was the wrong question.
  • Door — group completion (P60): the tier sibling. P60 completed one monoid, in one place, by a universal property; this page ran the same discipline point by point over a whole site. The \\(\\eta\\) that embeds and the \\(\\eta\\) that merges are the same arrow, honestly priced on both pages — and P60's warning returns here louder: repairs that erase are verdicts.
  • Door — the diagram chase (P47): the consumer. Cohomology is computed from resolutions, resolutions are built by sheafified functors, and the exactness that makes a chase run is sheaf-exactness — this page's repairs are what P47's squares are drawn in. The door is one move away and already half-open.
  • Door — representable functor (P48): the universal property, one language up. "Every presheaf maps into sheaves, restriction-respectingly" assembles into a functor, and the repair represents it: sheafification is the left adjoint to the forgetful functor from sheaves to presheaves. 7 · Lineage

  • 1946 — Leray; 1950–51 — Cartan; 1955 — Serre; 1957 — Grothendieck. The sheaf's own history, pinned on P33's page and quoted here as cross-page pins: Leray's deliberately abstract machine, born in a prison camp and published in 1946; Cartan's seminar rebuilding it for complex analysis; Serre's FAC running it on algebraic varieties; Grothendieck's Tôhoku paper re-founding the bookkeeping. Sheafification enters this story quietly: a sheaf, once named, immediately raises this page's question — what is the nearest sheaf to data that is not one yet?
  • 1958 — Godement. Topologie algébrique et théorie des faisceaux (Hermann, 1958; web-verified for this page) wrote the young theory down as a reference, and the canonical map — the flèche — from a presheaf to the sheaf of germs of its sections is this page's \\(\\eta\\) by the étalé route: Encounter 3's stalks, germs, and continuous sections, published three years after Tôhoku as bookkeeping. The plus construction of Box P62.A is the decree version of the same repair; both produce the left adjoint, and this page checks their receipt counts against each other on every presheaf of the census.
  • 1963–64 (published 1972–73) — Artin, Grothendieck, Verdier. SGA 4 moved the whole story to the site — regions and covers abstracted away from any underlying space — and defined sheaves, and sheafification, on it; the seminar ran in 1963–64 and was published as Springer's Lecture Notes in Mathematics 269, 270, and 305 in 1972–73 (publication data web-verified for this page). The site is why this page's diamond world is lawful: covers are declared structure, not geography, and the sheaf census changes when the declaration does — SGA 4's world, run here at desk size. Down the same decade and the next two doors: Giraud's stacks (1971) gluing categories with the same law this page glues sets, and the Lawvere–Tierney elementary topos with sheafification as an intrinsic closure — both quoted as literature-consistent, not source-verified, per the scope box.

None of this history is load-bearing for the pattern; the receipts above are internal to this page, and the lineage is a map of doors, not a proof.

Exercise 1 ●○○ — the empty law and the almanac, by hand. (a) On the two-island world, list all covers of the empty stretch — including the empty family — and explain why exactly one matching family exists on the empty cover; conclude that \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) must carry exactly one record, for any sheaf \\(F\\). (b) The almanac presheaf \\(P = (1,2,2,2)\\) with identity restrictions: check it is separated — two different country-records differ on the cover \\(\\{E, W\\}\\) — and then exhibit the matching family on that cover that no country-record witnesses. (c) Compute \\(P^+(X)\\) by hand: list the matching families on the cover \\(\\{E,W\\}\\), quotient by Box P62.A's equivalence, and verify the count is \\(4\\).

Hint

For (a), a matching family on a cover \\(C\\) assigns a record to each member, agreeing on pairwise overlaps — with no members, there is one such assignment, the empty one. For (b), the report \\((0 \\text{ on } E,\\; 1 \\text{ on } W)\\) agrees on the (empty) overlap. For (c), two families are equivalent when their readings agree on every overlap of a member of one with a member of the other — most of the overlap checks are vacuous here, but the trivial family \\((\\{X\\}, s)\\) is equivalent to \\((\\{E,W\\}, (s,s))\\), and that is where the count drops.

Worked answer

(a) The covers of \\(\\varnothing\\) are the empty family and \\(\\{\\varnothing\\}\\). On the empty family there is exactly one assignment (none at all), it is vacuously matching, and gluing demands every record of \\(F(\\varnothing)\\) witness it while locality demands no two do: \\(|F(\\varnothing)| = 1\\). (b) The records \\(0, 1 \\in P(X)\\) restrict to \\((0,0)\\) and \\((1,1)\\) on the cover — different tuples, so separated. The report \\((0 \\text{ on } E, 1 \\text{ on } W)\\) is matching — \\(E \\cap W = \\varnothing\\) — and a witness \\(s \\in P(X)\\) would need \\(s|_E = 0\\) and \\(s|_W = 1\\), but restriction is the identity: no such \\(s\\). Gluing fails. (c) Matching families on \\(\\{E,W\\}\\): all \\(2 \\times 2 = 4\\) pairs. Equivalences: the trivial family \\((\\{X\\}, 0)\\) restricts to \\((0,0)\\), so it merges with the pair \\((0,0)\\); likewise \\((\\{X\\},1)\\) with \\((1,1)\\); the pairs \\((0,1)\\) and \\((1,0)\\) match no trivial family. Classes: \\(\\{\\text{const } 0\\}\\), \\(\\{\\text{const } 1\\}\\), \\((0,1)\\), \\((1,0)\\) — four records, which is \\(P^+(X)\\), and the second round changes nothing: \\(P^+ = (1,2,2,4)\\) is already the repair.

Hint

(b) Families on the trivial cover: \\(1\\); on \\(\\{b,c\\}\\): \\(4\\); the trivial family is equivalent to the pair it restricts to, so \\(|F^+(a)| = 1 + 4 - 1 = 4\\) — the receipt \\((1,2,2,1) \\to (4,2,2,1)\\). Exercise 4 ◆ — the consultant's two offices.

Two offices keep identical bookkeeping: three regions — a point-office \\(\\{o\\}\\), the whole country \\(X\\), and the empty stretch — with records \\(F(\\varnothing) = \\{\\star\\}\\), \\(F(\\{o\\}) = \\{0, 1\\}\\), \\(F(X) = \\{0, 1\\}\\), and restriction \\(X \\to \\{o\\}\\) the identity. Worked answer

The records do not decide; the site does, and the two offices must be auditing under two covering rules — this page's Key 2, arriving as a consulting report. Office A's world: the closed-point world of §4's Encounter 3, where the point \\(c\\) beside \\(o\\) has \\(X\\) as its only neighborhood, so every cover of \\(X\\) contains \\(X\\) itself — the gluing law has nothing to bite, the empty stretch is honest, and the bookkeeping is a sheaf (one of the \\(8\\)). Office B's world: the two-island shape in disguise — a second open region inside \\(X\\) beside \\(\\{o\\}\\), with \\(X\\) covered by two proper opens — where the two country-records \\(0\\) and \\(1\\) restrict to the same readings on both members of that cover: locality fails, and the repair merges them (Exhibit A's verdict). Worked answer
