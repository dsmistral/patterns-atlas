The Patterns Atlas · P54 · the ninth move of tier 5

Compactify by stable degenerations

When marked points collide or a smooth surface pinches its neck, do not let them crash — zoom in at the collision rate to sprout a new component, and keep only trees that have no continuous symmetries.

Projective line \(\mathbb{P}^1\), Möbius transformations, and moduli spaces. The projective line \(\mathbb{P}^1 = \mathbb{C} \cup \{\infty\}\) is the complex plane closed up into a sphere by adding one point at infinity. A Möbius transformation is a fractional linear map \(z \mapsto \frac{az+b}{cz+d}\) with \(ad - bc \ne 0\); given any three distinct points \((z_1, z_2, z_3)\) on \(\mathbb{P}^1\), there is a unique Möbius transformation that places them at \((0, 1, \infty)\). A moduli space \(\mathcal{M}_{0,n}\) is the parameter space of all configurations of \(n\) distinct labeled points \((z_1, \dots, z_n)\) on \(\mathbb{P}^1\), where two configurations count as the same if a Möbius transformation carries one to the other.

1 · Before we begin

P21 taught us why compactness is the gold standard of existence arguments: on the 1-metre target strip, halving the interval guarantees that sixteen shots accumulate at a limit point inside the strip. P33 showed us how local patches glue across overlaps to define global invariants, and P53 counted the infinitesimal knob-turns \(H^1\) and obstructions \(H^2\) of a smooth family.

Here is the tension that P53 left on its doorstep. Suppose each point in our parameter space represents a smooth geometric object — say, five distinct beacons on a sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1\), or a smooth surface of genus \(g\). As long as the beacons stay apart and the surface stays smooth, we can move our knobs freely. But if we follow a sequence where two or three beacons drift together, the limit configuration has fewer distinct points and falls right out of the moduli space! Because the space of smooth configurations is open (non-compact), sequences can escape through the missing boundary, and intersection counts or integrals over the parameter space fail to be well-defined. How do we seal the missing boundary so that every degenerating sequence lands on a unique, well-behaved limit object?

2 · Meet the problem: three beacons colliding at two different speeds

Suppose we place five distinct labeled beacons \((z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4, z_5)\) on the projective line \(\mathbb{P}^1\). Using our canonical-form reflex (P05), we spend the unique Möbius transformation on the first three beacons to lock

\[z_1 = 0, \qquad z_2 = 1, \qquad z_3 = \infty.\]

Once \((z_1, z_2, z_3) = (0, 1, \infty)\) are pinned down, the entire configuration is recorded by the remaining two coordinates \((z_4, z_5)\), subject to the rule that no two beacons may occupy the same spot: \(z_4, z_5 \notin \{0, 1, \infty\}\) and \(z_4 \ne z_5\). Now watch what happens along the one-parameter family parameterized by \(\varepsilon \to 0^+\) with a fixed ratio \(c \notin \{0, 1\}\):

\[z_1(\varepsilon) = 0, \quad z_2(\varepsilon) = 1, \quad z_3(\varepsilon) = \infty, \quad z_4(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon, \quad z_5(\varepsilon) = c\,\varepsilon.\]

Take the naive limit \(\varepsilon \to 0\) in the original coordinate \(z\): both \(z_4(\varepsilon) \to 0\) and \(z_5(\varepsilon) \to 0\), so beacons \(1, 4,\) and \(5\) crash into a single pile at \(z = 0\)! Worse still, the naive limit \((0, 1, \infty, 0, 0)\) has completely forgotten the number \(c\): whether \(z_5\) approached \(z_1\) at half the distance of \(z_4\) (\(c = \frac{1}{2}\)) or ten times the distance (\(c = 10\)), the naive crash looks identical.

Look at the second row of the table. Before \(\varepsilon\) reaches zero, point a microscope at \(z=0\) with magnification \(1/\varepsilon\), defining a rescaled coordinate \(w = z/\varepsilon\) (P56). In the microscope coordinate \(w\), beacon \(1\) sits at \(w_1 = 0\), beacon \(4\) sits at \(w_4 = \varepsilon/\varepsilon = 1\), and beacon \(5\) sits at \(w_5 = c\varepsilon/\varepsilon = c\). As \(\varepsilon \to 0\), these three beacons never collide at all on the \(w\)-sphere! Meanwhile, the distant beacons \(z_2 = 1\) and \(z_3 = \infty\) have \(w\)-coordinates \(1/\varepsilon \to \infty\) and \(\infty\), so from the microscope's point of view they merge into the point at \(w = \infty\).

Glue the two views together at the point where the microscope attaches to the macroscope — setting \(z=0\) on the original sphere equal to \(w=\infty\) on the zoomed sphere, as drawn in Fig P54.1. In the limit \(\varepsilon = 0\), our single sphere has become a nodal curve with two smooth \(\mathbb{P}^1\) components joined at a node \(N\):

  • The macro component \(\mathbb{P}^1_z\) carries beacons \(z_2 = 1\), \(z_3 = \infty\), and the attachment node \(N\) at \(z = 0\) — three special points in total, which pins down its Möbius coordinates completely.
  • The bubble component \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\) carries beacons \(w_1 = 0\), \(w_4 = 1\), \(w_5 = c\), and the attachment node \(N\) at \(w = \infty\) — four special points in total, preserving the exact relative ratio \(c \in \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}\)!
Nothing crashed, no information was lost, and if \(c\) itself later drifts toward \(0, 1,\) or \(\infty\), a second microscope sprouts a third sphere so the three spheres form a chain.

3 · Name the move

Why didn't we sprout a new bubble sphere when only one beacon sat by itself? Suppose a bubble sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\) carried only one beacon \(w_1 = 0\) and one attachment node \(N\) at \(w = \infty\) — just two special points in total. Then every rescaling \(w \mapsto \lambda w\) with \(\lambda \in \mathbb{C}^*\) would fix both \(0\) and \(\infty\), giving an infinite continuous group of automorphisms that all represent the exact same geometric object! If you allow components with infinite automorphism groups into your boundary, the quotient space fails to be Hausdorff (distinct boundary points cannot be separated by open neighborhoods).

Nodal curve, special points, and the stability condition \(2g - 2 + n > 0\). A nodal curve is a connected union of smooth curves (components) meeting only at transverse double points called nodes (locally modeled by \(zw = 0\) as \(\varepsilon \to 0\) in \(zw = \varepsilon\)). On any single component \(C_i\) of genus \(g_i\), its special points are the marked beacons landing on \(C_i\) together with the half-nodes where \(C_i\) meets other components (or itself). A nodal curve is Deligne–Mumford stable if it has no marked points at nodes and every component \(C_i\) has only finitely many automorphisms fixing its special points — which holds if and only if \(2g_i - 2 + n_i > 0\) on every component (at least \(n_i \ge 3\) special points when \(g_i = 0\), and at least \(n_i \ge 1\) when \(g_i = 1\)).

Pattern P54 — Compactify by stable degenerations. When a moduli space \(\mathcal{M}\) of smooth geometric objects (marked curves, algebraic varieties, or holomorphic maps) is non-compact because marked points can collide or necks can pinch, do not collapse colliding configurations to a single singular point. Instead, enlarge \(\mathcal{M}\) to \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}\) by adjoining nodal trees of components formed by rescaling each collision or neck-pinch at its natural separation rate, subject to the stability condition:

\[\bigl|\operatorname{Aut}(C, p_1, \dots, p_n)\bigr| < \infty \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad 2g_i - 2 + n_i > 0 \;\;\text{on every irreducible component } C_i.\]

The resulting Deligne–Mumford–Kontsevich compactification \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}\) is compact (P21), Hausdorff, and stratified by normal-crossing boundary divisors whose strata are products of lower-dimensional moduli spaces glued at nodes (P33).

Or, in words: Never let two marked points touch or a neck pinch into nothing. Whenever \(k \ge 2\) points approach one another on a sphere, zoom in at their separation speed to sprout a new bubble sphere at the collision point, and enforce the three-anchor rule (\(n_i \ge 3\) special points per sphere) so every bubble is rigidly pinned down.

Box P54.A — The three rules of the stability audit.

  1. No collisions on any component: Marked points \(p_1, \dots, p_n\) must remain pairwise distinct and may never sit on a node \(N\). Whenever \(k \ge 2\) points try to collide at \(z_0\), replace \(z_0\) by a node \(N\) and transfer the \(k\) colliding points onto a new \(\mathbb{P}^1\) bubble.
  2. The three-anchor floor on genus \(0\) (\(2g_i - 2 + n_i > 0\)): Count special points \(n_i = (\text{marked points on } C_i) + (\text{half-nodes on } C_i)\). If \(g_i = 0\), require \(n_i \ge 3\); if \(g_i = 1\), require \(n_i \ge 1\); if \(g_i \ge 2\), require \(n_i \ge 0\). (For stable maps \(u : C \to X\), unstable components with \(2g_i - 2 + n_i \le 0\) are permitted only if \(\int_{C_i} u^*\omega > 0\), i.e., the map is non-constant on \(C_i\).)
  3. Contract unstable ghosts: If any component violates Rule 2 (for instance, a genus-0 bridge with \(n_i = 2\) nodes and \(0\) beacons, or a cap with \(n_i = 1\) node and \(1\) beacon), collapse that component to a point so its special points merge into a single node or marked point.

4 · Worked exemplars

Exemplar 1 · The thrice-punctured sphere \(\mathcal{M}_{0,4}\) and its three nodal boundary trees

Take four distinct labeled points \((z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4)\) on \(\mathbb{P}^1\). Using P05 (Reduce to canonical form), apply the unique Möbius map that sends \((z_1, z_2, z_3) \mapsto (0, 1, \infty)\). That map moves the fourth point to the cross-ratio

\[\lambda = \frac{(z_4 - z_1)(z_2 - z_3)}{(z_4 - z_3)(z_2 - z_1)} \in \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}.\]

Thus the open moduli space is the thrice-punctured sphere \(\mathcal{M}_{0,4} \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}\). What lives at the three missing punctures \(\lambda \in \{0, 1, \infty\}\)?

  • As \(\lambda \to 0\) (\(z_4 \to z_1 = 0\)): Zoom in around \(z = 0\) with microscope \(w = z/\lambda\). On the bubble sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\), \(z_1\) stays at \(w=0\), \(z_4\) stays at \(w=1\), and the node \(N\) sits at \(w=\infty\); on the macro sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_z\), the node \(N\) sits at \(z=0\) alongside \(z_2=1\) and \(z_3=\infty\). Each component carries \(2\) marked points plus \(1\) half-node (\(n_i = 3\), rigid!), forming the balanced tree \((14 \mid 23)\).
  • As \(\lambda \to 1\) (\(z_4 \to z_2 = 1\)): Zoom in around \(z = 1\) with \(w = (z-1)/(\lambda-1)\). Beacons \(\{2, 4\}\) land on the bubble sphere while \(\{1, 3\}\) remain on the macro sphere, forming the balanced tree \((24 \mid 13)\).
  • As \(\lambda \to \infty\) (\(z_4 \to z_3 = \infty\)): Zoom in around \(z = \infty\) with \(w = \lambda/z\). Beacons \(\{3, 4\}\) land on the bubble sphere while \(\{1, 2\}\) remain on the macro sphere, forming the balanced tree \((34 \mid 12)\).

Adjoining those three two-sphere nodal curves fills in the three punctures cleanly: \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4} \cong \mathbb{P}^1\). Notice a remarkable combinatorial dividend: because any two points on \(\mathbb{P}^1\) are homologous in \(H_0(\mathbb{P}^1; \mathbb{Z})\), the three boundary trees represent the same homology class \([(14 \mid 23)] = [(24 \mid 13)] = [(34 \mid 12)]\).

Exemplar 2 · Blowing up the three triple-collision corners in \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}\) (the Petersen graph)

Return to our Encounter with five beacons \((z_1, \dots, z_5)\), where \((z_1, z_2, z_3) = (0, 1, \infty)\) and the free pair \((z_4, z_5)\) moves inside the product of two spheres \(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1\). Why isn't \(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1\) already the right compactification of \(\mathcal{M}_{0,5}\)? Count the prohibited collision lines inside \(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1\):

  • Three vertical lines where \(z_4\) hits a fixed beacon: \(z_4 = 0\) (collision \(\{1,4\}\)), \(z_4 = 1\) (\(\{2,4\}\)), \(z_4 = \infty\) (\(\{3,4\}\)).
  • Three horizontal lines where \(z_5\) hits a fixed beacon: \(z_5 = 0\) (\(\{1,5\}\)), \(z_5 = 1\) (\(\{2,5\}\)), \(z_5 = \infty\) (\(\{3,5\}\)).
  • One diagonal line where the two moving beacons hit each other: \(z_4 = z_5\) (\(\{4,5\}\)).

That is only \(3 + 3 + 1 = 7\) collision lines — yet five beacons treat all five labels symmetrically (P08), so there must be \(\binom{5}{2} = 10\) boundary divisors \(D_{ij}\) (one for each pair \(\{i,j\} \subset \{1,2,3,4,5\}\) bubbling off onto a 3-pointed sphere while the complementary trio \(\{k,l,m\}\) plus the node \(N\) sits on a 4-pointed sphere)! Where are the missing \(10 - 7 = 3\) divisors \(D_{23}, D_{13}, D_{12}\)?

Look at the three corners \((0,0)\), \((1,1)\), and \((\infty,\infty)\) in \(\mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1\). At \((0,0)\), three lines (\(z_4 = 0\), \(z_5 = 0\), and \(z_4 = z_5\)) all pass through the same point! As we proved in Section 2, approaching \((0,0)\) along slope \(z_5 = c\,z_4\) sprouts a 4-pointed bubble carrying \(\{1, 4, 5, N\}\) and leaves \(\{2, 3, N\}\) on the macro sphere — which is the exact tree of divisor \(D_{23}\)! Replacing each of the three triple-collision points \((0,0), (1,1), (\infty,\infty)\) by its \(\mathbb{P}^1\) of approach slopes \(c = z_5/z_4\) (the algebraic operation of blowing up a point) inserts the three missing divisors \(D_{23}, D_{13}, D_{12}\):

Stratum Codimension Complex Dimension Geometric Meaning Combinatorial Count in \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}\)
Codimension \(0\) \(2\) Single smooth \(\mathbb{P}^1\) with \(5\) distinct beacons \(1\) open top cell \(\mathcal{M}_{0,5}\)
Codimension \(1\) \(1\) Two \(\mathbb{P}^1\)'s joined at \(1\) node: partition \(\{i,j\} \mid \{k,l,m\}\) \(\binom{5}{2} = 10\) divisors \(D_{ij} \cong \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4} \cong \mathbb{P}^1\)
Codimension \(2\) \(0\) Chain of three \(\mathbb{P}^1\)'s joined at \(2\) nodes: \(\{i,j\} \mid \{m\} \mid \{k,l\}\) \(5 \times 3 = 15\) corner points (\(D_{ij} \cap D_{kl}\) for \(\{i,j\}\cap\{k,l\}=\varnothing\))

How you could have found this: two boundary divisors \(D_{ij}\) and \(D_{kl}\) can happen simultaneously (forming a 2-node chain of three spheres) if and only if the two pairs of colliding beacons are disjoint: \(\{i,j\} \cap \{k,l\} = \varnothing\). The graph whose \(10\) vertices are the 2-element subsets of \(\{1,2,3,4,5\}\) and whose \(15\) edges connect disjoint pairs is the famous Petersen graph drawn in Fig P54.3.

Exemplar 3 · Kontsevich stable maps and counting curves by boundary recursion

What if our objects are not bare curves \(C\), but holomorphic maps \(u : C \to \mathbb{P}^2\) of degree \(d\) passing through \(3d - 1\) generic points in the projective plane? Maxim Kontsevich adapted P54 to maps by modifying Rule 2 of Box P54.A: a genus-0 component \(C_i \subset C\) is map-stable if either

  • \(u|_{C_i}\) is a constant map (\(d_i = 0\)) and \(C_i\) carries at least \(n_i \ge 3\) special points, or
  • \(u|_{C_i}\) has positive degree (\(d_i \ge 1\)), in which case the image curve \(u(C_i) \subset \mathbb{P}^2\) already locks down the continuous Möbius group even if \(C_i\) carries fewer than \(3\) special points!
Look at what happens when we pull back the fundamental homology equivalence \([(12 \mid 34)] = [(13 \mid 24)]\) from \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4} \cong \mathbb{P}^1\) (Exemplar 1) via the forgetful map \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0, 3d}(\mathbb{P}^2, d) \to \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}\). For degree \(d = 2\) (conics in \(\mathbb{P}^2\), which depend on \(3(2) - 1 = 5\) points), choose four marked points \(p_1, p_2, p_3, p_4\) plus two extra constraints. A degenerate degree-2 map at the boundary \((12 \mid 34)\) must split its total degree \(d = 2\) across two components \(C_L \cup_N C_R\) as \(d_L + d_R = 2\) with \(d_L = 1, d_R = 1\) (two intersecting lines, each determined by \(2\) points, intersecting in \(d_L d_R = 1 \cdot 1 = 1\) node!). Consequently, if \(N_d\) denotes the number of rational degree-\(d\) curves through \(3d - 1\) generic points in \(\mathbb{P}^2\), equates the two boundary splittings to give Kontsevich's recursion:

\[N_d = \sum_{d_1 + d_2 = d} N_{d_1} N_{d_2} \left[ d_1^2 d_2^2 \binom{3d-4}{3d_1-2} - d_1^3 d_2 \binom{3d-4}{3d_1-1} \right].\]

Starting from the single base fact \(N_1 = 1\) (one straight line through two points), plug \(d = 2\) (\(d_1 = d_2 = 1\), where \(3d - 4 = 2\)) into the formula:

\[N_2 = (1)(1)\left[ 1^2\cdot 1^2 \binom{2}{1} - 1^3\cdot 1 \binom{2}{2} \right] = 2 - 1 = 1 \quad\text{(one conic through 5 points)}.\]

Plug in \(d = 3\) (\(3d - 4 = 5\), summing over \((d_1, d_2) \in \{(1,2), (2,1)\}\)):

\[N_3 = \left[4\binom{5}{1} - 2\binom{5}{2}\right] + \left[4\binom{5}{4} - 8\binom{5}{5}\right] = (20 - 20) + (20 - 8) = 12 \quad\text{(12 rational cubics through 8 points!)}.\]

By compactifying the space of smooth curves with nodal two-component degenerations, an enumerative geometry question in degree \(d\) reduces algebraically to products of lower degrees \(d_1 + d_2 = d\).

5 · When the trick breaks

Stable compactification solves the missing-limit problem by trading open varieties for stratified nodal boundaries, but three sharp obstacles govern where it breaks or changes behavior:

Exhibit A — Allowing unstable components (\(2g_i - 2 + n_i \le 0\)) destroys Hausdorffness. Suppose you forget Rule 3 of Box P54.A and allow a chain of bare \(\mathbb{P}^1\) bridges with only two nodes (\(n_i = 2\)) and zero marked points. Because scaling \(w \mapsto \lambda w\) on an \(n_i = 2\) sphere is an isomorphism, a family with plumbing parameter \(zw = \varepsilon\) can absorb any factor \(\lambda\) into \(w\), making it impossible to separate distinct limit configurations in the quotient topology.

Mitigation: Enforce the stability inequality \(2g_i - 2 + n_i > 0\) (or positive map energy \(\int_{C_i} u^*\omega > 0\)) on every component and contract all unstable ghosts automatically.

Price: Forgetting a marked point (\(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g, n+1} \to \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g, n}\)) requires a stabilization pass whenever removing the \(n+1\)-st point drops a component from \(n_i = 3\) down to \(n_i = 2\).

Default: Always run the \(2g_i - 2 + n_i > 0\) component check after any forgetting or gluing operation.

Exhibit B — Multiply-covered map bubbles jump in dimension and obstruct smoothness. While the moduli space of stable curves \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\) is always a smooth orbifold (Deligne–Mumford stack) of pure complex dimension \(3g - 3 + n\) (because \(H^2 = 0\) in its deformation complex, P53), the space of stable maps \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X, \beta)\) can have non-zero obstruction spaces \(H^1(C, u^*TX) \ne 0\) whenever a bubble component is a multiple cover \(z \mapsto z^k\) (\(k \ge 2\)) of a sphere with negative normal bundle.

Mitigation: Replace the naive fundamental class of \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X, \beta)\) with the virtual fundamental class \([\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X, \beta)]^{\text{vir}}\) constructed in P64.

Price: Invariants become rational numbers \(\in \mathbb{Q}\) (due to finite automorphism groups \(\mathbb{Z}/k\mathbb{Z}\) of \(k\)-fold covers) rather than naive integer counts.

Default: Check convexity of the target (\(H^1(\mathbb{P}^1, u^*TX) = 0\), which holds for \(X = \mathbb{P}^m\) in genus \(0\)) before equating virtual counts with literal geometric curve counts.

Exhibit C — Higher-dimensional varieties need more than one rescaling parameter (KSBA vs. GIT). For curves (dimension \(1\)), every neck pinch is locally a double point \(zw = \varepsilon\), which has a canonical minimal resolution. For surfaces and higher-dimensional varieties, degenerations can develop log-canonical singularities or depend on the choice of an ample polarization line bundle (Geometric Invariant Theory).

Mitigation: Specify the canonical bundle positivity condition (\(K_X\) ample for Kollár–Shepherd-Barron–Alexeev stable surfaces) or fix the stability chamber explicitly.

Price: Birational flips (wall-crossings) when the stability polarization changes.

Default: Use Deligne–Mumford stability directly for 1-dimensional domains (curves and strips).

Honest scope of this orientation page. We proved compactness and smoothness explicitly on genus-0 configuration spaces \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}\) and \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}\), where every component is a sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1\) with trivial automorphism group. For higher genus \(g \ge 1\), smooth curves can possess finite non-trivial automorphism groups (for instance, every elliptic curve \((E, p_1)\) has the involution \(x \mapsto -x\) of order \(2\), and the hyperelliptic curve \(y^2 = x^6 - 1\) has extra symmetries), so \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\) is a smooth orbifold (Deligne–Mumford stack) rather than a manifold.

7 · Lineage interlude

From Bernhard Riemann's \(3g - 3\) count to Deligne–Mumford curves and Kontsevich's stable maps. Bernhard Riemann calculated in his 1857 memoir on Abelian functions that a compact Riemann surface of genus \(g \ge 2\) depends on \(3g - 3\) complex parameters ("moduli"), yet for over a century geometers lacked a canonical way to close the moduli space without introducing intractable singularities. In 1969 Pierre Deligne and David Mumford (Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 36, 75–109) proved the irreducibility of \(\mathcal{M}_g\) in arbitrary characteristic by introducing stable curves — nodal curves satisfying \(2g_i - 2 + n_i > 0\) — and showing that \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\) is a smooth, proper Deligne–Mumford stack with normal-crossing boundary divisors. Finn Knudsen (Mathematica Scandinavica, 1983) proved that the stabilization-and-contraction functor makes \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\) the universal curve over \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\). In 1985 Mikhail Gromov discovered bubble-tree compactness for pseudoholomorphic curves, and in 1994 Maxim Kontsevich (Enumeration of rational curves via torus actions, Birkhäuser 1995) combined Deligne–Mumford stability with Gromov's bubble trees to define the space of stable maps \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X, \beta)\) and solve the century-old problem of counting rational plane curves of arbitrary degree \(d\).

8 · Exercises

Exercise 1 ●○○ — Two-rate collision on \(\mathcal{M}_{0,5}\) and the cross-ratio on the bubble

Consider the family of five beacons on \(\mathbb{P}^1\) given for \(\varepsilon > 0\) by

\[z_1(\varepsilon) = 0, \quad z_2(\varepsilon) = 1, \quad z_3(\varepsilon) = \infty, \quad z_4(\varepsilon) = 2\varepsilon + \varepsilon^2, \quad z_5(\varepsilon) = 6\varepsilon - \varepsilon^2.\]

(a) Introduce the microscope coordinate \(w = z / \varepsilon\) around \(z = 0\) and find the limiting positions \(w_1, w_4, w_5, w_N\) on the bubble sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\) as \(\varepsilon \to 0\).
(b) Apply a Möbius normalization (P05) on \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\) that sends \((w_1, w_4, w_N) \mapsto (0, 1, \infty)\), and find the exact canonical cross-ratio coordinate of \(w_5\). Which boundary divisor \(D_{ij}\) of \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}\) contains this limit point?

Hint

Divide each coordinate by \(\varepsilon\) and let \(\varepsilon \to 0\). On the bubble \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\), the node \(w_N\) where the macro sphere attaches is at \(w_N = \infty\); to map \((0, 2, \infty) \mapsto (0, 1, \infty)\), simply divide \(w\) by \(2\).

Worked answer

(a) Microscope limits on \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\): Substituting \(w = z/\varepsilon\) gives

\[w_1(\varepsilon) = 0, \quad w_4(\varepsilon) = 2 + \varepsilon \xrightarrow{\varepsilon \to 0} 2, \quad w_5(\varepsilon) = 6 - \varepsilon \xrightarrow{\varepsilon \to 0} 6,\]

while the distant beacons \(z_2 = 1\) and \(z_3 = \infty\) move to \(w_2(\varepsilon) = 1/\varepsilon \to \infty\) and \(w_3(\varepsilon) = \infty\), merging into the attachment node \(w_N = \infty\). Thus on \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\) the four special points are \((w_1, w_4, w_5, w_N) = (0, 2, 6, \infty)\).

(b) Canonical cross-ratio and divisor label: The unique Möbius map on \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\) sending \((w_1, w_4, w_N) = (0, 2, \infty) \mapsto (0, 1, \infty)\) is \(\widetilde{w} = w/2\). Under this map, \(w_5 = 6\) lands at

\[\widetilde{w}_5 = \frac{6}{2} = 3 \in \mathbb{P}^1 \setminus \{0, 1, \infty\}.\]

Because beacons \(\{2, 3\}\) remain on the rigid 3-pointed macro sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_z\) (carrying \(\{z_2=1, z_3=\infty, z_N=0\}\)) while \(\{1, 4, 5\}\) sit on \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\), this stable curve lies in the interior of the boundary divisor \(D_{23}\) (equivalently \(D_{145}\)) at cross-ratio coordinate \(\lambda = 3\).

Exercise 2 ●●○ — Two-scale cascade: sprouting a three-sphere chain

Now modify the family of Exercise 1 so that beacons \(4\) and \(5\) approach \(z_1 = 0\) at two different powers of \(\varepsilon\):

\[z_1(\varepsilon) = 0, \quad z_2(\varepsilon) = 1, \quad z_3(\varepsilon) = \infty, \quad z_4(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon, \quad z_5(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon^2.\]

(a) Show that a single zoom \(w = z/\varepsilon\) separates \(z_4\) from \(z_1\), but leaves \(z_1\) and \(z_5\) colliding at \(w = 0\).
(b) Introduce a second microscope \(u = z/\varepsilon^2 = w/\varepsilon\) to resolve the collision of \(z_1\) and \(z_5\). Draw the resulting 3-component stable tree, count the special points \(n_i\) on each of the three \(\mathbb{P}^1\) components, and identify the exact edge \(D_{ij} \cap D_{kl}\) of the Petersen graph (Fig P54.3) where this limit point sits.

Hint

At scale \(w = z/\varepsilon\), beacon 4 stops at \(w_4 = 1\), while beacons 1 and 5 both go to \(w = 0\). Zooming a second time with \(u = w/\varepsilon\) puts \(u_1 = 0\), \(u_5 = 1\), and sends \(w_4 = 1\) to \(u = 1/\varepsilon \to \infty\).

Worked answer

(a) First microscope \(w = z/\varepsilon\): In the \(w\)-coordinate, \(w_4(\varepsilon) = 1\) and \(w_2(\varepsilon) = 1/\varepsilon \to \infty\) (forming node \(N_1\) joining \(\mathbb{P}^1_z\) and \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\)). However, \(w_1(\varepsilon) = 0\) and \(w_5(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon^2/\varepsilon = \varepsilon \to 0\), so beacons \(1\) and \(5\) still collide at \(w = 0\).

(b) Second microscope \(u = z/\varepsilon^2\) and the 3-sphere chain: Zooming around \(w = 0\) with \(u = w/\varepsilon = z/\varepsilon^2\) gives \(u_1(\varepsilon) = 0\), \(u_5(\varepsilon) = \varepsilon^2/\varepsilon^2 = 1\), while \(w_4 = 1\) moves off to \(u_4(\varepsilon) = 1/\varepsilon \to \infty\), creating a second node \(N_2\) (where \(w = 0\) on \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\) attaches to \(u = \infty\) on \(\mathbb{P}^1_u\)). The limit is a chain of three \(\mathbb{P}^1\) components:

  • Left sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_z\): carries beacons \(\{2, 3\}\) and node \(N_1\) (\(n_1 = 2 + 1 = 3\) special points, rigid).
  • Middle sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_w\): carries beacon \(\{4\}\) and both nodes \(N_1, N_2\) (\(n_2 = 1 + 2 = 3\) special points, rigid).
  • Right sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1_u\): carries beacons \(\{1, 5\}\) and node \(N_2\) (\(n_3 = 2 + 1 = 3\) special points, rigid).

Every component has \(n_i = 3\), so the limit has \(0\) remaining continuous parameters: it is the isolated codimension-2 corner point \(D_{23} \cap D_{15}\) corresponding to the tree \(\{2,3\} - \{4\} - \{1,5\}\), which is the edge connecting vertex \(D_{23}\) and vertex \(D_{15}\) on the outer pentagon of the Petersen graph in Fig P54.3.

Exercise 3 ●●● — Boundary strata census of \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}\) and Kontsevich's \(N_4 = 620\)

(a) In \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}\) (complex dimension \(6 - 3 = 3\)), every codimension-1 boundary divisor corresponds to a 1-node tree splitting the \(6\) labeled beacons into two groups \(A \sqcup B\) with \(|A|, |B| \ge 2\). Count how many boundary divisors have a \((2 \mid 4)\) split versus a \((3 \mid 3)\) split, and compute the total number of codimension-1 boundary divisors in \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}\).
(b) Using \(N_1 = 1\), \(N_2 = 1\), and \(N_3 = 12\) from Exemplar 3, evaluate Kontsevich's recursion formula for \(d = 4\) to find the exact number \(N_4\) of rational degree-4 plane curves passing through \(3(4) - 1 = 11\) generic points in \(\mathbb{P}^2\).

Hint

For (a), remember that a \((3 \mid 3)\) partition is unordered: splitting \(\{1,2,3\} \mid \{4,5,6\}\) is the same divisor as \(\{4,5,6\} \mid \{1,2,3\}\), so divide \(\binom{6}{3}\) by \(2\). For (b), at \(d=4\) we have \(3d - 4 = 8\), and \((d_1, d_2)\) ranges over \((1,3)\), \((2,2)\), and \((3,1)\).

Worked answer

(a) Boundary divisors of \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}\): A 1-node stable tree on \(6\) marked points partitions \(\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\) into two blocks \(A \sqcup B\) of size at least \(2\) (so each component has \(|A|+1 \ge 3\) and \(|B|+1 \ge 3\) special points):

  • \((2 \mid 4)\) splits: Determined uniquely by choosing which \(2\) beacons form the smaller block: \(\binom{6}{2} = 15\) divisors (each isomorphic to \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3} \times \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5} \cong \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}\)).
  • \((3 \mid 3)\) splits: Determined by choosing an unordered partition into two blocks of \(3\): \(\frac{1}{2}\binom{6}{3} = \frac{20}{2} = 10\) divisors (each isomorphic to \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4} \times \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4} \cong \mathbb{P}^1 \times \mathbb{P}^1\)).

Total codimension-1 boundary divisors in \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,6}\): \(15 + 10 = 25 = 2^{6-1} - 6 - 1\).

(b) Computing \(N_4\) via Kontsevich's recursion: For \(d = 4\), \(3d - 4 = 8\), and we sum over \((d_1, d_2) \in \{(1,3), (2,2), (3,1)\}\) using \(N_1 = 1, N_2 = 1, N_3 = 12\):

  • Term \((d_1, d_2) = (1, 3)\): \(N_1 N_3 \left[ 1^2\cdot 3^2 \binom{8}{1} - 1^3\cdot 3 \binom{8}{2} \right] = 12 \bigl[ 9(8) - 3(28) \bigr] = 12(72 - 84) = -144\).
  • Term \((d_1, d_2) = (2, 2)\): \(N_2 N_2 \left[ 2^2\cdot 2^2 \binom{8}{4} - 2^3\cdot 2 \binom{8}{5} \right] = 1 \bigl[ 16(70) - 16(56) \bigr] = 1120 - 896 = +224\).
  • Term \((d_1, d_2) = (3, 1)\): \(N_3 N_1 \left[ 3^2\cdot 1^2 \binom{8}{7} - 3^3\cdot 1 \binom{8}{8} \right] = 12 \bigl[ 9(8) - 27(1) \bigr] = 12(72 - 27) = +540\).

Summing the three boundary contributions gives \(N_4 = -144 + 224 + 540 = 620\) rational quartic curves through \(11\) generic points in \(\mathbb{P}^2\) (first computed by Hieronymus Zeuthen in 1873 by painstaking degeneration analysis, and recovered here in three lines of arithmetic).

Exercise 4 ◆ — Pattern-unknown diagnosis: stable, unstable, or wrong tool?

Inspect each geometric degeneration below and diagnose how to handle it:

  1. A genus-0 nodal curve consists of three smooth spheres \(C_1 - C_2 - C_3\) linked in a chain (nodes \(N_{12}\) and \(N_{23}\)). Beacons \(\{1, 2, 3\}\) sit on \(C_1\), beacon \(\{4\}\) sits on \(C_3\), and \(C_2\) carries no beacons at all. Is this curve in \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}\)?
  2. A smooth torus (\(g = 1\)) with zero marked points (\(n = 0\)) pinches a non-contractible loop to form a nodal rational curve (a sphere \(\mathbb{P}^1\) with two points \(0\) and \(\infty\) glued together at a single self-node). Does this self-nodal curve satisfy \(2g_i - 2 + n_i > 0\)?
  3. You want to find the maximum of \(f(x,y) = x^2 y - x^4 - y^4\) on the closed disk \(x^2 + y^2 \le 1\).
Worked answer
  1. Unstable in \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}\) — apply Rule 3 of Box P54.A (contract two components!): Count special points on each sphere: \(C_1\) has \(3\) beacons \(+ 1\) node \(= 4\) (stable); \(C_2\) has \(0\) beacons \(+ 2\) nodes \(= 2\) (unstable, \(2(0)-2+2 = 0 \ngtr 0\)); \(C_3\) has \(1\) beacon \(+ 1\) node \(= 2\) (unstable). Contracting the tail \(C_3\) deposits beacon \(4\) onto the node of \(C_2\), and contracting \(C_2\) deposits beacon \(4\) onto the node of \(C_1\). The stabilized curve is a single smooth sphere \(C_1\) carrying all four beacons \(\{1, 2, 3, 4\}\).
  2. Unstable without a marked point (\(2(1)-2+0 = 0\)): A bare genus-1 torus with \(n = 0\) marked points has a 1-dimensional continuous translation group \(z \mapsto z + c\), and its pinched limit (\(\mathbb{P}^1\) with \(0\) and \(\infty\) glued, giving \(n_i = 2\) half-nodes on \(\mathbb{P}^1\)) has the multiplicative group \(\mathbb{C}^*\) (\(w \mapsto \lambda w\)). To apply P54 in genus \(1\), you must pin down at least \(n \ge 1\) marked point (\(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,1}\)), which gives the pinched \(\mathbb{P}^1\) two half-nodes plus one marked point (\(n_i = 3 > 2\), stable!).
  3. Simpler pattern suffices (P21 · Catch the limit point / Weierstrass extreme value theorem): The closed disk \(x^2 + y^2 \le 1\) in \(\mathbb{R}^2\) is already compact and \(f\) is continuous; no moduli blowup or nodal compactification (P54) is needed.

Exercise 5 ◐ — Exploration: Stasheff associahedra and the real manifold \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0, n}(\mathbb{R})\)

Restrict the five beacons \((z_1, z_2, z_3, z_4, z_5)\) to lie on the real projective circle \(\mathbb{RP}^1 = \mathbb{R} \cup \{\infty\}\) in cyclic clockwise order \(z_1 < z_2 < z_3 < z_4 < z_5\).
(a) When the cyclic order is fixed, only adjacent blocks of beacons can collide without crossing one another. Show that out of the \(10\) complex boundary divisors \(D_{ij}\) of \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,5}\), exactly \(5\) divisors can bound the cyclic chamber \(0 = z_1 < z_4 < z_5 < z_2 = 1 < z_3 = \infty\), forming a pentagon (the 2-dimensional Stasheff associahedron \(K_4\)).
(b) Verify that the \(5\) vertices of this pentagon correspond to the \(C_3 = \frac{1}{4}\binom{6}{3} = 5\) binary parenthesizations of four factors \(((ab)c)d\), \((a(bc))d\), \((ab)(cd)\), \(a((bc)d)\), and \(a(b(cd))\).

Hint

In the cyclic order \((1, 4, 5, 2, 3)\) around the circle, the five adjacent pairs are \(\{1,4\}\), \(\{4,5\}\), \(\{5,2\}\), \(\{2,3\}\), and \(\{3,1\}\). Look at the region \(0 < z_4 < z_5 < 1\) inside the square \([0,1]^2\) in Fig P54.3A: it is a right triangle whose two acute corners \((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\) are blown up into edges!

Worked answer

(a) From a triangle to a pentagon by blowing up two corners: With \(z_1 = 0\), \(z_2 = 1\), \(z_3 = \infty\), the cyclic ordering \(0 = z_1 < z_4 < z_5 < z_2 = 1\) cuts out the open triangle \(\{(z_4, z_5) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \mid 0 < z_4 < z_5 < 1\}\). Its three sides are \(z_4 = 0\) (\(D_{14}\)), \(z_4 = z_5\) (\(D_{45}\)), and \(z_5 = 1\) (\(D_{25}\)). Blowing up the bottom-left triple collision \((0,0)\) (where \(z_1, z_4, z_5\) meet) replaces that vertex with the edge \(D_{145} = D_{23}\), and blowing up the top-right triple collision \((1,1)\) (where \(z_4, z_5, z_2\) meet) replaces that vertex with the edge \(D_{245} = D_{13}\). Chopping two corners off a triangle produces a pentagon with \(5\) edges (\(D_{14}, D_{23}, D_{45}, D_{13}, D_{25}\)) and \(5\) vertices!

(b) Equivalence with the \(C_3 = 5\) Catalan parenthesizations: Viewbeacon \(z_3 = \infty\) as the root output of a tree and the remaining four beacons Ordered left-to-right as \((a, b, c, d) = (z_1, z_4, z_5, z_2)\). Each codimension-1 edge of the pentagon brackets either two or three consecutive letters (e.g., \((ab)cd\) for \(D_{14}\), \((abc)d\) for \(D_{23}\), \(a(bc)d\) for \(D_{45}\), \(a(bcd)\) for \(D_{13}\), and \(ab(cd)\) for \(D_{25}\)), and each of the \(5\) corners where two compatible brackets meet gives a complete binary parenthesization: \(((ab)c)d\), \((a(bc))d\), \(a((bc)d)\), \(a(b(cd))\), and \((ab)(cd)\). In general, the closure of a single cyclic chamber in \(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0, n+1}(\mathbb{R})\) is the \((n-2)\)-dimensional Stasheff associahedron \(K_{n-1}\) with \(C_{n-1}\) vertices.
