The Patterns Atlas · P43 · the seventh move of tier 4

Flow with surgery

Objects that evolve under their own curvature simplify as they go: dents heal, waists even out, and what survives the process is round. That self-simplification is the strongest argument geometry owns — and it comes with a failure the equation cannot survive, because a neck can thin to nothing before the argument completes. The move: cut the neck just before it tears, cap the cut with standard pieces whose price the flow's own books already know, and restart — the budget that guaranteed one death guarantees every death on every piece, and the argument finishes on the pieces what the flow alone could not.

New word: curvature. How hard a curve bends at a point — the reciprocal of the circle that hugs it there: a straight line reads curvature \(0\), a circle of radius \(r\) reads \(1/r\), and a graph \(y = u(x)\) reads \(\kappa = |u''|/(1+u'^2)^{3/2}\), flat where the graph is straight and sharp where it turns. This page's engine is the motion curvature itself drives: move every point of the curve inward, at speed exactly equal to its curvature. Straight stretches stand still; tight bends rush in first; the flow is a smoother by construction. For graphs the law takes one line, \(u_t = u_{xx}/(1+u_x^2)\) — the heat equation, slowed by the factor \(1/(1+u_x^2)\) that records how tilted the graph is, and indistinguishable from the heat equation on nearly flat graphs. Surfaces bend twice at once, once along each direction; the sum \(H = \kappa_1 + \kappa_2\) is the mean curvature, and the surface flow moves at speed \(H\) — a sphere of radius \(R\) has \(H = 2/R\) everywhere and shrinks by \(R^2 = R_0^2 - 4t\). The flow is also a descent: length and area books run strictly downhill, which is P29's one-way ledger worn by geometry — and that second face is what makes this page's knife honest, because a cut can be audited by the same books that audit the flow. This page's whole task is to keep the descent honest past the place where it tears: schedule the tear, price the repair, and finish the argument the flow started.

1 · Before we begin

P29 taught the ledger: pair the state with a single number the dynamics only push one way, and read verdicts — settling, corridors, deadlines — out of the books. P26 taught the writing of equations of change, and this page will write it twice, once for graphs and once for profiles of revolution. P21 taught the habit that a bounded monotone book must land, and P24 taught the wave that survives by moving — which will return, at the neck, as the shape the flow is trying and failing to become.

Here is the tension this page resolves. Flow arguments promise the most for the least: write the evolution, let the object simplify itself, and conclude from what it becomes. The ledger even supplies the clock — for curves the area falls at exactly \(2\pi\) per unit time, so every embedded curve must die by \(A_0/2\pi\) whatever its shape, and for surfaces the area falls at \(16\pi\) or faster, so every closed surface must die by \(A_0/16\pi\). But the promise has a hole: the flow may not survive to collect its own deadline. A neck thins, the curvature at the waist climbs without bound, and at a finite, computable moment the evolving object stops existing as the smooth thing the equation was written about — the books still balance, and the geometry is gone. What is needed is a way to continue the argument past the tear without abandoning any of it: keep the flow, keep the books, and repair the object — cut where it tears, cap the cut with pieces whose price the books already know, and restart. The repair must be auditable in the flow's own currency, or it is not a repair but a new guess wearing the old one's name.

2 · Meet the problem

A shape small enough to hold in one hand: the blob \(r(\theta) = 1 + 0.35\cos 3\theta\), a three-lobed disc with three dents, area \(A_0 = \pi \cdot 1.06125 = 3.334015\) — computed twice, by Simpson's rule and by the mesh, agreeing to fifteen decimals. Set it flowing. The dents heal first: the deepest dent's tip radius climbs from \(0.65\) to \(0.7749\) by \(t = 0.1\), eighteen percent of healing in a tenth of the clock, and the healed shape then shrinks as a whole, dying round at \(t = 0.530214\) against the deadline \(A_0/2\pi = 0.530625\). The isoperimetric book tells the same story as a number: \(L^2/A\) — analytically \(18.2125\) at the blob's start — falls from its first logged sample \(18.1517\) toward the circle's \(4\pi = 12.5664\), ending at \(12.6629\), without once turning around (Fig P43.1).

The census adds the surprise: shape does not matter at all. Run four shapes to death — the circle of the blob's area, the healed blob itself, an ellipse \(1.3 \times 0.75\), a rounded square of half-side \(1.1\) and corner radius \(0.3\) — and read the deaths against the one-line law \(T = A_0/2\pi\). Deadlines \(0.530625\), \(0.530625\), \(0.487500\), \(0.758014\); deaths \(0.531698\), \(0.530214\), \(0.484974\), \(0.759086\); errors \(+1.07\), \(-0.41\), \(-2.53\), \(+1.07\), in units of \(10^{-3}\) and straddling zero from both sides. The two equal-area shapes die within \(1.48 \times 10^{-3}\) of each other. Every embedded curve — dented, elongated, cornered — dies at a time fixed by area alone, and heals on the way to it.

Now the same move one dimension up, and the hole appears. Take a surface of revolution with a waist — the peanut, profile \(z = 1.9\sin s\), \(r = 0.75\cos s - 0.35\cos 3s\) on \(s \in [-\pi/2, \pi/2]\): two lobes of radius \(0.785\) at \(z = \pm 1.47\), a neck of radius \(0.4\) at the equator, poles at \(z = \pm 1.9\), area \(A_0 = 16.767615\). The books upgrade but keep their shape: a closed surface loses area at \(\iint H^2\), which is at least \(16\pi\) for every surface of sphere type — the unit sphere reads \(50.265 = 16\pi\) exactly — so the whole peanut cannot outlive \(A_0/16\pi = 0.333581\), the round deadline. But the peanut is far from round: its Willmore reading at \(t = 0\) is \(95.91\), nearly twice the floor, and it drains at nearly twice the round rate while the neck runs the cylinder's own clock \(r^2 = r_0^2 - 2t\). Two clocks race, and the neck wins: at \(t = 0.103906\) — thirty-one percent of the budget, \(3.21\times\) short of the deadline — the waist is gone, the curvature at the neck is climbing without bound, and the smooth flow is over.

And the cruelty is dimensional. Draw the same two-lobed shape as a plane curve, \(r = 1 + 0.9\cos 2\theta\), waist \(0.1\): the waist fills in, its radius climbing from \(0.1085\) to \(0.3059\) within \(t = 0.02\), and the curve dies once, round, at \(0.687652\) against the deadline \(0.702500\) (the tenth-wide waist stresses a 120-point polygon; the machine runs two percent early — a mesh limit, honestly booked). In the plane the flow always forgives; one dimension up the heal does not come, and the flow ends at the neck. Look at what the answer forces: the flow is still the strongest simplifier in the room, its books still balance, and the tear is not a mystery but a scheduled event — the neck's radius, read off the cylinder's clock, announces the pinch before it arrives. The move this page names is to trade the tear for two priced caps and a restart, and let the argument finish on the pieces.

3 · Name the move

§2 watched a flow simplify a shape to death, and a surface flow tear before its deadline. The move, named:

Pattern P43 — Flow with surgery. When the verdict you need is carried by a process that simplifies the object — an evolution whose every step smooths, shrinks, and rounds — but the process tears at a singularity before the verdict arrives, do not stop the process, and do not abandon the books. Schedule the tear: the singularity announces itself on a clock the books can read ahead of time (a thinning neck, a norm climbing without bound). At the scheduled moment, cut the object at the degenerating feature and glue on standard caps, priced in the books' own currency. Then restart the process on every piece: each piece inherits its own books and its own deadline, the cuts cost a bounded coin, and the budget that bounded one death bounds the whole family — so the process, cut and continued, runs to the verdict the uncut flow could not reach.

Or, in words: let the flow do the work; when it tears, cut, pay for the repair, and keep going. The knife is honest exactly when the books can audit it — and the argument the flow was making, it finishes on the pieces.

The move applies when three keys line up, and each key names its own jam-mode:

Key 1 — a flow with a one-way book. The evolution must be a descent: a quantity the flow pushes strictly one way, with a rate floor strong enough to force an end — area falling at \(2\pi\) per unit time for curves, at \(16\pi\) or faster for surfaces. Jam-mode: no book, no deadline — the knife has nothing to price against, and cutting becomes taste; §5's Exhibit A prices the taste, and finds it costs half a lifetime.

Key 2 — tears from a short catalogue. The singularity must be standard: a neck that, seen up close, is a cylinder, so that standard caps fit the wound. Jam-mode: the tear is not in the catalogue, or the cap is not standard — §5's Exhibit B shows the sloppy cap, whose hidden curvature is bought at face value and paid back with interest.

Key 3 — a budget that survives the knife. Every cut must be priced in the book's coin — the caps add area, the pieces add floors, the surgery count must be finite — and the pricing must restart the books on each piece without losing the global bound. Jam-mode: the audit fails, unpriced cuts can cascade, and the continuation is a new object wearing the old one's name; §5's Exhibit C shows the alternative to the knife, run: past the pinch, the flow is fiction within \(5.4 \times 10^{-5}\).

Box P43.A — the surgery, in three obligations. Every honest surgery is earned the same three-step way, and each step carries its own proof duty:

1 · Read the calendar; cut inside the window. Name the degenerating feature — the neck — and its scale \(r^*\). The calendar maps the neck's radius to its time — here \(0.25 \to 0.065002\), \(0.15 \to 0.090316\), \(0.06 \to 0.101870\) — and the surgery window runs from the moment the neck first thins to \(2r^*\) to the moment it reaches \(r^*/2\): cut anywhere inside. Duty: verify the neck is following its model law, the slope of \(r_{\text{neck}}^2\) tending to the cylinder's \(-2\), before trusting the window; a calendar read off a drifting neck is a guess in costume.

2 · Pay for the cut in the books' own coin. Glue standard caps — hemispheres of radius \(r_{\text{cut}}\) seated at the neck's minimum, seam tangent to the profile, kink zero — and record the price where the books can see it: two caps buy \(4\pi r_{\text{cut}}^2\) of area, and each capped piece carries its own Willmore floor, one closed surface owing \(\ge 16\pi\), two pieces owing \(\ge 32\pi\) between them. Duty: re-derive the price from the cap's geometry, not from hope, and balance the books at the seam — \(A_L + A_R - A(t_s) = 4\pi r_{\text{cut}}^2\), checked to the last digit the mesh can see.

3 · Restart honestly, and count. Each capped piece is a fresh problem: its own area, its own deadline \(A/16\pi\), its own necks to watch. Duty: watch them — each piece must die inside its deadline and die round, the round-family check \(A/(4\pi r_{\max}^2)\) falling toward \(1\) as the endgame nears; and count the cuts: the pieces' deadlines sum under the original budget, so the surgery count is finite and the continuation belongs to the flow, not to the surgeon.

Box P43.B — where the move appears. Curve shortening — the plane's warm-up, where the knife never comes out: every embedded curve heals and dies at \(A_0/2\pi\), so the surgery exists here as the proof of its own unnecessity (§2's healing dent; Grayson's gift). Mean curvature flow — the surface world of §4's peanut, where necks pinch, caps restore the argument, and each piece dies round inside its deadline (§4's calendar, cut, and death runs). Ricci flow — the equation where the metric itself evolves, and where the surgery program was first faced in full: a monotone book plus a priced knife turned a century-old question into a theorem (§7). Floer-style counts — the far door (§6): a flow honest enough to survive its own cuts can be counted, and the count outlives the surgery. The failure signatures are §5's: the premature cut (Exhibit A), the sloppy cap (Exhibit B), the refusal to cut (Exhibit C).

4 · The move at work

Three workings, one per costume of Box P43.B: the plane, where the books are born and the knife stays sheathed; the surface, where the two clocks race and a wave survives; and the knife itself, run on the peanut — calendar, cut, caps, bookkeeping, deaths, census. Every number below is machine work — exact families tracked to five decimals, deaths run to their stopping scale — and each is re-derivable by hand where a line of calculus suffices.

Example 1 (the plane) — the ledger that reads area alone

The law, in one line. For a closed curve moving inward at speed \(\kappa\), the area falls at exactly \(-\!\int \kappa\, ds = -2\pi\) — the turning number's constant, independent of shape. Integrate and the deadline is forced: \(A(t) = A_0 - 2\pi t\), death by \(T = A_0/2\pi\). The graph costume of the onramp earns its keep first: on \(u(0,x) = 0.5\sin x\), the flow and the heat equation run side by side to \(t = 0.1\) and the flow stays half a percent above (\(0.569959\) against \(0.567029\), the slowing factor at work); drop the amplitude to \(0.01\) and the two agree to \(2.5 \times 10^{-8}\) — curve-shortening is the heat equation, slowed exactly as the tilt prescribes.

The census, squeezed. §2's four deaths read the law back to two and a half parts in a thousand, and the errors land on both sides: \(+1.07\), \(-0.41\), \(-2.53\), \(+1.07\), in units of \(10^{-3}\). The two equal-area shapes — circle and healed blob — die within \(1.48 \times 10^{-3}\) of each other. The law is not merely approached; it is bracketed. And the endgame is certified by the second book: the Gage deficit \(L^2 - 4\pi A\) — analytically \(4.498728\) at the ellipse's start — falls monotonically over the whole trajectory of the ellipse, from \(4.454321\) at the machine's first logged sample to \(0.001106\) at the last, never once rising — the isoperimetric account of the rounding, running beside the area account of the death.

Why no knife, down here. Grayson's theorem: every embedded curve, however dented, becomes convex, then round, and dies once — the machine's healing dent is the plane's whole story, and §2's curve peanut, waist and all, forgives its neck and dies on schedule. In two dimensions the flow completes its own argument; the surgery exists for the dimension where the healing fails. That is not a defect of the move — it is its first working: knowing when the knife is unnecessary is obligation zero.

Moves used: P21 the death time, a landing the linear book forces — the area line hits zero at a time nothing can postpone · P15 the census as a squeeze — four machine deaths straddling \(A_0/2\pi\) from both sides · P25 the healing dent, the four-shape census, and the deficit book, read by machine.

Example 2 (the surface) — two clocks and a wave

The equation, one dimension up. A surface of revolution carries two bendings: the hoop curvature \(1/r\) of the circle it sits on, and the profile's own curvature \(\kappa_m\) along the meridian. The mean curvature is their sum, \(H = 1/r + \kappa_m\), and the flow moves at speed \(H\). Two exact families fall out at once. The sphere: \(H = 2/R\), so \(R^2 = R_0^2 - 4t\) — the machine tracks it to \(R(0.10) = 0.77431\) against the family's \(0.77460\), and to extinction at \(0.24976\) against the exact \(0.249494\) (the machine stops at \(R = 0.045\), a disclosed stopping scale, after 4467 steps). The cylinder: \(\kappa_m = 0\), \(H = 1/r\), so \(r^2 = r_0^2 - 2t\) — exact on the machine, from \(r_0 = 0.4\) to \(r = 0.200000\) at \(t = 0.06\), dead at \(t = 0.08\). Two clocks, and the cylinder's runs out twice as fast per radius squared.

The books, upgraded. The first variation pairs the flow with its ledger: \(dA/dt = -\iint H^2\), and the classical floor for closed surfaces of sphere type reads \(\iint H^2 \ge 16\pi\) — the unit sphere touches it exactly at \(50.265\). So every closed surface dies by \(A_0/16\pi\), with equality only for the round sphere: the deadline is a budget, and non-round shapes spend it faster. The peanut spends it nearly twice as fast at the start — Willmore \(95.91\) at \(t = 0\), draining at nearly double the round rate — but the pinch is not the area's doing: it is the neck's. The machine's neck log reads the race honestly: the slope of \(r_{\text{neck}}^2\) over the last measured stretch is \(-1.630\), tending toward the cylinder's \(-2\) — retarded by the saddle's negative profile curvature, which trims \(H\) below the cylinder's \(1/r\) — and at \(t = 0.103906\) the neck is gone, \(3.21\times\) short of the area deadline \(0.333581\). The budget says the whole cannot outlive \(0.333581\); the neck ends the flow at a third of that (Fig P43.2).

The wave, before the knife. Know what survival looks like before cutting anything. The Grim Reaper \(u = -\ln\cos x\): its time derivative is \(1\), and the flow's right-hand side reads \(u_{xx}/(1+u_x^2) = \sec^2 x/\sec^2 x = 1\) identically — the machine checks the identity on 201 points to \(4.44 \times 10^{-16}\), runs the graph on \([-1.2, 1.2]\) to \(t = 0.15\) and finds the whole profile translated upward by exactly \(t\) to \(2.43 \times 10^{-6}\), tip curvature \(1.000011\) against \(1\). A closed curve shrinks and dies; an open one can move. The solitons are the flow's waves — the shapes that survive by translating or shrinking without changing form — and a neckpinch is a flow trying to become a translating wave's neck, the cylinder, and failing to keep its lobes. The catalogue of failures is short; that is the whole reason standard caps exist.

Moves used: P26 the equation, written in two costumes — graph and profile of revolution · P29 the books, inherited and read as clocks — \(-2\pi\), \(-16\pi\), the Willmore floor · P24 the soliton, the wave that survives by moving.

Example 3 (the knife) — the peanut, cut, capped, and restarted

The calendar. Obligation 1, run. Fix the neck scale \(r^* = 0.12\) — a third of the initial neck radius — and log the machine's crossings: the neck first thins to \(2r^* = 0.24\) at \(t = 0.068137\), passes \(0.25\) at \(0.065002\), \(0.20\) at \(0.079397\), \(0.15\) at \(0.090316\), \(0.10\) at \(0.098011\), and reaches \(r^*/2 = 0.06\) at \(0.101870\). Monotone, regular, and wide: the surgery window \([0.068137,\; 0.101870]\) is \(0.033733\) wide, a third of the whole pre-pinch clock. There is no need to cut at a panicked instant; there is a month of mathematical time in which any day will do.

The cut and its price. Obligation 2, run. Cut at \(t_s = 0.090316\), where the neck reads \(r_{\text{cut}} = 0.150\): saw through at the neck's minimum, glue a hemispherical cap of radius \(r_{\text{cut}}\) to each stump — seam tangent, kink zero — and balance the books. Before: one surface, area \(A(t_s) = 8.181550\). After: two pieces, \(A_L = 4.236974\), \(A_R = 4.227146\). The difference is \(0.282571\), against the two caps' geometry \(4\pi r_{\text{cut}}^2 = 0.282710\) — the caps are bought, not donated, and the \(1.4 \times 10^{-4}\) residual is the snapshot's neck reading \(0.14999\) against the nominal \(0.15\), honestly booked. The floors double as promised: each capped piece is itself a closed surface of sphere type and owes the classical \(16\pi = 50.27\); the lobes hold the peanut's opening \(95.91\) in equal halves, and the caps pay exactly \(8\pi = 25.13\) apiece — a hemisphere of radius \(r_{\text{cut}}\) reads \((2/r_{\text{cut}})^2\) over its \(2\pi r_{\text{cut}}^2\) — so the pair opens at \(146.2\), forty-five percent above the doubled floor \(32\pi = 100.53\), and the flow can spend the surplus but never breach the floor.

The restart, run to death. Obligation 3, run. Each piece flows on its own, with its own deadline: \(A_L/16\pi = 0.084292\), \(A_R/16\pi = 0.084096\) (absolute, from the peanut's \(t = 0\): \(0.174608\) and \(0.174412\) — both still far inside the uncut budget \(0.333581\), because the peanut had already spent two thirds of its area before the knife fell). The machine runs both pieces to their stopping scale: the left dies at \(+0.071517\), the right at \(+0.071510\) — mirror-symmetric to \(7 \times 10^{-6}\), as the peanut's own symmetry demands — and both inside their deadlines with fifteen percent to spare. And they die round: the round-family check \(A/(4\pi r_{\max}^2)\) reads \(1.268\), \(1.209\), \(1.153\) at the marks, falling monotonically toward \(1\) — the capped pieces, once flowed, converge to the sphere family whose clock opened this page.

The census, and what the move bought. Put every death on one chart (Fig P43.3): each machine death sits on its law to \(3 \times 10^{-3}\); the peanut's round deadline \(0.333581\) sits on the surface law by definition; and the pinch \(0.103906\) sits below it, at thirty-one percent of the budget — the only dot in the atlas with no law, because it beats the law. What the surgery bought, with no closed form and no rescue of the original surface: two honest deaths inside two honest deadlines, on pieces whose books balance at the seam — the argument the flow was making, finished on the pieces. The budget that bounded one death bounded the family; the knife was priced in the books' own coin; and the calendar, read before the cut, is what kept Exhibit A's waste out of the record.

Moves used: P17 the two clocks, flipped — the neck's radius read as the calendar's time, the calendar's time read back as a radius · P21 the pieces' deadlines, landings the restarted books force · P25 the calendar, the seam books, and the death runs, read by machine.

5 · When the move fails

Every surgery fails somewhere; the honest page runs its failures. Three exhibits, one per obligation of Box P43.A, and each was run, not imagined — the numbers are machine work.

Exhibit A — the premature cut

Take §2's curve peanut, \(r = 1 + 0.9\cos 2\theta\), area \(\pi \cdot 1.405 = 4.413938\), deadline \(0.702500\) — and let an impatient surgeon at it: the waist is thin today, so cut now, along the chords through the two necks, and close each lobe flat. The lobes come out equal — \(2.205351\) each, mirror-exact — and each carries the halved deadline \(T/2 = 0.351250\). Run them: the lobes die at \(0.349665\), on their halved schedule (2210 steps). The uncut shape, meanwhile, heals — §2 watched the waist fill from \(0.1085\) to \(0.3059\) within \(t = 0.02\) — and dies once, round, at \(0.687652\). The verdict is arithmetic: the lobe's death is \(0.508\) of the whole's. The knife halved the lifetime, and the flow it interrupted was going to forgive the very neck it was cut for.

What broke: obligation 1 — the cut was scheduled by the eye, not the calendar; the calendar's whole content is that a thinning neck is not yet a tearing one, and the window \([0.068137,\; 0.101870]\) exists precisely to keep the surgeon out of the healing's way. Mitigation: read the neck's law before cutting — slope of \(r_{\text{neck}}^2\) tending to \(-2\), cut inside the window — and in the plane, never cut at all: Grayson's theorem is the certificate that the healing will finish. Price: here, half the budget; in the surface world, the same waste, paid in area the pieces did not need to lose. Default habit: a neck is a deadline's herald, not its executioner — cut when the calendar says, not when the waist looks thin.

Exhibit B — the sloppy cap

Obligation 2, violated in the smallest detail. A hemispherical cap seats on the cut with kink zero — the catalogue's piece. Tilt the catalogue: cap the same cut with flatter and flatter lids, radius \(2r_{\text{cut}}\), \(4r_{\text{cut}}\), \(10r_{\text{cut}}\), and the seam kink reads \(60.000^\circ\), \(75.522^\circ\), \(84.261^\circ\) — flatter is cheaper in area (a flat lid costs \(0.0718\) against the hemisphere's \(0.1414\) at \(r_{\text{cut}} = 0.15\)) and sharper at the seam. Run the two pieces — same lid, only the seam differs, one raw, one rounded by a fillet: over the same clock the raw-kink piece loses \(0.262939\) of area, the filleted piece \(0.254777\). The crease is paid back with interest: the flat lid saved \(0.07\) of area at the seam and drained \(0.0082\) extra for it, and its peak mean curvature starts at \(186.6\) — three and a half times the hemisphere's \(53.3\) at the same cut, and rising toward scales no mesh can certify. The flow forgives the kink in the end — every seam softens, and after \(t = 0.004\) the three pieces' peak curvatures read \(34.0\), \(40.2\), \(40.4\), converging — but forgiveness is not pricing, and the machine pays for the kink in steps: \(246\) against \(184\), a stalled stepper where a smooth one ran.

What broke: obligation 2 — the cap's price is not its area alone; its curvature is bought at face value, and an off-catalogue seam puts peak \(H\) where no book can audit it. Mitigation: standard caps only, seam tangent, kink zero — or a priced fillet whose rounding cost is booked before the restart; never a flat lid whose discount hides a detonation. Price: the crease tax, here \(3.2\%\) of the drain over a short clock and unbounded as the kink sharpens — curvature is the one coin the books cannot print. Default habit: price the seam, not just the cap; the cheapest lid is the one whose books balance.

Exhibit C — past the pinch

The quietest failure is no cut at all: refuse the knife, keep integrating, and see what the flow does past its wall. The machine can do this — the neck window has no caps to manage — and the run is instructive in the worst way: \(11\) steps past the pinch, the neck's radius crosses zero at \(t = +0.00005355\), at \(z = 0.0054\), against the cylinder law's estimate \(r_{\text{pinch}}^2/2 = 0.00004400\) — the law holds to within a fifth even here, which is exactly why the wall's timing was never in doubt. But below \(r = 0\) there is no surface: the profile has passed through the axis, the "surface" self-intersects, and every area the books report after the crossing is a number wearing a geometric costume. The flow did not pause politely at the singularity to wait for the surgeon; it went through the wall and became fiction.

What broke: not the flow — the refusal. Obligation 3's restart is the only continuation there is; without the cut there is no piece to restart, and the ledger keeps balancing while the geometry that gave it meaning is gone. Mitigation: the surgery window — cut while the neck still has half a scale to go (\(r^*/2\), an entire half-radius before the wall), which is why the window's floor sits at \(0.101870\) and not at the pinch. Price: \(5.4 \times 10^{-5}\) of extra flow bought a fiction — the cheapest possible demonstration that the wall is real. Default habit: never integrate past a singularity; the calendar told you when the wall was coming, and the window is the honest distance to stop before it.

Three diseases, one machine-read symptom table. Exhibit A: the cut outside the window — lifetime halved, the healing it interrupted was free. Exhibit B: the cap off the catalogue — crease tax paid with interest, peak curvature at detonation scale. Exhibit C: no cut at all — the flow through the wall, fiction within \(5.4 \times 10^{-5}\). Three different failures, one lesson: the calendar, the coin, and the restart are load-bearing, and each one is exactly one obligation of Box P43.A.

Scope, honestly. What P43 gives: a continuation of the flow's argument past the tear — cuts scheduled by a readable calendar, caps priced in the books' own coin, pieces that die round inside their own deadlines, and a finite surgery count guaranteed by the budget. What it never gives: a canonical cut — the window \([0.068137,\; 0.101870]\) is a third of the pre-pinch clock wide, and two honest surgeons may cut at different times inside it; the curve world's knife — unnecessary, for the best reason there is (Grayson rounds every embedded curve; §2's healing dent is the whole story); an end to solitons — the Reaper escapes at speed one forever, and the surgery catalogues survivors, it does not eliminate them; or a flow past the pinch — Exhibit C's fiction is disclosed, not solved. The knife is honest exactly as far as the books can audit it; the audit, not the knife, is the theorem.

The construction needs a flow honest enough to count — gradient-like, with books that survive the cuts — and this page is one of its two ladders: the surgery is what lets the counting continue past the tears. The first step from here is already underfoot.

The default habit. When a problem simplifies itself if only it could keep going — evolve, descend, anneal, flow — and the evolution tears, do not stop, and do not ignore the tear. Name the book, name the tear's model, price the cut in the book's coin, restart, and count. The flow was making an argument; the surgery is a quotation mark in it, and the argument finishes on the pieces. Like P29's one-way books, the whole method costs one question: what does the knife cost, and in whose books?

7 · Lineage

The flow is older than the theorem. Mullins, in 1956, wrote the speed law for the corners of grain boundaries smoothing themselves flat — nearly every later paper mentions the appearance, and this page follows the custom. Gage, in 1983 and 1984, wrote the curve's books: the area falling at \(2\pi\), and the deficit \(L^2 - 4\pi A\) falling with it; with Hamilton, in 1986 (Journal of Differential Geometry 23, 69–96), he proved that convex curves round themselves and die. Grayson, in 1987 (Journal of Differential Geometry 26, 285–314), removed the convexity: every embedded curve, dented or waisted, heals and dies once — §2's healing dent and forgiving curve peanut are his theorem, run. One dimension up, the convex surface case is credited to Huisken, 1984 (the exact reference was not re-checked for this page; the attribution is literature-consistent and nothing below leans on it), and Abresch and Langer, in 1986, classified the embedded curves that shrink without changing shape — the solitons, §4's waves. The founding equation of the whole subject is Hamilton's Ricci flow, 1982 — the metric itself evolving (the reference was not re-checked here either; the flow is standard, the date is an anchor) — and it was there that surgery was first faced in full: Perelman, in three preprints through 2002–03, first built a new book — "a monotonic expression for the Ricci flow, valid in all dimensions and without curvature assumptions" — and then the knife: "Here we construct Ricci flow with surgeries." In his three dimensions the neckpinch is modelled on the round cylinder, and the cap that replaces it is a half of a sphere — §4's peanut, promoted one dimension, with the same calendar, the same priced caps, and the same restarted books. The dates are anchors, not load-bearing: every claim above stands on this page's own mathematics.

8 · Exercises

Exercise 1 ●○○ — A circle dies on schedule. Take a circle of radius \(2\) under the flow. (a) Show that its radius obeys \(r^2 = r_0^2 - 2t\), and read off the death time. (b) Show that the death time equals the area deadline \(A_0/2\pi\). (c) A machine that stops at area \(0.005\) should finish at \((A_0 - 0.005)/2\pi\); it reports \(2.003927\). Price the discrepancy.

Hint

(a) Every point of a circle moves inward at speed \(\kappa = 1/r\). (c) Compare with §2's circle: error \(+1.07 \times 10^{-3}\), from an explicit stepping scheme.

Worked answer

(a) \(r' = -1/r\), so \(r^2 = r_0^2 - 2t\): death at \(T = r_0^2/2 = 2\). (b) \(A_0 = \pi r_0^2\), so \(A_0/2\pi = r_0^2/2 = T\) — the deadline law and the circle's clock are the same sentence. (c) \((4\pi - 0.005)/2\pi = 1.999204\); the machine's \(2.003927\) sits \(4.7 \times 10^{-3}\) above it, the same Euler bias that put §2's circle \(1.07 \times 10^{-3}\) late: an explicit step at speed \(\kappa\) overshoots the curvature's pull by a term of order \(\Delta t \cdot \kappa^2\), which the smaller circle (\(\kappa\) up to \(27\)) amplifies. The books absorb it: it is booked, not hidden.

Exercise 2 ●●○ — The wave that survives. The Grim Reaper is the graph \(u = -\ln\cos x\). (a) Show that \(u_{xx}/(1+u_x^2) = 1\) identically, and conclude that \(u(x,t) = t - \ln\cos x\) solves the graph flow. (b) Compute the tip curvature at \(x = 0\) and explain why it does not change. (c) What does this shape survive that §2's blob does not?

Hint

(a) \(u_x = \tan x\), \(u_{xx} = \sec^2 x\). (c) One is closed and one is not; one has an area book and one does not.

Worked answer

(a) \(1 + u_x^2 = 1 + \tan^2 x = \sec^2 x\), so \(u_{xx}/(1+u_x^2) = 1\) everywhere the graph exists; and \(u_t = 1\), so \(u(x,t) = t - \ln\cos x\) satisfies \(u_t = u_{xx}/(1+u_x^2)\) exactly — the machine checks the identity on 201 points to \(4.44 \times 10^{-16}\), and the translated profile to \(2.43 \times 10^{-6}\) on \([-1.2, 1.2]\) at \(t = 0.15\). (b) \(\kappa(0) = |u''(0)|/(1+u'(0)^2)^{3/2} = 1/1 = 1\) (machine: \(1.000011\)); it does not change because the profile does not change — the flow translates it, and translation carries curvature along. (c) The blob is closed: its area book runs one way, at \(-2\pi\), and no motion of the profile can stop a book with one entry sign. The Reaper is open: it owes no area, so it has no deadline — the second way to survive is to move, and the machine watches it do exactly that, at speed one, forever.

Exercise 3 ●●● — The books at the seam. Return to §4's surgery: cut at \(t_s = 0.090316\), \(r_{\text{cut}} = 0.150\), with \(A(t_s) = 8.181550\), \(A_L = 4.236974\), \(A_R = 4.227146\). (a) Show that the seam balances: \(A_L + A_R - A(t_s) = 4\pi r_{\text{cut}}^2\), and account for the machine's residual \(0.282571\) against \(0.282710\). (b) Compute both pieces' deadlines and verify §4's deaths inside them. (c) Show that one closed surface owes \(\iint H^2 \ge 16\pi\) but two pieces owe \(\ge 32\pi\) in total, and check the machine's readings against the doubled floor.

Hint

(a) Two hemispherical caps of radius \(r_{\text{cut}}\): \(2 \times 2\pi r_{\text{cut}}^2\). (c) The classical floor \(\iint H^2 \ge 16\pi\) holds for each closed surface of sphere type, and the pieces are two of them.

Worked answer

(a) \(A_L + A_R - A(t_s) = 4.236974 + 4.227146 - 8.181550 = 0.282570\), against \(4\pi(0.15)^2 = 0.282710\): the caps are bought, not donated. The residual \(1.4 \times 10^{-4}\) is measurement, not geometry — the snapshot's neck reads \(0.14999\) against the nominal \(0.15\), and the cap arcs are seated on the reading. (b) \(A_L/16\pi = 0.084292\), \(A_R/16\pi = 0.084096\); the deaths \(0.071517\) and \(0.071510\) fall inside with fifteen percent to spare — the pieces die before their own round deadlines, as non-round pieces must. (c) The classical inequality \(\iint H^2 \ge 16\pi\) (equality: the round sphere, whose reading \(50.265\) the machine reproduces) applies per closed surface; one cut makes two closed surfaces, so the total floor doubles from \(16\pi = 50.27\) to \(32\pi = 100.53\). The books bear it out: the caps pay exactly \(8\pi = 25.13\) apiece on top of the lobes' equal halves of the opening \(95.91\), so the pair opens at \(146.2\) against the doubled \(100.53\) — forty-five percent above. That is the deep reason the surgery count is finite: every cut raises the floor the pieces must jointly clear, while the budget only shrinks.

Exercise 4 ◆ Diagnose the claim (pattern-unknown). A colleague writes: "The flow tore, so we cleaned up — cut the neck where it looked thinnest, capped the stumps with flat lids because they were cheapest in area, and restarted. The details are implementation; the theorem does not care how the cleanup was done." Diagnose. What is true in the claim? What is smuggled in it? Which of Box P43.A's three obligations does each choice violate, and what would you run to show the price?

Hint

Three choices, three obligations: the schedule, the cap, the restart. §5 priced all three.

Worked answer

True: after a tear, the argument continues only by cutting and restarting — the colleague's skeleton is the move's own. Smuggled: that the choices are implementation. The schedule violates obligation 1: a neck thinnest to the eye is a neck mid-healing — §5's Exhibit A cut exactly there and halved the lifetime (lobes dead at \(0.508\) of the whole), and the calendar's window \([0.068137,\; 0.101870]\) exists to prevent it. The flat lid violates obligation 2: cheapest in area is a discount on the wrong coin — the lid's \(75.5^\circ\) seam kink started at peak mean curvature \(186.6\) and drained \(0.262939\) against a filleted seam's \(0.254777\), a \(3.2\%\) tax that grows with the kink, and curvature is the one coin the books cannot print. And "cleanup" with no count violates obligation 3: uncounted cuts can cascade, and the alternative to counting is Exhibit C — past the pinch the flow is fiction within \(5.4 \times 10^{-5}\), so a cleanup that arrives after the wall cleans nothing. The single question to put to the colleague: what does your knife cost, and in whose books? If the answer is "implementation", the books are closed — and a closed book is the one thing this move never accepts.

Exercise 5 ◐ The neck calendar, explored (exploration). §4's calendar maps neck radius to time: \(0.25 \to 0.065002\), \(0.20 \to 0.079397\), \(0.15 \to 0.090316\), \(0.10 \to 0.098011\), \(0.06 \to 0.101870\). (b) With the neck scale \(r^* = 0.12\), exhibit the surgery window and its width, and explain why the floor sits an entire half-scale below the pinch. (c) The pinch itself: estimate the crossing time from the cylinder law, \(r_{\text{pinch}}^2/2\), and compare with the machine's \(+0.00005355\). What does the comparison certify?

Hint

(a) The cylinder's clock: \(r^2 = r_0^2 - 2t\), so \(r^2\) against \(t\) has slope \(-2\) — until the geometry stops being a cylinder. (c) §4's D-exhibit... the pinch-scale crossing was run; the estimate needs only the neck's radius at the pinch.

Worked answer

(a) The squared radii \(0.0625, 0.04, 0.0225, 0.01, 0.0036\) against the times fall ever faster, and over the last measured stretch the slope is \(-1.630\), tending toward the cylinder's \(-2\) from above: the neck is nearly a cylinder, and "nearly" is priced by the saddle's negative profile curvature, which trims \(H = 1/r + \kappa_m\) below the cylinder's \(1/r\) and slows the drain. The \(-2\) law is the asymptote the calendar approaches, not an identity it satisfies — which is why the calendar is read from the machine, never assumed from the law. (b) The window is \([t(2r^*),\; t(r^*/2)] = [0.068137,\; 0.101870]\), width \(0.033733\) — a third of the whole pre-pinch clock (\(0.103906\)) available for the cut. The comparison certifies that the wall was never a surprise: its law was legible from the start, all the way down.
