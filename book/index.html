What this book is

This is not a textbook. There are no chapters on "quadratics" or "integration". Instead, this book teaches the moves — the reusable patterns of mathematical problem-solving — because moves, not topics, are what transfer to problems you have never seen before.

Each pattern gets one page: you meet a problem first, watch the move work, learn when to reach for it, learn when it fails, and then practice diagnosing it yourself on graded exercises. Some problems need several moves at once; those live on separate pages of their own.

Who it is for: anyone who has finished class-12 mathematics. No proof-training or olympiad background is assumed. Where the book climbs above school math, it says so and builds a ladder.

How to read it

The ladder path (default): read the spine pages in order, P01 → P17. Each page opens by recalling the pages before it.

The repair path: stuck on a problem? Jump to the diagnosis guide (planned): answer three questions — what kind of claim is it, what shape does the problem have, what tools do you have — and get pattern candidates.

The dip-in path: every page is self-contained enough to read alone; prerequisites are linked at the top.

Why these patterns

The selection is not taste. It comes from measuring a large machine-readable knowledge graph of mathematical techniques (15,941 nodes, 26,614 edges), where each technique records the theorems it helped prove: the spine patterns are the most-reused techniques of school-accessible mathematics, ranked by how many distinct theorems they participate in — e.g. reduce-to-canonical-form fires in 430 chains, compose-with-identity in 354, squeeze in 343. The evidence number for every pattern is printed on its page, honestly — including where the graph data is noisy.
