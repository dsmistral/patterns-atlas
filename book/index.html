What this book is

This is not a textbook. There are no chapters on "quadratics" or "integration". Instead, this book teaches the moves — the reusable patterns of mathematical problem-solving — because moves, not topics, are what transfer to problems you have never seen before.

Each pattern gets one page: you meet a problem first, watch the move work, learn when to reach for it, learn when it fails, and then practice diagnosing it yourself on graded exercises. Some problems need several moves at once; those live on separate pages of their own.

Who it is for: anyone who has finished class-12 mathematics — and is willing to keep growing. You start at the spine; nothing before that is assumed. But you do not stay at the entry level: the ladder runs all the way to the patterns behind research-level results (Ricci flow, spectral sequences, Floer homology), with every rung built from pages you have already climbed. The book is self-contained: when it climbs above school math, it says so and builds the ladder itself.

How to read it

The ladder path (default): read the spine in order, P01 → P17, then keep climbing tier by tier. Each page opens by recalling the pages before it.

The repair path: stuck on a problem? Jump to the diagnosis guide (planned): answer three questions — what kind of claim is it, what shape does the problem have, what tools do you have — and get pattern candidates.

The dip-in path: every page is self-contained enough to read alone; prerequisites are linked at the top.

Tier-5 pages are orientation pages: they tell you what a research tool is for, show one showcase result honestly labeled as a sketch, and map which of this book's pages you need first. No page ever silently assumes outside knowledge.

Why these patterns

The selection is not taste. It comes from measuring a large machine-readable knowledge graph of mathematical techniques (15,941 nodes, 26,614 edges), where each technique records the theorems it helped prove. The spine patterns are the most-reused techniques of school-accessible mathematics, ranked by how many distinct theorems they participate in — e.g. reduce-to-canonical-form fires in 430 chains, compose-with-identity in 354, squeeze in 343. The higher tiers cover the full 57-technique registry behind that graph plus its highest-participation extras — nothing was deferred for being hard; patterns are ordered by difficulty, never excluded for it. The evidence number for every pattern is printed on its page, honestly — including where the graph data is noisy.
